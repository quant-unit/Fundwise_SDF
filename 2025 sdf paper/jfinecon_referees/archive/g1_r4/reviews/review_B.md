## Part 2: Assessment and Recommendations for Decisions (D1–D3)

### D1. L1 inference

- **Recommended option:** **Option (d) combined with Option (a)** — **do not include L1 tables in the main paper** (where they do not belong theoretically given T1 and are not currently included in `sdf_paper.tex`); if L1 point estimates are retained at all (e.g., as a brief robustness note in the Online Appendix on median/outlier resistance), **apply (a): remove `SE_A`, `SE_JK`, `t_A`, and significance stars completely**, reporting only $\hat\theta$, $\mathrm{SD}_{CV}$, and $\mathrm{SR}_{CV}$, with an explicit caveat that L1 sits at single-vintage kinks.
- **Reasons:**
  1. **Theoretical degeneracy under T1:** By T1 ($\bar\epsilon_{v,H} = A_{v,H} PV_v$), minimizing $V^{-1}\sum_v |e_{v,H}(\theta)|$ pins the 1-parameter estimate to the zero-NPV root $\beta_{v^*}^*$ of a single vintage $v^*$ in 40 of 48 cells (verified in `l1_kink.R`), and the 2-parameter estimate to 1–2 vintages. That is an extreme-order-statistic/median-type criterion that contradicts the paper's narrative on cross-vintage risk averaging and $U_{v,H}$.
  2. **All current L1 SEs are irreparably broken:** `SE_A` and `SE.indep` fail both at kinks (`~1e-4`) and away from kinks (`0.51`–`11.50` because `num.hess` measures $e_v''$ rather than $2f_e(0)(e_v')^2$). Analytic L1/LAD asymptotics (option (c); Koenker 2005, *Quantile Regression*, and Powell 1991 kernel sparsity estimation — *from memory*) require estimating the error density $f_e(0)$ at zero, which is infeasible with $V = 25$–$27$ dependent vintages ($N_{\text{eff}} \approx 5$–$14$).
  3. **Delete-one jackknife and $hv$-block CV:**
     - *Delete-one jackknife:* **Yes, it is inconsistent here just as for the sample median** (Efron 1982; Shao & Wu 1989 — *from memory*): dropping one vintage leaves the pinning vintage unchanged in most leave-one-out fits and jumps discretely when the pinning vintage is dropped. Delete-$d$ jackknife or $m$-out-of-$n$ / block subsampling (option (b)) could fix the asymptotics in large samples, but with $V = 25$–$27$ serially correlated vintages, a block size $d$ large enough to preserve dependence leaves too few units to validate or calibrate reliably.
     - *Does `SD_CV` say anything valid about sampling uncertainty?* **Not as a valid standard error or confidence interval.** Checked in `check_l1_cv_and_alpha.R`: in `BO EW MKT L1 H=0` ($\hat\beta = 1.25225$), 5 of 8 CV folds (`1988`–`2000`) return **literally `1.252`** because vintage 2010 remains in the training sample, while the last 3 folds jump to `1.325`–`1.356` (`SD_CV = 0.0467`); in `VC EW MKT L1 H=0`, folds swing from `0.803` to `2.361` (`SD_CV = 0.6680`). Thus `SD_CV` measures **sensitivity to dropping a 9-year block of vintages** (specifically, whether a single pinning cohort drives the estimate), not calibrated sampling uncertainty of the full-sample estimator.
- **What this changes in tables/paper:**
  - Zero impact on `sdf_paper.tex` (L1 is already absent there).
  - In `plot_empirical_estimates.R` (if L1 LaTeX tables continue to be generated for an appendix), remove `SE_A`, `t_A`, stars, and the `SE_JK` robustness table for `error_function == "L1_Ridge"`, and fix the duplicate LaTeX label (`tab:empirical_bo_mkt` is currently reused by L1).

### D2. Excluding degenerate SDFs (from F2 and F3)

- **Critical empirical correction to the brief's premise:** The brief asks *"Would (a) change any of the 254 confirmed entries? It should not, if the floor lies below 0.69."* **This premise is false.** Checking all 240 non-alias Multi rows in `check_f2_2021_detail.R` shows that **18 confirmed VC Multi rows have $\min_t G_t < 0.50$**, down to **`0.3945`** (`VC FW MKT+ME H=60`), `0.4052` (`VC FW MKT+Alpha H=270`), `0.4321` (`VC EW MKT+ME H=60`), and `0.4955` (`VC FW MKT+IA H=60`). A floor of `0.50` **would** bind and change ~18 VC Multi entries!
- **Sensitivity check of floor (a) alone vs box (b) vs (a)+(b):**
  - At `floor = 0.35`, all 254 confirmed entries are untouched, **but** `VC FW MKT+EG H=0` still has its global minimum on the floor boundary (`(2.390, 9.028)`, `0.05464 < 0.05559`). Only for `floor >= 0.40` does `(0.130, 3.306)` become the global minimum for `VC FW EG H=0` — right on top of `VC FW ME H=60` (`0.3945`)!
  - Moreover, even at `floor = 0.40`, `0.50`, or `0.60` (or box `[-4,4]x[-4,4]`), when vintage 2003 is removed in `VC FW MKT+EG H=0`, the interior basin disappears and the refit walks to the constraint boundary (`SE_JK(EG) = 2.957` at floor `0.40`, `2.000` at `0.50`, `1.244` at `0.60`; `1.164` in box `[-4,4]x[-4,4]`).
- **Recommended option:** **Option (a) paired with a mild compact domain box (Option (b)) — i.e., directly enforcing the paper's existing Assumption A7** ([revision_plan.tex](file:///Users/christausch/github/Fundwise_SDF/2025%20sdf%20paper/jfinecon_referees/revision_plan.tex#L520), *checked in source*: *"compact admissible domain $\Theta$ with uniform positivity margin $G_u(\theta) \ge \underline{g} > 0$"*). Specifically:
  - Enforce **$G_u(\theta) \ge \underline{g}$ with $\underline{g} = 0.25$ (or $0.30$)** across all sample months (note: `brent.interval = [-1, 4]` already implies $\underline{g} \approx 0.0786$ in October 1987, and all 254 confirmed Multi entries have $\min_t G_t \ge 0.3945$), **plus** a compact parameter box on non-$\alpha$ factor loadings such as **$\beta_m \in [-4, 4]$** (or `[-4, 5]`; across all 240 Multi rows, observed `MKT` is in `[0.108, 2.540]` and non-Alpha `Coef` is in `[-2.579, 3.408]`, so `[-4, 4]` has zero binding effect on any confirmed estimate while keeping consistency with the `[-1, 4]` 1-factor domain).
  - **Why not (c), (d), or (e)?**
    - *(c) Keep local minimum from fixed start:* Methodologically indefensible (`revision_plan.tex` rightly rejects it).
    - *(d) `exp.aff`:* Changes the SDF model to $\prod_k (1 + F_{k,t})^{\beta_k}$, breaking comparability with the linear SDF of Driessen, Lin, and Phalippou (2012) and Korteweg and Nagel (2016) (*checked in [sdf_paper.tex](file:///Users/christausch/github/Fundwise_SDF/2025%20sdf%20paper/tex/sdf_paper.tex#L895-L902)*) and all proofs T1–T8.
    - *(e) $\lambda > 0$ ridge/lasso penalty:* Shrinks all loadings toward zero and alters every point estimate, standard error, and population target.
  - **Literature standard:** Positivity $m_t > 0$ is foundational in Hansen and Jagannathan (1991, *JPE* — *from memory*) for no-arbitrage SDF bounds, and bounding $G_t = 1/M_t \ge \underline{g} > 0$ on a compact $\Theta$ is already the paper's own Assumption A7 ([revision_plan.tex](file:///Users/christausch/github/Fundwise_SDF/2025%20sdf%20paper/jfinecon_referees/revision_plan.tex#L520), *checked in source*), extending the `[-2%, 2%]` $\alpha$ bound motivated by DLP12 Footnote 8 (*checked in [sdf_paper.tex](file:///Users/christausch/github/Fundwise_SDF/2025%20sdf%20paper/tex/sdf_paper.tex#L914)*).
  - **How to handle jackknife refits:** Apply the **exact same constrained estimator** ($\min_t G_t \ge \underline{g}$ and box bounds) in `jackknife.se()` — never use a different domain for jackknife refits than for the full sample, and never silently drop a refit like 2003. Instead, **detect and flag** when any leave-one-out refit hits a parameter or positivity bound (analogous to `at.bound` for the full-sample fit; e.g., `jk.at.bound` count in the cache and a dagger/footnote on `SE_JK` when $>0$). Under `[-4, 4]`, leaving out 2003 in `VC FW MKT+EG H=0` still hits `EG = 4.000`, giving `SE_JK(EG) = 1.164` — which accurately and honestly tells the reader that `VC FW MKT+EG` at $H = 0$ depends heavily on vintage 2003 and sits near the T5 non-coercivity cliff.
- **What this changes in tables/paper:**
  - All 254 confirmed Multi table entries remain **unchanged** (when $\underline{g} \le 0.35$ and box is `[-4, 4]`).
  - `VC FW MKT+EG H=0, 0.1` `SE_JK` changes from `(2.634, 9.780)` to `(0.204, 1.164)` (under `[-4, 4]`), flagged as hitting the jackknife bound; `NATRES MKT+EG` edge failures are also eliminated.
  - Note on implementation: replace Nelder–Mead's discontinuous `1e10` barrier with `L-BFGS-B` for the box plus either an augmented-Lagrangian / smooth barrier for $G_t \ge \underline{g}$ (or `nloptr` / `constrOptim` since $G_t(\theta) \ge \underline{g}$ is a **linear inequality constraint** $A\theta \ge b$ in $\theta$! Because $1 + r_{f,t} + \beta^\top F_t \ge \underline{g}$ is linear in $\theta$, `stats::constrOptim()` or `nloptr` handles it smoothly without Nelder–Mead stalling on a `1e10` wall).
