\section{Proofs of Lemma~\ref{lem:deal_level_timing}, Proposition~\ref{theo:inception_value}, Theorem~\ref{thm:finite_pool} (T1--T8), Supplementary Lemmas, and Proposition~\ref{prop:optimal_H}}
\label{sec:appendix_theory}

This appendix proves Lemma~\ref{lem:deal_level_timing} and Proposition~\ref{theo:inception_value} (Subsection~\ref{sec:proof_timing}), proves parts T1--T8 of Theorem~\ref{thm:finite_pool} in the numbering of the main text (Subsections~\ref{sec:proof_T1}--\ref{sec:proof_T8}), states three supplementary results used in the discussion (Subsection~\ref{sec:supplementary_lemmas}: large-$\alpha$ attenuation at $H=0$ versus coercivity at $H>0$, Lemma~\ref{lem:coercivity} and Figure~\ref{fig:exploding_alpha}; infill consistency, Lemma~\ref{lem:infill}; and multiple roots of the mean pricing moment, Lemma~\ref{lem:multiple_roots}), and proves and quantifies Proposition~\ref{prop:optimal_H} on optimal horizon theory and cross-vintage dependence (Subsection~\ref{sec:appendix_optimal_H}). Throughout, $\theta$ lies in the positive-return admissible domain $\Theta$ where $G_u(\theta) = 1 + r_{f,u} + \alpha + \beta^\top F_u > 0$ on the relevant dates, so $\Psi_t(\theta) = \prod_{u=1}^t G_u(\theta)^{-1} > 0$ and $R^b_{a,b}(\theta) = \Psi_a(\theta)/\Psi_b(\theta) > 0$. We write $\mathcal{G}_v$ for the conditioning information $(\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{pub}},\mathcal{W}_v)$ of the main text: the public factor-path and window information $\mathcal{C}_v$, the eligibility event $n_v \ge 2$, the fund sizes $\{s_{v,i}\}$, and the vintage origin $t_v^0$. Fund weights $w_{v,i}$ (the $\omega_{v,i}$ of the main text) and the horizon scale $A_{v,H}(\theta)$ are $\mathcal{G}_v$-measurable. Differentiation under the expectation is justified by the dominated-convergence moment envelopes established in Appendix~\ref{sec:proofs_asymptotics_H} (Lemma~\ref{lem:mixing_moment_inheritance}).

\subsection{Proofs of Lemma~\ref{lem:deal_level_timing} and Proposition~\ref{theo:inception_value}}
\label{sec:proof_timing}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:deal_level_timing}]
Part~(1) follows from the law of iterated expectations since $\Psi_{t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Inv}}}/\Psi_\tau$ is $\mathcal{F}_{t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Inv}}}$-measurable when $\tau \le t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Inv}}$ and $\mathbb{E}[\delta_{i,j}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Inv}}}] = 0$.
Part~(2) holds because $\Psi_{t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Inv}}}/\Psi_\tau$ is $\mathcal{F}_\tau$-measurable when $\tau > t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Inv}}$.
For Part~(3), conditioning first on $\mathcal{F}_{t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Inv}}}$ gives
\[
\mathbb{E}\!\left[\frac{\Psi_{t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Inv}}}}{\Psi_{t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Future}}}}\,\delta_{i,j} \,\middle|\, \mathcal{F}_{t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Inv}}}\right]
= \mathrm{Cov}\!\left(\frac{\Psi_{t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Inv}}}}{\Psi_{t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Future}}}},\,\delta_{i,j} \,\middle|\, \mathcal{F}_{t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Inv}}}\right)
+ \mathbb{E}\!\left[\frac{\Psi_{t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Inv}}}}{\Psi_{t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Future}}}} \,\middle|\, \mathcal{F}_{t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Inv}}}\right]
\underbrace{\mathbb{E}\!\left[\delta_{i,j} \,\middle|\, \mathcal{F}_{t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Inv}}}\right]}_{=0},
\]
and taking $\mathbb{E}[\cdot \mid \mathcal{F}_\tau]$ yields \eqref{eq:future_value_bias_derivation}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{theo:inception_value}]
Because $\tau \le t_i^0 \le t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Inv}}$ for every deal $j$ in fund $i$, summing $\sum_t (\Psi_t/\Psi_\tau)CF_{i,t} = \sum_{j=1}^{J_i}(\Psi_{t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Inv}}}/\Psi_\tau)\delta_{i,j}$ and applying \eqref{eq:zero_deal_expectation} term by term yields zero.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Proof of T1 (Exact horizon rescaling identity)}
\label{sec:proof_T1}

For any unit (fund or constant-composition vintage portfolio) with common vintage origin $t_v^0$ and cash-flow stream $\{CF_{v,t}\}_{t=t_v^0}^{T_{\max}}$, the time-$\tau$ revalued pricing error at any date $\tau \in \mathcal{T}_{v,H}$ (illustrated schematically in Figure~\ref{fig:npvs}) is
\[
\epsilon_{v,\tau}(\theta)
= \sum_{t=t_v^0}^{T_{\max}} \frac{\Psi_t(\theta)}{\Psi_\tau(\theta)} CF_{v,t}
= \frac{\Psi_{t_v^0}(\theta)}{\Psi_\tau(\theta)} \sum_{t=t_v^0}^{T_{\max}} \frac{\Psi_t(\theta)}{\Psi_{t_v^0}(\theta)} CF_{v,t}
= R^b_{t_v^0,\tau}(\theta)\, PV_v(\theta),
\]
where the factorization holds identically whether $\tau < t_v^0$, $\tau = t_v^0$, or $\tau > t_v^0$ because $\Psi_t(\theta)/\Psi_\tau(\theta) = (\Psi_{t_v^0}(\theta)/\Psi_\tau(\theta))\cdot(\Psi_t(\theta)/\Psi_{t_v^0}(\theta))$ pathwise on the positive-return domain. Averaging over $\tau \in \mathcal{T}_{v,H}$ yields
\[
\bar\epsilon_{v,H}(\theta)
= \frac{1}{|\mathcal{T}_{v,H}|} \sum_{\tau \in \mathcal{T}_{v,H}} \epsilon_{v,\tau}(\theta)
= \underbrace{\left(\frac{1}{|\mathcal{T}_{v,H}|} \sum_{\tau \in \mathcal{T}_{v,H}} R^b_{t_v^0,\tau}(\theta)\right)}_{A_{v,H}(\theta)\,>\,0} PV_v(\theta).
\]
In particular, $\bar\epsilon_{v,H}(\theta)$ is an exact path- and parameter-dependent scalar multiple of the inception NPV $PV_v(\theta)$. Because all dates $\tau \in \mathcal{T}_{v,H}$ share the common factor $PV_v(\theta)$, averaging across $\tau$ does \emph{not} average imperfectly correlated date-specific pricing errors; conditional on the factor path, all $\epsilon_{v,\tau}(\theta)$ are perfectly collinear with $PV_v(\theta)$. \qed

\subsection{Proof of T2 (Horizon mean preservation under (X); the compounding wedge under (P) alone)}
\label{sec:proof_T2}

Under inception pricing condition~(P), $\mathbb{E}[PV_v(\theta_0)\mid\mathcal{F}_{t_v^0}] = 0$ for $\mathcal{F}_{t_v^0}$-measurable fund weights. Therefore, for any integrable $\mathcal{F}_{t_v^0}$-measurable weight $z_v$ (including any pre-inception discounting factor $\Psi_{t_v^0}(\theta_0)/\Psi_\tau(\theta_0)$ with $\tau \le t_v^0$, or $z_v = 1$), the law of iterated expectations gives
\[
\mathbb{E}[z_v PV_v(\theta_0)] = \mathbb{E}\!\left[z_v \,\mathbb{E}[PV_v(\theta_0)\mid\mathcal{F}_{t_v^0}]\right] = 0.
\]
When $\tau > t_v^0$, the compounding weight $R^b_{t_v^0,\tau}(\theta_0) = \Psi_{t_v^0}(\theta_0)/\Psi_\tau(\theta_0)$ depends on public factor realizations after $t_v^0$ and is generally not $\mathcal{F}_{t_v^0}$-measurable. Under~(P) alone, taking expectations yields the compounding-horizon wedge (repaired Lemma~1(3) of the original submission):
\[
\mathbb{E}\!\left[\bar\epsilon_{v,H}(\theta_0)\,\middle|\,\mathcal{F}_{t_v^0}\right]
= \mathrm{Cov}\!\left(A_{v,H}(\theta_0),\, PV_v(\theta_0)\,\middle|\,\mathcal{F}_{t_v^0}\right)
=: b_{v,H},
\]
which is generically nonzero whenever cash-flow pricing errors covary with realized benchmark returns over $\mathcal{T}_{v,H}$. Under the stronger path-exogeneity condition~(X), $\mathbb{E}[PV_{v,i}(\theta_0)\mid\mathcal{G}_v] = 0$ for every fund, hence $\mathbb{E}[PV_v(\theta_0)\mid\mathcal{G}_v] = \sum_i w_{v,i}\,\mathbb{E}[PV_{v,i}(\theta_0)\mid\mathcal{G}_v] = 0$; since $A_{v,H}(\theta_0)$ is $\mathcal{G}_v$-measurable, $\mathbb{E}[\bar\epsilon_{v,H}(\theta_0)\mid\mathcal{G}_v] = A_{v,H}(\theta_0)\,\mathbb{E}[PV_v(\theta_0)\mid\mathcal{G}_v] = 0$, and $\mathbb{E}[\bar\epsilon_{v,H}(\theta_0)] = 0$ at every horizon $H \ge 0$. \qed

\subsection{Proof of T3 (Inception L2 variance tilt)}
\label{sec:proof_T3}

Let $m_v(\theta) := \mathbb{E}[PV_v(\theta)\mid\mathcal{F}_{t_v^0}]$. Under Condition~(P) with $\mathcal{F}_{t_v^0}$-measurable fund weights, $m_v(\theta_0) = 0$. The conditional variance decomposition gives
\[
\mathbb{E}\!\left[PV_v(\theta)^2\right] = \mathbb{E}\!\left[\operatorname{Var}\!\left(PV_v(\theta)\,\middle|\,\mathcal{F}_{t_v^0}\right)\right] + \mathbb{E}\!\left[m_v(\theta)^2\right].
\]
The gradient of the second term, $2\,\mathbb{E}[m_v(\theta)\nabla_\theta m_v(\theta)]$, vanishes at $\theta_0$ because $m_v(\theta_0) = 0$, which gives the first equality of T3; the second equality is direct differentiation of $\mathbb{E}[PV_v(\theta)^2]$. The gradient is generically nonzero: in the single-deal unit of Section~\ref{sec:fundwise_lmd_estimator} it equals $2\sigma_N^2\nabla_\theta\log\Psi_T(\theta_0)$ (Equation~\eqref{eq:one_deal_gradient} at $H=0$), which is nonzero whenever $\sigma_N^2 > 0$. \qed

\subsection{Proof of T4 (Finite-$n_v$ L2 decomposition)}
\label{sec:proof_T4}

Under constant-composition pooling, $PV_v(\theta) = \sum_{i=1}^{n_v} w_{v,i} PV_{v,i}(\theta)$. Suppose funds within vintage $v$ share conditional mean $\mu_v(\theta) = \mathbb{E}[PV_{v,i}(\theta)\mid\mathcal{G}_v]$, common conditional variance $\sigma_v^2(\theta) = \mathrm{Var}(PV_{v,i}(\theta)\mid\mathcal{G}_v)$, and common pairwise conditional covariance $c_v(\theta) = \mathrm{Cov}(PV_{v,i}(\theta), PV_{v,j}(\theta)\mid\mathcal{G}_v)$ for $i \neq j$. For equally weighted funds ($w_{v,i} = 1/n_v$),
\[
\mathbb{E}[PV_v(\theta)^2\mid\mathcal{G}_v]
= \mu_v(\theta)^2 + \mathrm{Var}(PV_v(\theta)\mid\mathcal{G}_v)
= \mu_v(\theta)^2 + c_v(\theta) + \frac{\sigma_v^2(\theta) - c_v(\theta)}{n_v}.
\]
(For general $\mathcal{G}_v$-measurable weights $w_{v,i}$ with Herfindahl index $H_v = \sum_{i=1}^{n_v} w_{v,i}^2$, $1/n_v$ is replaced by $H_v$, giving $c_v(\theta) + H_v(\sigma_v^2(\theta) - c_v(\theta))$.) Multiplying by $A_{v,H}(\theta)^2$ (which is $\mathcal{G}_v$-measurable) and taking unconditional expectations yields
\[
Q_H^{\mathrm{L2}}(\theta)
= \mathbb{E}\!\left[A_{v,H}(\theta)^2 \mu_v(\theta)^2\right]
+ \mathbb{E}\!\left[A_{v,H}(\theta)^2 c_v(\theta)\right]
+ \mathbb{E}\!\left[A_{v,H}(\theta)^2 \frac{\sigma_v^2(\theta) - c_v(\theta)}{n_v}\right],
\]
with $n_v^{-1}$ kept inside the expectation when fund counts vary across vintages. T4 is the case $c_v \equiv 0$ of conditionally independent funds; the general case is used for T8 and Lemma~\ref{lem:infill}. Under~(X), $\mu_v(\theta_0) = 0$ almost surely, so the signal term $\mathbb{E}[A_{v,H}(\theta_0)^2\mu_v(\theta_0)^2]$ and its gradient vanish at $\theta_0$, while the noise terms depend on $\theta$ through $A_{v,H}(\theta)^2$ and the discounted-payoff dispersion; T6 gives their gradient. \qed

\subsection{Proof of T5 (Horizon gradient decomposition)}
\label{sec:proof_T5}

By T1, $\bar\epsilon_{v,H}(\theta)^2 = A_{v,H}(\theta)^2 PV_v(\theta)^2$ pathwise, so the product rule gives
\[
\nabla_\theta \bar\epsilon_{v,H}(\theta)^2 = 2A_{v,H}(\theta)^2 PV_v(\theta)\nabla_\theta PV_v(\theta) + 2A_{v,H}(\theta)\nabla_\theta A_{v,H}(\theta)\,PV_v(\theta)^2;
\]
taking expectations yields T5. The same identity holds for the sample criterion,
\[
\nabla_\theta Q_{V,H}^{\mathrm{L2}}(\theta)
= \underbrace{\frac{2}{V}\sum_{v=1}^V A_{v,H}(\theta)^2 PV_v(\theta)\,\nabla_\theta PV_v(\theta)}_{\text{Term~(I): reweighted inception score}}
+ \underbrace{\frac{2}{V}\sum_{v=1}^V A_{v,H}(\theta)^2 PV_v(\theta)^2\,\nabla_\theta \log A_{v,H}(\theta)}_{\text{Term~(II): Scale-Jacobian term}}.
\]
Term~(I) is the gradient of the Fix-$A$ criterion $V^{-1}\sum_v A_{v,H}(\bar\theta)^2 PV_v(\theta)^2$ with the horizon weights held fixed at $\bar\theta = \theta$ (Table~\ref{tab:fixA_jacobian}); Term~(II) comes from the $\theta$-dependence of $A_{v,H}$. \qed

\subsection{Proof of T6 (The horizon displays the tilt; it does not remove it)}
\label{sec:proof_T6}

Under the conditions of T4 ($c_v \equiv 0$, equal weights),
\[
Q_H^{\mathrm{L2}}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}[A_{v,H}(\theta)^2\mu_v(\theta)^2] + \mathbb{E}[n_v^{-1}A_{v,H}(\theta)^2\sigma_v^2(\theta)].
\]
The gradient of the first term, $2\,\mathbb{E}[A_{v,H}^2\mu_v\nabla_\theta\mu_v + A_{v,H}\nabla_\theta A_{v,H}\,\mu_v^2]$, vanishes at $\theta_0$ because $\mu_v(\theta_0) = 0$ under~(X). Differentiating the second term gives
\[
\nabla_\theta Q_H^{\mathrm{L2}}(\theta_0)
= \mathbb{E}\!\left[n_v^{-1}A_{v,H}(\theta_0)^2\left(\nabla_\theta\sigma_v^2(\theta_0) + 2\,\sigma_v^2(\theta_0)\,\nabla_\theta\log A_{v,H}(\theta_0)\right)\right]
\]
(with $n_v^{-1}$ replaced by $\sum_i w_{v,i}^2$ under general $\mathcal{G}_v$-measurable weights). At $H = 0$, $A_{v,0} \equiv 1$ and only $\mathbb{E}[n_v^{-1}\nabla_\theta\sigma_v^2(\theta_0)]$ remains, the inception tilt of T3; for $H > 0$ the Scale-Jacobian term is added. Whenever $\theta_0$ is interior and $\nabla_\theta Q_H^{\mathrm{L2}}(\theta_0) \neq 0$, the target $\theta_H^\dagger$ differs from $\theta_0$; a horizon with $\theta_H^\dagger = \theta_0$ requires the two terms to cancel.

\emph{Signs in the single-exit deal model.} Let fund $i$ in vintage $v$ make deals $j$ with outlay $c_j > 0$ at $a_j \ge t_v^0$ and exit payoff $c_j R^b_{a_j,b_j}(\theta_0)(1+\varepsilon_{i,j})$ at $b_j > a_j$, with independent mean-zero shocks, $\mathrm{Var}(\varepsilon_{i,j}\mid\mathcal{G}_v) = \nu_j > 0$, deals identically distributed across the funds of a vintage, and $c_v \equiv 0$. At $\theta$, fund $i$'s pricing error is $PV_{v,i}(\theta) = \sum_j c_j \Psi_{t_v^0,a_j}(\theta)[\Psi_{a_j,b_j}(\theta)R^b_{a_j,b_j}(\theta_0)(1+\varepsilon_{i,j}) - 1]$, so
\[
\sigma_v^2(\theta) = \sum_j c_j^2 \nu_j \left(\frac{R^b_{a_j,b_j}(\theta_0)}{R^b_{t_v^0,b_j}(\theta)}\right)^2.
\]
Write $S_{t_v^0,b_j}(\theta) := \nabla_\theta \log R^b_{t_v^0,b_j}(\theta)$ and $\bar S_{v,H}(\theta) := \nabla_\theta \log A_{v,H}(\theta)$. Using
\[
R^b_{t_v^0,b_j}(\theta_0) = R^b_{t_v^0,a_j}(\theta_0)\,R^b_{a_j,b_j}(\theta_0),
\]
the gradient display above becomes
\[
\nabla_\theta Q_H^{\mathrm{L2}}(\theta_0)
= 2\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[n_v^{-1}\, A_{v,H}(\theta_0)^2 \sum_j \frac{c_j^2 \nu_j}{R^b_{t_v^0,a_j}(\theta_0)^2}\left(\bar S_{v,H}(\theta_0) - S_{t_v^0,b_j}(\theta_0)\right)\right],
\]
keeping $n_v^{-1}$ inside the expectation when fund counts vary across vintages. At $H=0$, $\bar S_{v,0}(\theta_0) = 0$. Since $\partial_\alpha \log R^b_{t_v^0,b_j}(\theta_0) = \sum_{u=t_v^0+1}^{b_j} G_u(\theta_0)^{-1} > 0$, $\partial_\alpha Q_0^{\mathrm{L2}}(\theta_0) < 0$ strictly. For single-factor $\beta$, $\partial_\beta \log R^b_{t_v^0,b_j}(\theta_0) = \sum_{u=t_v^0+1}^{b_j} F_u/G_u(\theta_0)$; when these sums are positive over the deal windows (positive realized excess market returns), $\partial_\beta Q_0^{\mathrm{L2}}(\beta_0) < 0$ and the inception tilt of $\beta_0^\dagger$ is upward. As $H$ rises, $\bar S_{v,H}(\theta_0)$ increases from $0$; once it exceeds the deal sensitivities $S_{t_v^0,b_j}(\theta_0)$, the sign of the gradient reverses and the target is pulled downward (for $\alpha$ under $G_u \equiv 1$, at $2T+1$ valuation dates; Section~\ref{sec:fundwise_lmd_estimator}). In the joint $(\alpha,\beta)$ model at $H=0$, first-order linearization gives $\theta_0^\dagger - \theta_0 \approx -[\nabla^2 Q_0^{\mathrm{L2}}(\theta_0)]^{-1}\nabla Q_0^{\mathrm{L2}}(\theta_0)$. If $[\nabla^2 Q_0^{\mathrm{L2}}(\theta_0)]_{\alpha\beta} > 0$ (its sample analog at $(\alpha,\beta) = (0,1)$ is positive in all four of our samples, Table~\ref{tab:alpha_beta_identification}), the off-diagonal element of the inverse Hessian is negative, so the large negative $\alpha$-score shifts $\alpha_0^\dagger$ upward and $\beta_0^\dagger$ downward, formalizing the qualitative observation in \citet[p.~522]{DLP12}. \qed

\subsection{Proof of T7 (Identification of $U_{v,H}$ under bounded $n_v \ge 2$)}
\label{sec:proof_T7}

For any vintage $v$ with $n_v \ge 2$ funds and normalized fund weights $w_{v,i} > 0$ ($\sum_{i=1}^{n_v} w_{v,i} = 1$; $w_{v,i} = 1/n_v$ under EW, $w_{v,i} = s_{v,i}/\sum_k s_{v,k}$ under FW), the cross-fund pair-product statistic is
\[
U_{v,H}(\theta)
= \frac{\sum_{i \neq j} w_{v,i} w_{v,j}\, \bar\epsilon_{v,i,H}(\theta)\, \bar\epsilon_{v,j,H}(\theta)}{\sum_{i \neq j} w_{v,i} w_{v,j}}
= A_{v,H}(\theta)^2 \frac{\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n_v} w_{v,i} PV_{v,i}(\theta)\right)^2 - \sum_{i=1}^{n_v} w_{v,i}^2 PV_{v,i}(\theta)^2}{1 - \sum_{i=1}^{n_v} w_{v,i}^2},
\]
using $\sum_{i\neq j}w_{v,i}w_{v,j} = 1 - \sum_i w_{v,i}^2$. Under the conditions of T7, funds $i \neq j$ are conditionally independent given $\mathcal{G}_v$, so $\mathbb{E}[PV_{v,i}(\theta)PV_{v,j}(\theta)\mid\mathcal{G}_v] = \mu_{v,i}(\theta)\mu_{v,j}(\theta)$ and
\[
\mathbb{E}\!\left[U_{v,H}(\theta)\,\middle|\,\mathcal{G}_v\right]
= A_{v,H}(\theta)^2\,
  \frac{\sum_{i \neq j} w_{v,i} w_{v,j} \mu_{v,i}(\theta)\mu_{v,j}(\theta)}{\sum_{i \neq j} w_{v,i} w_{v,j}};
\]
all own-fund variance terms cancel for every finite $n_v \ge 2$. Multiplying by the $\mathcal{G}_v$-measurable weight $q_v$ and taking expectations gives the display in T7. Under~(X), $\mu_{v,i}(\theta_0) = 0$ for every $i$, so the expectation is $0$ at $\theta_0$ for every $H$. With a common conditional mean, the pair sum equals $\mu_v(\theta)^2\sum_{i\neq j}w_{v,i}w_{v,j}$ and the conditional expectation is $A_{v,H}(\theta)^2\mu_v(\theta)^2 \ge 0$, so $\theta_0$ is a global minimizer. With size-dependent means, $\sum_{i\neq j}w_{v,i}w_{v,j}\mu_{v,i}\mu_{v,j} = (\sum_i w_{v,i}\mu_{v,i})^2 - \sum_i w_{v,i}^2\mu_{v,i}^2$ can be negative away from $\theta_0$, so $\theta_0$ need not minimize the population criterion and identification must be assumed. Letting $V \to \infty$ under the uniform law of large numbers of Appendix~\ref{sec:proofs_asymptotics_H}, $V^{-1}\sum_v q_v U_{v,H}(\theta)$ converges uniformly on $\Theta$ to $\mathbb{E}[q_v U_{v,H}(\theta)]$ (normalizing by $\sum_v q_v$ instead of $V$, as in $\widehat U_H$, rescales the limit by $1/\mathbb{E}[q_v]$ and leaves the minimizer unchanged). If $\theta_0$ is its unique minimizer on the compact $\Theta$, $\hat\theta_H^U \xrightarrow{p} \theta_0$ at every fixed $H \ge 0$ with bounded $n_v \ge 2$ \citep[Theorem~2.1]{NM94}. \qed

\subsection{Proof of T8 (Shared vintage shocks)}
\label{sec:proof_T8}

Under the conditions of T8, the funds of vintage $v$ have common conditional mean $\mu_v(\theta)$ and pairwise conditional covariance $c_v(\theta)$ given $\mathcal{G}_v$, so $\mathbb{E}[PV_{v,i}(\theta)PV_{v,j}(\theta)\mid\mathcal{G}_v] = \mu_v(\theta)^2 + c_v(\theta)$ for $i \neq j$. Substituting into the pair representation of $U_{v,H}$ in the proof of T7 gives $\mathbb{E}[U_{v,H}(\theta)\mid\mathcal{G}_v] = A_{v,H}(\theta)^2(\mu_v(\theta)^2 + c_v(\theta))$; own-fund variances again cancel. Multiplying by $q_v$ and taking expectations yields T8. Because $\mu_v(\theta_0) = 0$, the gradient at $\theta_0$ is $\mathbb{E}[q_v A_{v,H}(\theta_0)^2(\nabla_\theta c_v(\theta_0) + 2c_v(\theta_0)\nabla_\theta\log A_{v,H}(\theta_0))]$, the expression of T6 with $n_v^{-1}\sigma_v^2$ replaced by $c_v$. \qed

\begin{lemma}[Large-$\alpha$ attenuation at $H=0$ versus coercivity at $H>0$]
\label{lem:coercivity}
Fix $\beta$ and drop the box constraint on $\alpha$.
(a) As $\alpha \to +\infty$, $PV_{v,i}(\alpha,\beta) \to CF_{v,i,t_v^0}$, so $Q_0^{\mathrm{L2}}(\alpha,\beta) \to \mathbb{E}[CF_{v,t_v^0}^2]$; under a predetermined origin with $CF_{v,t_v^0} = 0$, both $Q_0^{\mathrm{L2}}$ and $U_{v,0}$ tend to $0$. Hence, whenever $\mathbb{E}[PV_v(\theta_0)^2] > \mathbb{E}[CF_{v,t_v^0}^2]$, the $H=0$ squared-loss criterion is lower as $\alpha \to +\infty$ than at $\theta_0$.
(b) If $H > 0$, so that $\mathcal{T}_{v,H}$ contains a date $\tau^\ast$ after the first nonzero cash flow $t_v^{\mathrm{first}} \ge t_v^0$, then $|\bar\epsilon_{v,H}(\alpha,\beta)| \to +\infty$ and $Q_H^{\mathrm{L2}}(\alpha,\beta) \to +\infty$; $U_{v,H}(\alpha,\beta) \to +\infty$ whenever at least two funds of the vintage have a nonzero cash flow at $t_v^{\mathrm{first}}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(a) As $\alpha \to +\infty$, $G_u(\alpha,\beta) \to +\infty$ for every month $u$, so $\Psi_{t_v^0,t}(\alpha,\beta) = \prod_{u=t_v^0+1}^t G_u(\alpha,\beta)^{-1} \to 0$ for every $t > t_v^0$, while $\Psi_{t_v^0,t_v^0} = 1$; hence $PV_{v,i}(\alpha,\beta) \to CF_{v,i,t_v^0}$ and the limits follow.
(b) For $t = t_v^{\mathrm{first}} < \tau^\ast$, the compounding factor $R^b_{t_v^{\mathrm{first}},\tau^\ast}(\alpha,\beta) = \prod_{u=t_v^{\mathrm{first}}+1}^{\tau^\ast} G_u(\alpha,\beta)$ grows like $\alpha^{\tau^\ast - t_v^{\mathrm{first}}}$ and dominates every later cash flow, which grows at strictly lower degree in $\alpha$. Thus $|\bar\epsilon_{v,H}(\alpha,\beta)| \to +\infty$ and $Q_H^{\mathrm{L2}} \to +\infty$. For $U_{v,H}$, initial capital calls $CF_{v,i,t_v^{\mathrm{first}}} < 0$ have the same sign across funds, so the dominant pair products $CF_{v,i,t_v^{\mathrm{first}}}CF_{v,j,t_v^{\mathrm{first}}} > 0$ are strictly positive when at least two funds invest at $t_v^{\mathrm{first}}$, and $U_{v,H}(\alpha,\beta) \to +\infty$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Large-$\alpha$ behavior of linear mean-moment (IV) estimators]
\label{rem:iv_alpha}
Consider a linear mean-moment vector $\bar{g}_0(\alpha,\beta) = V^{-1}\sum_{v=1}^V z_v PV_v(\alpha,\beta)$ for joint $(\alpha,\beta)$ estimation. Because the moment is linear in $PV_v$, neither idiosyncratic fund noise nor zero-mean shared vintage shocks generate a population variance tilt at $\theta_0$. However, large-$\alpha$ boundary behavior depends critically on the origin convention:
(i) under a predetermined origin with $CF_{v,t_v^0} = 0$, $PV_v(\alpha,\beta) \to 0$ for every vintage as $\alpha \to +\infty$, so $\bar{g}_0(\alpha,\beta) \to 0$ for \emph{any} instrument vector $z_v$---making $\alpha = +\infty$ a spurious global zero of un-studentized GMM (and in a studentized or continuously-updated AR statistic, both $\bar{g}_0$ and its standard error vanish at the same rate $O(\alpha^{-\Delta})$ determined by the earliest nonzero cash flow, so the test statistic approaches a finite limit rather than $+\infty$);
(ii) under the first-cash-flow origin ($CF_{v,t_v^0} < 0$), $\bar{g}_0(\alpha,\beta) \to V^{-1}\sum_v z_v CF_{v,t_v^0} \neq 0$ (strictly negative for $z_{1,v} \equiv 1$), ruling out the $+\infty$ root, though strong positive cohort realizations can still push finite-sample roots above the $+2\%$ bound and two-parameter IV faces severe weak-instrument collinearity on $V \approx 25$ vintages;
(iii) compounding by $A_{v,H}(\alpha,\beta)$ ($H>0$) sends the moment to $-\infty$ as $\alpha \to +\infty$ under either origin, eliminating the predetermined-origin $+\infty$ root at the cost of requiring Condition~(X) rather than (P) alone.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[Infill consistency, $n_v \to \infty$]
\label{lem:infill}
(a) Fix $V$ and let $n_v \to \infty$ for each $v$. If common vintage shocks are absent and $\theta_0$ uniquely sets $V^{-1}\sum_v A_{v,H}(\theta)^2\tilde\mu_v(\theta)^2$ to zero on $\Theta$, where $\tilde\mu_v(\theta)$ is the vintage's realized conditional mean, then $\hat\theta_H^{\mathrm{L2}} \xrightarrow{p} \theta_0$ at every fixed $H \ge 0$; with unspanned common vintage shocks, fixed-$V$ infill converges instead to the random minimizer of that limit. (b) Letting both $n_v \to \infty$ and $V \to \infty$ eliminates the $O(1/n_v)$ term of T4, with limit criterion $\mathbb{E}[A_{v,H}(\theta)^2(\mu_v(\theta)^2 + c_v(\theta))]$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(a) Conditional on $\mathcal{G}_v$ and any common vintage shock, the constant-composition pool NPV converges in probability by a within-vintage law of large numbers to $\tilde\mu_v(\theta)$. Hence
\[
V^{-1}\sum_{v=1}^V A_{v,H}(\theta)^2 PV_v(\theta)^2 \xrightarrow{p} V^{-1}\sum_{v=1}^V A_{v,H}(\theta)^2 \tilde\mu_v(\theta)^2
\]
uniformly on the compact domain $\Theta$, and the minimizer converges to that of the limit. Without common shocks, $\tilde\mu_v(\theta_0) = 0$ for all $v$. (b) Apply the decomposition in the proof of T4 and the increasing-domain uniform law of large numbers of Appendix~\ref{sec:proofs_asymptotics_H}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Multiple roots of the mean pricing moment]
\label{lem:multiple_roots}
In a one-period model with risk-free gross return $R_f$, excess market return $F$ taking finitely many values with $\mathbb{E}[F] > 0$ and $\mathbb{P}(F < 0) > 0$, and PE payoff $(R_f + \beta_{\mathrm{PE}} F)N$ with $\mathbb{E}[N\mid F] = 1$, the single-factor mean pricing moment with $\alpha = 0$,
\[
m(\beta) = \mathbb{E}\!\left[\frac{R_f + \beta_{\mathrm{PE}} F}{R_f + \beta F} - 1\right]
= (\beta_{\mathrm{PE}} - \beta)\,g(\beta),
\qquad g(\beta) := \mathbb{E}\!\left[\frac{F}{R_f + \beta F}\right],
\]
has, besides the structural root $\beta_{\mathrm{PE}}$, a second root $\beta^\ast > 0$ on the positive-return domain, the growth-optimal exposure solving $g(\beta^\ast) = 0$; the two coincide only if $\beta_{\mathrm{PE}} = \beta^\ast$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$g(0) = \mathbb{E}[F]/R_f > 0$; $g$ is strictly decreasing because $g'(\beta) = -\mathbb{E}[F^2/(R_f+\beta F)^2] < 0$; and $g(\beta) \to -\infty$ as $\beta$ approaches the upper positivity boundary $R_f/|\min F|$, where the denominator of the term at $\min F < 0$ vanishes. Hence $g$ has exactly one root $\beta^\ast > 0$. For example, with $R_f = 1$ and $F \in \{-0.4, +0.6\}$ equally likely, $\beta_{\mathrm{PE}} = 1$ and $\beta^\ast = 5/12$ both satisfy $m(\beta) = 0$, so a ``smallest-root'' rule selects $5/12 \neq 1$.
\end{proof}

Under persistent vintage shocks ($c_v > 0$, $\rho_W > 0$), any instrument $z_v$ constructed from neighboring vintages' cash flows correlates with $PV_v(\theta_0)$; valid linear moments $\mathbb{E}[z_v PV_v(\theta_0)] = 0$ therefore require strictly exogenous path instruments $z_v = h(\mathcal{C}_v)$ (valid under (X)), $z_v = 1$ under (P) alone (T2), or $h$-block neighbor-excluding instruments (whereas quadratic cross-vintage leave-band-out pair products fail via rank-1 collapse and finite-$V$ indefiniteness, proved next).

\subsection{Proofs and quantitative analysis of Proposition~\ref{prop:optimal_H} (optimal horizon theory)}
\label{sec:appendix_optimal_H}

This subsection proves Parts~(i)--(iv) of Proposition~\ref{prop:optimal_H}, reports exact numerical lookup tables for $H_0^\ast(T_{\mathrm{eff}}, g, \sigma_m)$ and $T_{\mathrm{eff}}$ across shock mechanisms (Tables~\ref{tab:H_star_lookup}--\ref{tab:T_eff_mechanisms}), and verifies all three dependence regimes and the failure of cross-vintage leave-band-out pair products in a dedicated nonlinear cash-flow Monte Carlo study (Table~\ref{tab:mc_regimes_verification}).

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:optimal_H}]
We prove Parts~(i)--(iv) in sequence.

\paragraph{Part (i): Exact multi-deal duration matching, uniqueness, global bounds, and volatility shrinkage.}
Under Condition~(X), $\mu_v(\theta_0) = \mathbb{E}[PV_i(\theta_0) \mid \mathcal{G}_v] = 0$, so both $\mu_v(\theta)^2$ and its gradient $2\mu_v(\theta)\nabla_\theta\mu_v(\theta)$ vanish at $\theta = \theta_0$.
Differentiating $\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{L}_{v,H}(\theta) \mid \mathcal{G}_v] = q_v A_{v,H}(\theta)^2(\mu_v(\theta)^2 + \sigma_{\mathrm{eff},v}^2(\theta))$ under the expectation and evaluating at $\theta_0$ gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:app_score_general}
\nabla_\theta\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[\mathcal{L}_{v,H}(\theta) \,\middle|\, \mathcal{G}_v\right]\Big|_{\theta=\theta_0}
= q_v A_{v,H}(\theta_0)^2\Big(2\,\sigma_{\mathrm{eff},v}^2(\theta_0)\,\nabla_\theta\log A_{v,H}(\theta_0) + \nabla_\theta\sigma_{\mathrm{eff},v}^2(\theta_0)\Big).
\end{equation}
First, since $A_{v,H}(\theta) = |\mathcal{T}_{v,H}|^{-1}\sum_{\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{v,H}} R^b_{t_v^0,\tau}(\theta)$ and $\nabla_\theta R^b_{t_v^0,\tau}(\theta) = R^b_{t_v^0,\tau}(\theta)\,S_{v,\tau}(\theta)$, we have
\[
\nabla_\theta\log A_{v,H}(\theta_0)
= \frac{\sum_{\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{v,H}} R^b_{t_v^0,\tau}(\theta_0)\,S_{v,\tau}(\theta_0)}{\sum_{\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{v,H}} R^b_{t_v^0,\tau}(\theta_0)}
= \bar{S}_{v,H}^{\mathrm{comp}}(\theta_0).
\]
Second, write $\sigma_{\mathrm{eff},v}^2(\theta) = \sum_{s,t \ge t_v^0} D_{v,s}(\theta) D_{v,t}(\theta)\,\Sigma_v^{(s,t)}$ where $D_{v,t}(\theta) = R^b_{t_v^0,t}(\theta)^{-1}$ satisfies $\nabla_\theta D_{v,t}(\theta) = -D_{v,t}(\theta)\,S_{v,t}(\theta)$ and $\Sigma_v^{(s,t)} = \Sigma_v^{(t,s)}$ does not depend on $\theta$.
Differentiating yields
\[
\nabla_\theta\sigma_{\mathrm{eff},v}^2(\theta_0)
= -\sum_{s,t\ge t_v^0} D_{v,s}(\theta_0)D_{v,t}(\theta_0)\,\Sigma_v^{(s,t)}\bigl(S_{v,s}(\theta_0) + S_{v,t}(\theta_0)\bigr)
= -2\,\sigma_{\mathrm{eff},v}^2(\theta_0)\,\bar{S}_v^{\mathrm{eff}}(\theta_0),
\]
by symmetry of $\Sigma_v^{(s,t)}$. Substituting both expressions into \eqref{eq:app_score_general}, taking unconditional expectations, and averaging across $v = 1,\dots,V$ yields \eqref{eq:duration_matching_score}.

Now specialize to a constant per-period gross benchmark return $G_u(\theta_0) \equiv G = e^r > 0$.
For $\theta = \alpha$, $S_{v,t}^{(\alpha)}(\theta_0) = (t - t_v^0)/G$ (and for $\theta = \beta$ with constant excess return $F_u \equiv \bar{F} > 0$, $S_{v,t}^{(\beta)}(\theta_0) = (\bar{F}/G)(t - t_v^0)$).
In both cases the positive scalar factor ($1/G$ or $\bar{F}/G$) cancels from $\bar{S}_{v,H}^{\mathrm{comp}} - \bar{S}_v^{\mathrm{eff}} = 0$, leaving the discrete duration-matching equation
\[
\frac{\sum_{\tau=0}^{K_H} \tau\,G^\tau}{\sum_{\tau=0}^{K_H} G^\tau} = T_{\mathrm{eff}},
\qquad
T_{\mathrm{eff}} := \frac{\sum_{s,t\ge 0} G^{-(s+t)}\,\Sigma^{(s,t)}\,t}{\sum_{s,t\ge 0} G^{-(s+t)}\,\Sigma^{(s,t)}} > 0.
\]
Note that even on an arbitrary realized positive path $R_\tau > 0$, the discrete ratio $\bar\tau(K) := \frac{\sum_{\tau=0}^K \tau R_\tau}{\sum_{\tau=0}^K R_\tau}$ is strictly increasing in $K$ because
\[
\bar\tau(K+1) - \bar\tau(K)
= \frac{R_{K+1}}{\sum_{\tau=0}^{K+1} R_\tau}\,\bigl((K+1) - \bar\tau(K)\bigr) > 0.
\]
In the continuous-time counterpart on $[0,H]$, integration by parts gives
\[
\bar\tau(H;\,r)
= \frac{\int_0^H \tau e^{r\tau}\,d\tau}{\int_0^H e^{r\tau}\,d\tau}
= \frac{r^{-2}\bigl((rH - 1)e^{rH} + 1\bigr)}{r^{-1}(e^{rH} - 1)}
= H - \frac{1}{r} + \frac{H}{e^{rH} - 1}
\]
for $r \neq 0$, with continuous limit $\bar\tau(H;0) = H/2$ by L'H\^opital's rule.
Setting $x := rH > 0$, direct differentiation gives
\[
\frac{\partial \bar\tau(H;\,r)}{\partial H}
= 1 + \frac{(e^x - 1) - x e^x}{(e^x - 1)^2}
= \frac{e^x(e^x - 1 - x)}{(e^x - 1)^2} > 0 \qquad \forall\, x > 0,
\]
since $e^x > 1 + x$ for all $x \neq 0$.
Because $\lim_{H\downarrow 0}\bar\tau(H;r) = 0 < T_{\mathrm{eff}}$ and $\lim_{H\to\infty}\bar\tau(H;r) = \infty$ for $r \ge 0$, the intermediate value theorem guarantees a unique zero-bias root $H_0^\ast \in (0,\infty)$.
Next, interpret $\bar\tau(H;r) = \mathbb{E}_{\pi_r}[\tau]$ under the exponential-family density $\pi_r(\tau) = e^{r\tau}/\int_0^H e^{ru}\,du$ on $[0,H]$.
By the standard exponential-family cumulant identity,
\[
\frac{\partial \bar\tau(H;\,r)}{\partial r} = \operatorname{Var}_{\pi_r}(\tau) > 0 \qquad \forall\, H > 0.
\]
By the implicit function theorem, $\frac{\partial H_0^\ast}{\partial r} = -\frac{\operatorname{Var}_{\pi_r}(\tau)}{\partial_H \bar\tau(H_0^\ast;r)} < 0$, so $H_0^\ast$ is strictly decreasing in $r$.
For the global sandwich bounds \eqref{eq:H_star_bounds} when $r > 0$:
(a) $\bar\tau(H_0^\ast;r) < H_0^\ast$ immediately gives $T_{\mathrm{eff}} < H_0^\ast$;
(b) strict monotonicity in $r$ implies $T_{\mathrm{eff}} = \bar\tau(H_0^\ast;r) > \bar\tau(H_0^\ast;0) = H_0^\ast/2$, giving $H_0^\ast < 2\,T_{\mathrm{eff}}$;
(c) rewriting \eqref{eq:exact_tau_bar} as $H_0^\ast = T_{\mathrm{eff}} + \frac{1}{r} - \frac{H_0^\ast}{e^{rH_0^\ast}-1}$ and noting $\frac{H_0^\ast}{e^{rH_0^\ast}-1} > 0$ yields $H_0^\ast < T_{\mathrm{eff}} + \frac{1}{r}$.
Combining (a)--(c) establishes \eqref{eq:H_star_bounds}.
Furthermore, expanding $\frac{x}{e^x-1} = 1 - \frac{x}{2} + \frac{x^2}{12} + O(x^4)$ gives $\bar\tau(H;r) = \frac{H}{2} + \frac{rH^2}{12} + O(r^3 H^4)$, solving to $H_0^\ast = 2T_{\mathrm{eff}} - \frac{2}{3}rT_{\mathrm{eff}}^2 + O(r^2 T_{\mathrm{eff}}^3)$ for $rT_{\mathrm{eff}} \ll 1$, while $e^{rH}\gg 1$ yields the large-$rT_{\mathrm{eff}}$ asymptote $H_0^\ast \approx T_{\mathrm{eff}} + \frac{1}{r}$.

Finally, consider stochastic i.i.d.\ lognormal returns $r_u \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_r,\sigma_r^2)$ with arithmetic drift $r_{\mathrm{arith}} = \mu_r + \frac{1}{2}\sigma_r^2$ and continuous-time log-rate parameterization $R_t(\alpha) = \exp(\alpha t + X_t)$, where $X_t = \mu_r t + \sigma_r B_t$ is Brownian motion (so $R_t := R_t(0) = \exp(X_t)$ and $\partial_\alpha R_t(0) = t R_t$).
For constant-duration $\alpha$ with deterministic $T_{\mathrm{eff}}$, the zero-bias condition $\mathbb{E}[A_H(\theta_0)^2(\bar{S}_H^{\mathrm{comp}}(\theta_0) - T_{\mathrm{eff}})] = 0$ rewrites (using $A_H \partial_\alpha A_H = H^{-2}\int_0^H\int_0^H t\,R_s R_t\,ds\,dt$) as
\[
\bar\tau_{\mathrm{stoch}}(H;\,r_{\mathrm{arith}},\sigma_r)
:= \frac{\int_0^H\int_0^H t\,\mathbb{E}[R_s R_t]\,ds\,dt}{\int_0^H\int_0^H \mathbb{E}[R_s R_t]\,ds\,dt}
= T_{\mathrm{eff}}.
\]
Because $X_s + X_t$ is Gaussian with mean $\mu_r(s+t)$ and variance $\sigma_r^2(s + t + 2\min(s,t))$,
\[
\mathbb{E}[R_s R_t] = \exp\!\Big(r_{\mathrm{arith}}(s+t) + \sigma_r^2\min(s,t)\Big).
\]
Differentiating $\bar\tau_{\mathrm{stoch}}$ with respect to $\sigma_r^2$ (holding $r_{\mathrm{arith}}$ fixed) gives
\[
\frac{\partial \bar\tau_{\mathrm{stoch}}(H;\,r_{\mathrm{arith}},\sigma_r)}{\partial (\sigma_r^2)}
= \operatorname{Cov}_{\tilde\pi}\!\bigl(t,\,\min(s,t)\bigr) > 0,
\]
where $\tilde\pi(s,t) \propto \mathbb{E}[R_s R_t]$ on $[0,H]^2$ (positive covariance follows directly from the FKG / Harris inequality because both $t$ and $\min(s,t)$ are coordinatewise non-decreasing functions on $[0,H]^2$ and $\tilde\pi$ is log-supermodular: $\partial_{s,t}\log\tilde\pi = \sigma_r^2\,\delta(s-t) \ge 0$).
Hence $\bar\tau_{\mathrm{stoch}}(H; r_{\mathrm{arith}}, \sigma_r) > \bar\tau(H; r_{\mathrm{arith}})$ for all $\sigma_r > 0$, and strict monotonicity in $H$ implies $H_0^{\ast,\mathrm{stoch}} < H_0^{\ast,\mathrm{det}}(r_{\mathrm{arith}})$.

\paragraph{Part (ii): Cross-vintage dependence of nearby vintages.}
(a) For each vintage $v$, $U_{v,H}(\theta)$ pairs only funds $i \neq j \in \mathcal{I}_v$. Because $\nu_i \perp \nu_j$ and $\nu_i \perp \eta_v$ conditionally on $\mathcal{G}_v$,
\[
\mathbb{E}\!\left[U_{v,H}(\theta) \,\middle|\, \mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{pub}},\mathcal{W}\right]
= A_{v,H}(\theta)^2\bigl(\mu_v(\theta)^2 + c_{v,v}(\theta)\bigr),
\]
independently of $c_{v,w}(\theta)$ ($v \neq w$). By linearity of expectation,
\[
\mathbb{E}\!\left[\widehat{U}_H(\theta)\right]
= \frac{1}{\sum_v q_v}\sum_{v=1}^V q_v\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[A_{v,H}(\theta)^2\bigl(\mu_v(\theta)^2 + c_v(\theta)\bigr)\right],
\]
and under the increasing-domain mixing/local-dependence conditions of Proposition~\ref{prop:consistency_clt}, the ULLN ensures $\widehat{U}_H(\theta) \xrightarrow{p} Q_H(\theta)$ uniformly on $\Theta$. Thus off-diagonal covariances $c_{v,w}$ ($v \neq w$) do not enter $Q_H(\theta)$ or $H_0^\ast$. However, because the cross-vintage covariance matrix $[c_{v,w}(\theta_0)]_{v,w=1}^V$ is positive semidefinite, any pair with $c_{v,w}(\theta_0) \neq 0$ must satisfy $c_v(\theta_0) c_w(\theta_0) \ge c_{v,w}(\theta_0)^2 > 0$, so $c_v(\theta_0) > 0$ and $U_{v,0}$ inherits the inception variance tilt $\nabla_\theta c_v(\theta_0) \neq 0$.

(b) Under a stationary vintage shock $W_v$ with $\operatorname{Var}(W_v) = \sigma_W^2$ and $\operatorname{Cov}(W_v, W_w) = \sigma_W^2 \rho_W^{|y_v-y_w|}$, the within-vintage cross-fund cash-flow covariance $\Sigma_{v,U}^{(s,t)} = \sigma_W^2 \mu_{v,s}^{\mathrm{exit}}\mu_{v,t}^{\mathrm{exit}}$ is independent of $\rho_W$, so $T_{\mathrm{eff}}$ and $H_0^\ast$ are invariant to $\rho_W$.
By contrast, under a zero-mean calendar-time private shock $e_u \perp \mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{pub}}$ accumulated over each deal's active period $[a,b]$ (linearized multiplicative shock $\sum_{u=a+1}^b e_u$ with $\operatorname{Cov}(e_u,e_{u'}) = \gamma_e(|u-u'|)$), the cross-fund covariance between deals $(a_1,b_1)$ and $(a_2,b_2)$ in vintage $v$ is
\[
\operatorname{Cov}\!\left(\sum_{u=a_1+1}^{b_1} e_u,\;\sum_{u'=a_2+1}^{b_2} e_{u'}\right)
= \sum_{u=a_1+1}^{b_1}\sum_{u'=a_2+1}^{b_2} \gamma_e(|u-u'|),
\]
which reduces to $\sigma_e^2\,|[a_1,b_1]\cap[a_2,b_2]|$ when $e_u$ is i.i.d.
For any deal $(a_2,b_2)$, its expected overlap $\mathbb{E}_{a_1,b_1}[|[a_1,b_1]\cap[a_2,b_2]|]$ is strictly increasing in the exit date $b_2$ (holding $a_2$ fixed), and positively correlated with $b_2$ unconditionally. By Chebyshev's sum inequality, weighting exit dates $b_2$ by expected overlap strictly increases the effective duration $T_{\mathrm{eff}}$ relative to the unweighted PV-average exit date of a static shock $W_v$, shifting $H_0^\ast$ strictly upward.

(c) Differentiating $U_{v,H}(\theta)$ at $\theta_0$ (using $PV_i(\theta_0) = \eta_v + \nu_i$ and $\nabla_\theta PV_i(\theta_0) = \mu_v'(\theta_0) + \eta_v' + \nu_i'$) yields the score decomposition
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:app_score_decomp}
s_{v,H}(\theta_0)
= q_v A_{v,H}(\theta_0)^2\Big[\underbrace{2\,\mu_v'(\theta_0)\,\bar\xi_v}_{\text{linear score } s_{v,H}^{\mathrm{lin}}}
+ \underbrace{2\,\eta_v\eta_v' + 2\,\eta_v^2\,\bar{S}_{v,H}^{\mathrm{comp}}(\theta_0)}_{\text{quadratic cohort score } s_{v,H}^{\mathrm{quad}}}
+ s_{v,H}^{\mathrm{idio}}\Big],
\end{equation}
where $\bar\xi_v = \eta_v + \sum_i \tilde\omega_{v,i}\nu_i$ with $\tilde\omega_{v,i} = \omega_{v,i}(1-\omega_{v,i})/(1-\sum_k\omega_{v,k}^2)$, and $s_{v,H}^{\mathrm{idio}}$ collects mean-zero cross terms involving $\nu_i$ (uncorrelated across $v \neq w$).
Hence for $v \neq w$,
\[
\operatorname{Cov}\!\left(s_{v,H}^{\mathrm{lin}},\, s_{w,H}^{\mathrm{lin}} \,\middle|\, \mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{pub}}\right)
= 4\,q_v q_w A_{v,H}^2 A_{w,H}^2\,\mu_v'(\theta_0)\,\mu_w'(\theta_0)\,c_{v,w}(\theta_0),
\]
which enters $\Lambda_H^{\mathrm{cross}} = \lim_{V\to\infty} V^{-1}\sum_{v\neq w}\operatorname{Cov}(s_{v,H}, s_{w,H})$ directly.
Moreover, by standard second-order M-estimator expansion around $\theta_H^\dagger$ \citep{rilstone1996second,newey2004higher} (written for scalar $\theta$; the vector tensor analogue is immediate),
\[
\mathbb{E}[\hat\theta_H - \theta_H^\dagger]
= \frac{1}{V}\left[\mathcal{H}_H^{-2}\,C_{hs}(H) - \frac{1}{2}\mathcal{H}_H^{-3}\mathcal{D}_H\,\Lambda_H\right] + o(V^{-1}),
\]
where
\[
C_{hs}(H) := \lim_{V\to\infty}\frac{1}{V}\sum_{v,w}\operatorname{Cov}\!\bigl(h_{v,H}(\theta_H^\dagger),\, s_{w,H}(\theta_H^\dagger)\bigr),
\qquad
\mathcal{D}_H := \lim_{V\to\infty}\frac{1}{V}\sum_{v=1}^V \mathbb{E}\!\left[\nabla_{\theta\theta\theta}\mathcal{L}_{v,H}(\theta_H^\dagger)\right].
\]
Both $C_{hs}(H)$ and $\Lambda_H$ contain double sums over nearby vintages $v \neq w$, inflating the $O(1/V)$ finite-sample bias in proportion to $V / V_{\mathrm{eff}}$.
Finally, for a multiplicative shock $\eta_v(\theta) = W_v \tilde{g}_v(\theta)$ with $\nabla_\theta\log\tilde{g}_v(\theta_0) = -\bar{S}_v^{\mathrm{eff}}(\theta_0)$, the quadratic cohort score simplifies pointwise to
\[
s_{v,H}^{\mathrm{quad}}(\theta_0) = 2\,q_v A_{v,H}(\theta_0)^2\,\eta_v(\theta_0)^2\,\Big(\bar{S}_{v,H}^{\mathrm{comp}}(\theta_0) - \bar{S}_v^{\mathrm{eff}}(\theta_0)\Big).
\]
For Gaussian $(\eta_v,\eta_w)$, $\operatorname{Cov}(\eta_v^2,\eta_w^2) = 2\,c_{v,w}^2$, so both $\operatorname{Var}(s_{v,H}^{\mathrm{quad}})$ and $\operatorname{Cov}(s_{v,H}^{\mathrm{quad}}, s_{w,H}^{\mathrm{quad}})$ carry the factor $(\bar{S}_{v,H}^{\mathrm{comp}} - \bar{S}_v^{\mathrm{eff}})(\bar{S}_{w,H}^{\mathrm{comp}} - \bar{S}_w^{\mathrm{eff}})$ and collapse to zero near $H_0^\ast$.

\paragraph{Part (iii): Multi-parameter trade-off and MSE-optimal horizon.}
In dimension $p \ge 2$, $\nabla_\theta Q_H(\theta_0) = 0$ imposes $p$ nonlinear equations on one scalar $H \ge 0$, which admit no common solution in general unless the coordinate sensitivity vectors $S_{v,\tau}^{(1)}, \dots, S_{v,\tau}^{(p)}$ are collinear across $\tau$ (which holds approximately for $(\alpha,\beta)$ when excess returns have positive drift, but fails across non-market $q^5$ factors).
For the asymptotic MSE criterion $\mathrm{MSE}_V(H) = B_0(H)^2 + V^{-1}\mathcal{V}(H)$ with $B_0(H) := \theta_H^\dagger - \theta_0$:
when $\sigma_{\mathrm{eff},v}^2 \equiv 0$, $B_0(H) \equiv 0$ for all $H \ge 0$. Under homoscedastic independent vintages with $q_v = 1$, $s_{v,H}(\theta_0) = A_{v,H}^2 s_{v,0}(\theta_0)$ and $\mathcal{H}_H = \mathbb{E}[A_{v,H}^2]\,\mathcal{H}_0$, so Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
\frac{\mathcal{V}(H)}{\mathcal{V}(0)}
= \frac{\mathbb{E}[A_{v,H}^4]}{(\mathbb{E}[A_{v,H}^2])^2}
= 1 + \frac{\operatorname{Var}(A_{v,H}^2)}{(\mathbb{E}[A_{v,H}^2])^2} \ge 1,
\]
with strict inequality whenever $A_{v,H}$ varies across vintages ($H > 0$), proving $H_{\mathrm{MSE}}^\ast(V) = 0$.
When $\sigma_{\mathrm{eff},v}^2 > 0$ for scalar $\theta$ and $H_0^\ast > 0$ is a simple zero of $B_0(H)$ ($B_0(H_0^\ast) = 0$, $B_0'(H_0^\ast) \neq 0$), expand the first-order condition
\[
0 = \frac{d}{dH}\mathrm{MSE}_V(H)
= 2\,B_0(H)\,B_0'(H) + \frac{1}{V}\,\mathcal{V}'(H)
\]
around $H_0^\ast$ with $\delta_V := H_{\mathrm{MSE}}^\ast(V) - H_0^\ast = O(V^{-1})$:
\[
0 = 2\,[B_0'(H_0^\ast)]^2\,\delta_V + \frac{1}{V}\,\mathcal{V}'(H_0^\ast) + O(V^{-2})
\implies
H_{\mathrm{MSE}}^\ast(V) = H_0^\ast - \frac{\mathcal{V}'(H_0^\ast)}{2\,V\,[B_0'(H_0^\ast)]^2} + O(V^{-2}).
\]
When $\mathcal{V}'(H_0^\ast) > 0$, $H_{\mathrm{MSE}}^\ast(V) < H_0^\ast$ strictly at finite $V$, and decomposing $\mathcal{V}' = (\mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{indep}})' + (\mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{cross}})'$ shows that positive cross-vintage dependence pulls $H_{\mathrm{MSE}}^\ast(V)$ further inward whenever $(\mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{cross}})'(H_0^\ast) > 0$. As $V \to \infty$, $\delta_V = O(V^{-1}) \to 0$ so $H_{\mathrm{MSE}}^\ast(V) \to H_0^\ast$.

\paragraph{Part (iv): Rank-1 collapse and finite-$V$ indefiniteness of $\widetilde{U}_{H,m}^{\mathrm{cross}}$.}
Write the leave-band-out cross-vintage criterion in matrix form as
\[
\widetilde{U}_{H,m}^{\mathrm{cross}}(\theta)
= |\mathcal{P}_m|^{-1}\,\boldsymbol{\bar\epsilon}_H(\theta)^\top M_m \boldsymbol{\bar\epsilon}_H(\theta),
\]
where $\boldsymbol{\bar\epsilon}_H(\theta) = (\bar\epsilon_{1,H}(\theta),\dots,\bar\epsilon_{V,H}(\theta))^\top$, $(M_m)_{v,w} = \mathbf{1}_{\{|y_v - y_w| > m\}}$, and $|\mathcal{P}_m| = \sum_{v,w}(M_m)_{v,w} = V^2 - O(mV)$.
Notice the algebraic identity
\[
\widetilde{U}_{H,m}^{\mathrm{cross}}(\theta)
= \frac{V^2}{|\mathcal{P}_m|}\left(\frac{1}{V}\sum_{v=1}^V \bar\epsilon_{v,H}(\theta)\right)^2
- \frac{1}{|\mathcal{P}_m|}\sum_{|y_v-y_w|\le m}\bar\epsilon_{v,H}(\theta)\bar\epsilon_{w,H}(\theta).
\]
For fixed band $m$ as $V \to \infty$, the second term has $O(mV)$ summands divided by $|\mathcal{P}_m| = O(V^2)$, so it is $O_p(m/V) = o_p(1)$ uniformly on $\Theta$, while $V^2/|\mathcal{P}_m| \to 1$.
By the ULLN for the vintage mean $\bar{g}_H(\theta) := V^{-1}\sum_{v=1}^V \bar\epsilon_{v,H}(\theta) \xrightarrow{p} g_H(\theta) := \lim_{V\to\infty} V^{-1}\sum_v \mathbb{E}[\bar\epsilon_{v,H}(\theta)]$,
\[
\sup_{\theta \in \Theta}\left|\widetilde{U}_{H,m}^{\mathrm{cross}}(\theta) - g_H(\theta)^2\right| \xrightarrow{p} 0.
\]
Hence the population limit is $g_H(\theta)^2$; because $g_H(\theta_0) = 0$, its Hessian at $\theta_0$ equals
\[
\nabla_{\theta\theta}\!\left[g_H(\theta)^2\right]\Big|_{\theta=\theta_0}
= 2\,\nabla_\theta g_H(\theta_0)\,\nabla_\theta g_H(\theta_0)^\top,
\]
a matrix of rank at most~$1$.
Consequently, $\widetilde{U}_{H,m}^{\mathrm{cross}}$ cannot locally identify any parameter vector of dimension $p \ge 2$, and for scalar $\beta$ it vanishes at every root of $g_H(\beta) = 0$ (including the growth-optimal multiple root $\beta^\ast$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:multiple_roots}).
Finally, for finite $V$ and any $m \ge 0$, every diagonal entry of $M_m$ is zero, so $\operatorname{tr}(M_m) = \sum_{k=1}^V \lambda_k(M_m) = 0$.
Because $M_m \neq 0$ is real symmetric and has a positive top eigenvalue $\lambda_{\max}(M_m) \ge \mathbf{1}^\top M_m \mathbf{1} / V = |\mathcal{P}_m|/V > 0$, $M_m$ necessarily possesses strictly negative eigenvalues.
Explicitly, for $V=25$ annual vintages: at $m=0$, $\lambda_{\min}=-1.00$ (24 negative eigenvalues) vs.\ $\lambda_{\max}=24.00$; at $m=5$, $\lambda_{\min}=-8.50$ (12 negative) vs.\ $\lambda_{\max}=15.36$; at $m=7$, $\lambda_{\min}=-9.51$ (10 negative) vs.\ $\lambda_{\max}=12.62$; and at $m=10$, $\lambda_{\min}=-9.09$ (11 negative) vs.\ $\lambda_{\max}=9.39$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Nonlinear finite-$V$ Jensen offset under strong cross-vintage persistence]
\label{rem:jensen_finite_V}
When $V$ is small (e.g., $V=25$) and cross-vintage correlation is strong ($\rho_W \to 1$ or multi-year calendar overlap), the sample cross-vintage dispersion of $W_v$ falls below $\sigma_W^2$ while the common sample level $\bar{W} = V^{-1}\sum_v W_v$ enters nonlinearly through $\hat\alpha \approx T^{-1}\log(1 + \bar{W})$. For a mean-one lognormal shock $1+\bar{W} = \exp(Z - \frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Var}(Z))$, $\mathbb{E}[\log(1+\bar{W})] = -\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Var}(Z) < 0$, which injects a negative $O(1/V_{\mathrm{eff}})$ bias at finite $V$ that partially offsets the positive $O(1)$ inception variance tilt while inflating estimation variance dramatically. Table~\ref{tab:mc_regimes_verification} verifies both this finite-$V$ effect ($V=25$) and its disappearance at $V=100$.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Primitive DGP assumptions and inheritance of mixing and uniform moments}
\label{sec:primitive_assumptions}

Fix a compounding horizon $H \ge 0$ and let $Q_{V,H}(\theta) := V^{-1}\sum_{v=1}^V \ell_{v,H}(\theta)$ denote the sample criterion over $V$ observed vintages ordered by strictly increasing calendar years $y_1 < y_2 < \dots < y_V$ on the one-dimensional integer lattice $\mathbb{Z}$.
Here $\ell_{v,H}(\theta) = q_v \bar\epsilon_{v,H}(\theta)^2$ for squared-loss L2 on vintage portfolios ($q_v = 1$ in our baseline) and $\ell_{v,H}(\theta) = q_v U_{v,H}(\theta)$ for the cross-fund pair-product estimator over eligible vintages ($n_v \ge 2$; with outer normalization $\sum_v q_v / V$ absorbed into the constant scaling without affecting minimizers or studentized test statistics).
Write $Q_H(\theta) := \lim_{V\to\infty} V^{-1}\sum_{v=1}^V \mathbb{E}[\ell_{v,H}(\theta)]$ for the limiting population objective and $\theta_H^\dagger := \arg\min_{\theta\in\Theta} Q_H(\theta)$ for the population criterion target (equal to $\theta_0$ for $U_{v,H}$ under Theorem~\ref{thm:finite_pool}(T7)).
Unlike spatial random fields on $\mathbb{Z}^d$ ($d \ge 2$), vintage overlap in pooled cash-flow panels occurs strictly along one-dimensional calendar time $y_v \in \mathbb{Z}$, with occasional empty early vintage years handled by the calendar-distance Bartlett kernel $K(|y_v - y_w|/S)$ \citep{C99,KS11}.
We replace high-level criterion assumptions with primitive conditions directly on the underlying factor, shock, and cash-flow processes.

\begin{assume}[Primitive increasing-domain conditions A1--A5]
\label{ass:asymptotics_H}
Maintain the following five conditions for a fixed horizon $H \ge 0$ and some $\delta > 0$ (set $r := 2 + \delta$):
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[\textbf{A1}] \emph{(Compact positive-return domain and global identification.)} $\Theta \subset \mathbb{R}^p$ is compact and convex, and the monthly risk-free rate and factors have bounded support, $|r_{f,u}| + \|F_u\| \le \bar{F} < \infty$ almost surely, with $\Theta$ and $\bar{F}$ such that the monthly gross benchmark return $G_u(\theta) = 1 + r_{f,u} + \alpha + \beta^\top F_u$ satisfies $\inf_{u \ge 1,\,\theta \in \Theta} G_u(\theta) \ge \underline{G} > 0$ almost surely. Bounded support is what makes a fixed $\Theta$ possible: with unbounded factor support (e.g., Gaussian factors), $\inf_u G_u(\theta)$ is not bounded away from zero for any nonzero loading. In implementation, $\underline{G} = 0.08$, $\beta_{\mathrm{MKT}} \in [-1,4]$, $\alpha \in [-2\%,2\%]$/month, and the floor is imposed on the realized sample path (Equation~\eqref{eq:admissible_domain}); under bounded support this realized-path domain contains $\Theta$ and shrinks to it as the realized factors approach the edge of their support, which is immaterial for an interior target (A5). Monte Carlo designs with Gaussian factor draws impose the floor on the realized path in the same way, so the theory covers them only conditionally on that domain. The limiting objective $Q_H(\theta) = \lim_{V\to\infty} V^{-1}\sum_{v=1}^V \mathbb{E}[\ell_{v,H}(\theta)]$ exists on $\Theta$ and attains a unique global minimum at $\theta_H^\dagger \in \Theta$.

  \item[\textbf{A2}] \emph{(Bounded lifetimes, finite pools, and non-degenerate weights.)} Each fund $i \in \mathcal{I}_v$ has active cash flows confined to a bounded lifetime $t - t_v^0 \in \{0, 1, \dots, L_{\max}\}$ ($L_{\max} < \infty$ months). Per-vintage fund counts satisfy $1 \le n_v \le \bar{n} < \infty$ (with $2 \le n_v \le \bar{n}$ for eligible $U_{v,H}$ vintages), vintage weights satisfy $0 < \underline{q} \le q_v \le \bar{q} < \infty$, and normalized fund weights $\omega_{v,i} > 0$ ($\sum_{i \in \mathcal{I}_v}\omega_{v,i}=1$) satisfy $\inf_v \bigl(1 - \sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_v}\omega_{v,i}^2\bigr) \ge \underline{d} > 0$ almost surely. Calendar vintage years satisfy $y_1 < \dots < y_V$ in $\mathbb{Z}$ with bounded spacing $\sup_v (y_{v+1} - y_v) \le \bar{\Delta} < \infty$.

  \item[\textbf{A3}] \emph{(One-dimensional calendar-time weak dependence.)} Let $X_u := (r_{f,u}, F_u^\top, e_u^\top)^\top$ collect the monthly public factors and any latent calendar-time private-market shocks, and let $\eta_v$ collect vintage-level shocks anchored at $y_v$. The annual calendar-time vector process $Z_y := \bigl((X_{12(y-1)+m})_{m=1}^{12},\, \eta_y,\, \mathcal{W}_y\bigr)_{y \in \mathbb{Z}}$ is $\alpha$-mixing with mixing coefficients $\alpha_Z(k)$ of size $-\lambda$ with $\lambda > r/(r-2) = (2+\delta)/\delta$ (i.e., $\alpha_Z(k) = O(k^{-\lambda})$ so $\sum_{k=1}^\infty \alpha_Z(k)^{\delta/(2+\delta)} < \infty$; in particular, any finite-memory or geometrically mixing process satisfies A3). Fund-idiosyncratic shocks $\nu_{v,i}$ are generated as measurable functions of vintage $v$'s active calendar block $\{Z_y : y_v \le y \le y_v + \lceil L_{\max}/12 \rceil\}$ and mutually independent innovations $U_{v,i}$ that are independent of $\{Z_y\}_{y \in \mathbb{Z}}$.

  \item[\textbf{A4}] \emph{(Primitive moment envelopes on compounded returns, factors, and cash flows.)} Put $L_H := \max(L_{\max}, 12H)$ and define the single-vintage uniform envelopes
  \begin{align*}
  M_{v,H}^{(R)} &:= \sup_{\theta \in \Theta} \max_{0 \le k \le L_H} \Bigl(R^b_{t_v^0,\, t_v^0+k}(\theta) + D_{v,\,t_v^0+k}(\theta)\Bigr), \\
  M_v^{(F)} &:= \max_{1 \le k \le L_H}\bigl(1 + \|F_{t_v^0+k}\|\bigr),
  \qquad
  M_v^{(CF)} := \max_{i \in \mathcal{I}_v} \sum_{k=0}^{L_{\max}} |CF_{v,i,t_v^0+k}|.
  \end{align*}
  There exists a finite constant $C < \infty$ such that, with $r = 2 + \delta$,
  \[
  \sup_{v \ge 1} \mathbb{E}\!\left[\bigl(M_{v,H}^{(R)}\bigr)^{8r}\right] \le C,
  \qquad
  \sup_{v \ge 1} \mathbb{E}\!\left[\bigl(M_v^{(F)}\bigr)^{8r}\right] \le C,
  \qquad
  \sup_{v \ge 1} \mathbb{E}\!\left[\bigl(M_v^{(CF)}\bigr)^{8r}\right] \le C.
  \]

  \item[\textbf{A5}] \emph{(Interior optimum, nonsingular curvature, and partial-sum homogeneity.)} $\theta_H^\dagger \in \mathrm{int}(\Theta)$. Both the limiting Hessian $\mathcal{H}_H := \lim_{V\to\infty} V^{-1}\sum_{v=1}^V \mathbb{E}[\nabla_{\theta\theta}^2 \ell_{v,H}(\theta_H^\dagger)]$ and the long-run score covariance $\Lambda_H := \lim_{V\to\infty}\operatorname{Var}\bigl(V^{-1/2}\sum_{v=1}^V s_{v,H}(\theta_H^\dagger)\bigr)$ are symmetric positive definite, where $s_{v,H}(\theta) := \nabla_\theta \ell_{v,H}(\theta)$. For the fixed-$b$ FCLT (Corollary~\ref{cor:fixed_b_ar}), assume further that for every $\tau \in [0,1]$, $V^{-1}\sum_{v=1}^{\lfloor \tau V \rfloor} \mathbb{E}[\nabla_{\theta\theta}^2 \ell_{v,H}(\theta_H^\dagger)] \to \tau\,\mathcal{H}_H$ and $V^{-1}\operatorname{Var}\bigl(\sum_{v=1}^{\lfloor \tau V \rfloor} s_{v,H}(\theta_H^\dagger)\bigr) \to \tau\,\Lambda_H$, and that empty calendar years are sparse in the sense that $(y_V - y_1 + 1 - V)/V \to 0$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{assume}

Assumption~A4 transparently links our asymptotic conditions to the finite-sample heavy-tail behavior documented in Subsection~\ref{sec:v400_studentization_app}: because $\ell_{v,H}(\theta) \propto A_{v,H}(\theta)^2 PV_i(\theta) PV_j(\theta)$ is a fourth-order nonlinear product of compounded benchmark returns and cash flows, controlling $(2+\delta)$ moments of the score and Hessian via H\"older's inequality requires high finite moments of single-vintage compounded gross returns and payoffs. Under A1's bounded factor support and gross-return floor, the envelopes $M_{v,H}^{(R)} \le (1 + \bar{F} + \sup_{\Theta}(|\alpha| + \|\beta\|\bar{F}))^{L_H} + \underline{G}^{-L_H}$ and $M_v^{(F)} \le 1 + \bar{F}$ are bounded by deterministic constants, so A4 reduces to the cash-flow moment, which holds for bounded or lognormal deal multiples over bounded lifetimes. These bounds are finite but grow geometrically in $L_H$, so finite-sample score kurtosis can remain large when $L_H$ and deal or market volatility are substantial.
The homogeneity part of A5 is restrictive for private-equity panels. Pool sizes rise from $n_v = 2$ in the 1980s to $41$ (Buyout) and $56$ (VC) in the 2000s, and the idiosyncratic part of the vintage score variance falls with $n_v$, so $V^{-1}\operatorname{Var}\bigl(\sum_{v=1}^{\lfloor \tau V \rfloor} s_{v,H}(\theta_H^\dagger)\bigr)$ need not be linear in $\tau$. Without linearity the centered partial-sum process converges to a Gaussian process that is not a scaled Brownian bridge, the fixed-$b$ limit of the $t$-statistic depends on the variance profile, and the \citet{KV05} critical values are no longer exact. We use them as an approximation and check their size by design: the core Monte Carlo designs reproduce the observed vintage calendars and fund counts, and there $U_{v,0}$ rejects a true $\beta$ in $4.6\%$ (Buyout) and $6.4\%$ (VC) of draws (main-text Table~\ref{tab:mc_core}).
The next lemma verifies explicitly that the nonlinear criterion, score, and Hessian inherit 1D $\alpha$-mixing, uniform moment envelopes, and stochastic equicontinuity directly from Assumptions~A1--A4.

\begin{lemma}[Inheritance of mixing, Lipschitz moment envelopes, and uniform convergence]
\label{lem:mixing_moment_inheritance}
Suppose Assumptions~\ref{ass:asymptotics_H}(A1)--(A4) hold for a fixed horizon $H \ge 0$. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[\textbf{(a)}] \emph{(Finite-block mixing inheritance.)} Put $m_H := \lceil L_H/12 \rceil + 1$. For every fixed $\theta \in \Theta$, the vintage-indexed sequence $\bigl(\ell_{v,H}(\theta),\, s_{v,H}(\theta),\, \nabla_{\theta\theta}^2 \ell_{v,H}(\theta)\bigr)_{v \ge 1}$ is $\alpha$-mixing with mixing coefficients $\tilde\alpha(k) \le \alpha_Z(\max(k - m_H, 0))$, preserving the mixing size $-\lambda < -(2+\delta)/\delta$ of Assumption~A3 (and reducing to exact $m_H$-dependence when $Z_y$ is independent across calendar years).
  \item[\textbf{(b)}] \emph{(Explicit uniform moment and Lipschitz envelopes.)} The criterion $\ell_{v,H}(\theta)$ is infinitely differentiable on $\Theta$, and there exist finite constants $C_{m,H}$ ($m = 0,1,2,3$) depending only on $(L_H, \underline{G}, \bar{q}, \underline{d}, p)$ such that
  \begin{equation}
  \label{eq:leibniz_envelope}
  \sup_{\theta \in \Theta} \bigl\|\nabla_\theta^{(m)} \ell_{v,H}(\theta)\bigr\|
  \;\le\; C_{m,H}\,\bigl(M_{v,H}^{(R)}\bigr)^4\,\bigl(M_v^{(F)}\bigr)^m\,\bigl(M_v^{(CF)}\bigr)^2
  =: B_{v,m}, \qquad m \in \{0,1,2,3\},
  \end{equation}
  with $\sup_v \mathbb{E}[B_{v,m}^{2+\delta}] < \infty$ for $m \in \{0,1,2\}$ and $\sup_v \mathbb{E}[B_{v,3}^{1+\delta/2}] < \infty$. In particular, for all $\theta, \theta' \in \Theta$, $|\ell_{v,H}(\theta)-\ell_{v,H}(\theta')| \le B_{v,1}\|\theta-\theta'\|$ and $\|\nabla_{\theta\theta}^2\ell_{v,H}(\theta) - \nabla_{\theta\theta}^2\ell_{v,H}(\theta')\| \le B_{v,3}\|\theta-\theta'\|$.
  \item[\textbf{(c)}] \emph{(Uniform laws of large numbers.)} As $V \to \infty$,
  \[
  \sup_{\theta \in \Theta}\bigl|Q_{V,H}(\theta) - Q_H(\theta)\bigr| \xrightarrow{p} 0
  \qquad\text{and}\qquad
  \sup_{\theta \in \Theta}\bigl\|\nabla_{\theta\theta}^2 Q_{V,H}(\theta) - \nabla_{\theta\theta}^2 Q_H(\theta)\bigr\| \xrightarrow{p} 0,
  \]
  and both $Q_H(\theta)$ and $\nabla_{\theta\theta}^2 Q_H(\theta)$ are continuous on $\Theta$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Part (a).} By Assumption~A2, every cash flow of vintage $v$ occurs at $t \in [t_v^0, t_v^0 + L_{\max}]$ and the horizon average $\mathcal{T}_{v,H}$ lies in $[t_v^0, t_v^0 + 12H]$, so $\bigl(\ell_{v,H}(\theta), s_{v,H}(\theta), \nabla_{\theta\theta}^2 \ell_{v,H}(\theta)\bigr)$ is a measurable function solely of the finite calendar block $\bigl\{Z_y : y_v \le y \le y_v + m_H - 1\bigr\}$ together with vintage $v$'s conditionally independent fund shocks $\{\nu_{v,i}\}_{i\in\mathcal{I}_v}$. Because vintage years are strictly increasing integers ($y_{v+k} - y_v \ge k$), for any $v \ge 1$ and $k \ge 1$ the $\sigma$-fields generated by vintages $\{1,\dots,v\}$ and $\{v+k, v+k+1, \dots\}$ are contained in the $\sigma$-fields of $\{Z_y : y \le y_v + m_H - 1\}$ and $\{Z_y : y \ge y_{v+k}\}$ (augmented by independent idiosyncratic draws $\{\nu_{w,i}\}$, which cannot increase the $\alpha$-mixing coefficient). Since $y_{v+k} - (y_v + m_H - 1) \ge k - m_H + 1$, the resulting mixing coefficient satisfies $\tilde\alpha(k) \le \alpha_Z(\max(k - m_H, 0))$ for all $k \ge 1$, preserving $\sum_{k=1}^\infty \tilde\alpha(k)^{\delta/(2+\delta)} < \infty$.

\emph{Part (b).} Write $\tilde{F}_u := (1, F_u^\top)^\top$ (or $F_u$ when $\alpha$ is omitted), so $\|\tilde{F}_u\| \le M_v^{(F)}$ for all $u \in [t_v^0+1, t_v^0+L_H]$. Since $G_u(\theta) \ge \underline{G} > 0$ on $\Theta$ (A1), $D_{v,t}(\theta) = \prod_{u=t_v^0+1}^t G_u(\theta)^{-1}$ and $R^b_{t_v^0,\tau}(\theta) = \prod_{u=t_v^0+1}^\tau G_u(\theta)$ are smooth on $\Theta$. Direct differentiation gives $\nabla_\theta D_{v,t} = -D_{v,t} S_{v,t}$ and $\nabla_\theta R^b_{t_v^0,\tau} = R^b_{t_v^0,\tau} S_{v,\tau}$, where $S_{v,t}(\theta) = \sum_{u=t_v^0+1}^t G_u(\theta)^{-1}\tilde{F}_u$ satisfies $\|S_{v,t}(\theta)\| \le (L_H/\underline{G}) M_v^{(F)}$ uniformly on $\Theta$. Iterating the product rule up to order $m \in \{0,1,2,3\}$ shows that every $m$-th derivative of $D_{v,t}(\theta)$ and $R^b_{t_v^0,\tau}(\theta)$ is bounded in norm by $c_{m,H} M_{v,H}^{(R)} (M_v^{(F)})^m$ for constants $c_{m,H}$ depending only on $(L_H, \underline{G}, p)$. Summing over $k \in \{0,\dots,L_{\max}\}$ yields $\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\|\nabla_\theta^{(m)} PV_{v,i}(\theta)\| \le c_{m,H} M_{v,H}^{(R)} (M_v^{(F)})^m M_v^{(CF)}$ for every $i \in \mathcal{I}_v$, and averaging over $\tau \in \mathcal{T}_{v,H}$ yields $\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\|\nabla_\theta^{(m)} A_{v,H}(\theta)\| \le c_{m,H} M_{v,H}^{(R)} (M_v^{(F)})^m$. Writing $\ell_{v,H}(\theta) = q_v A_{v,H}(\theta)^2 \Psi_v^{\mathrm{pair}}(\theta)$, where $\Psi_v^{\mathrm{pair}}(\theta)$ is a bilinear combination of $PV_{v,i}(\theta) PV_{v,j}(\theta)$ with absolute coefficient sum bounded by $\underline{d}^{-1}$ (A2), applying the Leibniz rule to the four-factor product $A_{v,H} \cdot A_{v,H} \cdot PV_{v,i} \cdot PV_{v,j}$ immediately gives \eqref{eq:leibniz_envelope}.
For the moment bounds with $r = 2 + \delta$, applying the three-factor generalized H\"older inequality with exponents $(2, 4, 4)$ ($1/2 + 1/4 + 1/4 = 1$) and Lyapunov's inequality ($4mr \le 8r$ for $m \in \{0,1,2\}$) gives
\[
\mathbb{E}\!\left[B_{v,m}^r\right]
\le C_{m,H}^r
\left(\mathbb{E}\!\left[\bigl(M_{v,H}^{(R)}\bigr)^{8r}\right]\right)^{1/2}
\left(\mathbb{E}\!\left[\bigl(M_v^{(F)}\bigr)^{4mr}\right]\right)^{1/4}
\left(\mathbb{E}\!\left[\bigl(M_v^{(CF)}\bigr)^{8r}\right]\right)^{1/4}
\le C_{m,H}^r \max(1, C) < \infty.
\] For $m = 3$ with exponent $r/2 = 1 + \delta/2 > 1$, the same $(2,4,4)$ H\"older split yields
\[
\mathbb{E}\!\left[B_{v,3}^{r/2}\right]
\le C_{3,H}^{r/2}
\left(\mathbb{E}\!\left[\bigl(M_{v,H}^{(R)}\bigr)^{4r}\right]\right)^{1/2}
\left(\mathbb{E}\!\left[\bigl(M_v^{(F)}\bigr)^{6r}\right]\right)^{1/4}
\left(\mathbb{E}\!\left[\bigl(M_v^{(CF)}\bigr)^{4r}\right]\right)^{1/4}
< \infty,
\]
by Assumption~A4 and Lyapunov's inequality ($4r < 6r < 8r$). The Lipschitz bounds follow from the mean-value theorem on convex $\Theta$.

\emph{Part (c).} By Parts~(a)--(b), at every fixed $\theta \in \Theta$, $\ell_{v,H}(\theta)$ and $\nabla_{\theta\theta}^2 \ell_{v,H}(\theta)$ are $\alpha$-mixing with $\sum_k \tilde\alpha(k)^{\delta/(2+\delta)} < \infty$ and uniformly $L_{2+\delta}$-bounded, so the pointwise LLN for heterogeneous mixing sequences \citep[Theorem~20.21]{Davidson94} holds at every $\theta \in \Theta$. By Part~(b), both families satisfy the global Lipschitz condition with $L_{1+\delta/2}$-bounded (hence uniformly integrable) envelopes $B_{v,1}$ and $B_{v,3}$. On compact $\Theta$, \citet[Theorem~1 and Lemma~1]{Andrews92} therefore yield stochastic equicontinuity and both uniform laws of large numbers, along with continuity of $Q_H$ and $\nabla_{\theta\theta}^2 Q_H$ on $\Theta$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Proofs of Proposition~\ref{prop:consistency_clt} and Corollary~\ref{cor:fixed_b_ar} (consistency, CLT, fixed-$b$ inference, and AR inversion)}
\label{sec:proofs_prop3_cor1}

\begin{proposition}[Consistency and asymptotic normality around $\theta_H^\dagger$]
\label{prop:proof_cons_clt}
Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:asymptotics_H}(A1)--(A4), $\hat\theta_H \xrightarrow{p} \theta_H^\dagger$ as $V \to \infty$ (with $\theta_H^\dagger = \theta_0$ for $U_{v,H}$ under Theorem~\ref{thm:finite_pool}(T7)). If Assumption~\ref{ass:asymptotics_H}(A5) also holds with $\mathbb{E}[s_{v,H}(\theta_H^\dagger)] = 0$ for every $v$, then
\[
\sqrt{V}(\hat\theta_H - \theta_H^\dagger) \xrightarrow{d} \mathcal{N}(0,\Sigma_H),
\qquad
\Sigma_H = \mathcal{H}_H^{-1}\Lambda_H \mathcal{H}_H^{-1}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Consistency.} By Lemma~\ref{lem:mixing_moment_inheritance}(c), $\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}|Q_{V,H}(\theta) - Q_H(\theta)| \xrightarrow{p} 0$ with $Q_H$ continuous on compact $\Theta$. Since $\theta_H^\dagger$ is the unique global minimizer of $Q_H$ on $\Theta$ (A1), \citet[Theorem~2.1]{NM94} gives $\hat\theta_H \xrightarrow{p} \theta_H^\dagger$.

\emph{Asymptotic normality (score centering and exact integrated Hessian).} Because $\theta_H^\dagger \in \mathrm{int}(\Theta)$ (A5) and $\hat\theta_H \xrightarrow{p} \theta_H^\dagger$, the sample first-order condition $\nabla_\theta Q_{V,H}(\hat\theta_H) = 0$ holds with probability approaching one. Crucially, the limiting score vanishes at the criterion target $\theta_H^\dagger$---that is, $\lim_{V\to\infty} V^{-1}\sum_{v=1}^V \mathbb{E}[s_{v,H}(\theta_H^\dagger)] = \nabla_\theta Q_H(\theta_H^\dagger) = 0$ (justified by dominated convergence via the $L_{2+\delta}$-envelope $B_{v,1}$ in Lemma~\ref{lem:mixing_moment_inheritance}(b))---rather than at $\theta_0$ (for L2 with finite $n_v$, $\mathbb{E}[s_{v,H}(\theta_0)] \neq 0$ in general; Theorem~\ref{thm:finite_pool}, T3 and T6). Applying the fundamental theorem of calculus to each coordinate of $\nabla_\theta Q_{V,H}$ along the segment $\theta(u) = \theta_H^\dagger + u(\hat\theta_H - \theta_H^\dagger) \in \Theta$, $u \in [0,1]$, yields the exact integrated-Hessian expansion
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:integrated_hessian}
0 = \nabla_\theta Q_{V,H}(\hat\theta_H)
  = \nabla_\theta Q_{V,H}(\theta_H^\dagger)
  + \bar{\mathcal{H}}_{V,H}\,(\hat\theta_H - \theta_H^\dagger),
\qquad
\bar{\mathcal{H}}_{V,H} := \int_0^1 \nabla_{\theta\theta}^2 Q_{V,H}(\theta(u))\,du.
\end{equation}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:mixing_moment_inheritance}(c), $\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\|\nabla_{\theta\theta}^2 Q_{V,H}(\theta) - \nabla_{\theta\theta}^2 Q_H(\theta)\| \xrightarrow{p} 0$, and continuity of $\nabla_{\theta\theta}^2 Q_H$ at $\theta_H^\dagger$ together with $\hat\theta_H \xrightarrow{p} \theta_H^\dagger$ implies $\bar{\mathcal{H}}_{V,H} \xrightarrow{p} \mathcal{H}_H$, which is positive definite by A5.
By Lemma~\ref{lem:mixing_moment_inheritance}(a)--(b), the zero-mean vintage scores $\bigl(s_{v,H}(\theta_H^\dagger) - \mathbb{E}[s_{v,H}(\theta_H^\dagger)]\bigr)_{v=1}^V$ are 1D $\alpha$-mixing of size $-\lambda < -(2+\delta)/\delta$ with $\sup_v \mathbb{E}[\|s_{v,H}(\theta_H^\dagger)\|^{2+\delta}] < \infty$. Under the per-vintage centering of the statement ($\mathbb{E}[s_{v,H}(\theta_H^\dagger)] = 0$ for every $v$; Remark~\ref{rem:per_vintage_centering} discusses when it holds) and A5 ($\Lambda_H \succ 0$), the 1D $\alpha$-mixing CLT \citep[Corollary~3.1]{WW88} (see also \citealp[Theorem~24.6]{Davidson94}) yields
\[
\sqrt{V}\nabla_\theta Q_{V,H}(\theta_H^\dagger) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{V}}\sum_{v=1}^V s_{v,H}(\theta_H^\dagger) \xrightarrow{d} \mathcal{N}(0,\Lambda_H).
\]
Slutsky's theorem and \eqref{eq:integrated_hessian} give $\sqrt{V}(\hat\theta_H - \theta_H^\dagger) = -\bar{\mathcal{H}}_{V,H}^{-1}\sqrt{V}\nabla_\theta Q_{V,H}(\theta_H^\dagger) \xrightarrow{d} \mathcal{N}(0,\mathcal{H}_H^{-1}\Lambda_H\mathcal{H}_H^{-1})$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[When per-vintage score centering holds]
\label{rem:per_vintage_centering}
The definition of $\theta_H^\dagger$ makes only the cross-vintage average $V^{-1}\sum_{v}\mathbb{E}[s_{v,H}(\theta_H^\dagger)]$ vanish in the limit, whereas Proposition~\ref{prop:proof_cons_clt} and Corollary~\ref{cor:fixed_b_ar}(i) need each vintage's mean score to vanish.
(a)~For $U_{v,H}$ under the conditions of Theorem~\ref{thm:finite_pool}(T7), $\mathbb{E}[U_{v,H}(\theta)\mid\mathcal{G}_v]$ is a weighted sum of products $A_{v,H}(\theta)^2\mu_{v,i}(\theta)\mu_{v,j}(\theta)$ with $\mu_{v,i}(\theta_0) = 0$, so its gradient vanishes at $\theta_0$ in every vintage, whatever the pool sizes $n_v$; the condition holds at $\theta_H^\dagger = \theta_0$.
(b)~For L2 VYP, each vintage's expected criterion contains the variance tilt of Theorem~\ref{thm:finite_pool}(T3--T6), whose gradient at $\theta_0$ scales with $\sum_{i}\omega_{v,i}^2$ ($n_v^{-1}$ under equal weights). The vintage-level minimizers then coincide only when these terms do, for example when pool sizes and payoff variances are the same in every vintage. With $n_v$ rising from 2 to 41 (Buyout) and 56 (VC) in our panels, the mean scores $\mathbb{E}[s_{v,H}(\theta_H^\dagger)]$ of L2 are nonzero and only average to zero, Corollary~\ref{cor:fixed_b_ar}(i) does not cover L2, and its Bartlett estimator also absorbs the cross-vintage spread of these means. The empirical tables therefore report L2 standard errors for comparison but attach fixed-$b$ significance markers only to $U_{v,H}$.
\end{remark}

We now formalize fixed-$b$ asymptotic inference for both the studentized M-estimator Wald/$t$-statistics and the identification-robust Anderson--Rubin moment test on the 1D vintage calendar.

\begin{proposition}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:fixed_b_ar}: 1D fixed-$b$ Bartlett asymptotics and AR inversion]
\label{prop:proof_fixed_b_ar}
Fix a bandwidth ratio $b \in (0, 1]$ and set $S = \lfloor b V \rfloor$. Let $W_k(\cdot)$ denote a standard $k$-dimensional Brownian motion on $[0,1]$, $B_k(r) := W_k(r) - r W_k(1)$ the corresponding Brownian bridge, and
\[
\Xi_k(b) := \frac{2}{b}\int_0^1 B_k(r) B_k(r)^\top\,dr - \frac{1}{b}\int_0^{1-b}\Bigl(B_k(r) B_k(r+b)^\top + B_k(r+b) B_k(r)^\top\Bigr)\,dr.
\]
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[\textbf{(i)}] \emph{(Fixed-$b$ M-estimator Wald and $t$-statistics.)} Suppose Assumptions~\ref{ass:asymptotics_H}(A1)--(A5) hold with $\mathbb{E}[s_{v,H}(\theta_H^\dagger)] = 0$ for every $v$ and let $\hat\Lambda_H(b) := V^{-1}\sum_{v,w=1}^V K(|y_v - y_w|/S)\,\tilde{s}_{v,H}\tilde{s}_{w,H}^\top$ be the centered Bartlett estimator of \eqref{eq:shac_H}. Then as $V \to \infty$,
  \[
  \hat\Lambda_H(b) \;\Rightarrow\; \Lambda_H^{1/2}\,\Xi_p(b)\,\Lambda_H^{1/2}.
  \]
  Consequently, for any full-row-rank $q \times p$ contrast matrix $R$ testing $H_0: R\theta_H^\dagger = r_0$,
  \[
  W_V(R) := V\bigl(R\hat\theta_H - r_0\bigr)^\top \Bigl(R\hat{\mathcal{H}}_H^{-1}\hat\Lambda_H(b)\hat{\mathcal{H}}_H^{-1} R^\top\Bigr)^{-1}\bigl(R\hat\theta_H - r_0\bigr)
  \;\Rightarrow\; W_q(1)^\top \Xi_q(b)^{-1} W_q(1),
  \]
  and for $q = 1$ ($R = a^\top \neq 0$), the signed $t$-statistic converges to $t_{\mathrm{KV}}(b) := W_1(1)/\sqrt{\Xi_1(b)}$.

  \item[\textbf{(ii)}] \emph{(Identification-robust fixed-$b$ AR moment test.)} Suppose Condition~(P) holds at $\theta_0$ for $H = 0$ ($z_{v,0} \equiv 1$), or Condition~(X) holds for fixed $H > 0$ ($z_{v,H} = A_{v,H}(1)/A_{v,0}(1)$). Let $g_{v,H}(\theta_0) := z_{v,H} PV_v(\theta_0)$, and assume Assumptions~A2--A3, $\sup_v \mathbb{E}[|g_{v,H}(\theta_0)|^{2+\delta}] < \infty$, sparse calendar gaps $(y_V - y_1 + 1 - V)/V \to 0$, and $V^{-1}\operatorname{Var}\!\bigl(\sum_{v=1}^{\lfloor \tau V \rfloor} g_{v,H}(\theta_0)\bigr) \to \tau\,\Omega_g$ with $\Omega_g > 0$. Then, \emph{without} requiring unique roots, interior optimization, or a nonzero moment Jacobian $\mathbb{E}[\nabla_\theta g_{v,H}(\theta_0)]$, the centered Bartlett AR statistic of \eqref{eq:ar_set} satisfies
  \[
  t_V^{\mathrm{AR}}(\theta_0) := \frac{\sqrt{V}\,\bar{g}_H(\theta_0)}{\sqrt{\hat\Omega_g(\theta_0; b)}} \;\Rightarrow\; t_{\mathrm{KV}}(b).
  \]
  Hence the inverted set $\mathcal{C}_{1-\alpha}^{\mathrm{AR}} := \{\theta \in \Theta : |t_V^{\mathrm{AR}}(\theta)| \le c_\alpha^{\mathrm{fb}}(b)\}$ satisfies $\lim_{V\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(\theta_0 \in \mathcal{C}_{1-\alpha}^{\mathrm{AR}}) = 1 - \alpha$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Part (i).} By Lemma~\ref{lem:mixing_moment_inheritance}(a)--(b) and Assumption~A5, the vintage score sequence $\{s_{v,H}(\theta_H^\dagger)\}_{v=1}^V$ is 1D $\alpha$-mixing of size $-\lambda < -(2+\delta)/\delta$, uniformly $L_{2+\delta}$-bounded, and satisfies homogeneous partial-sum variance convergence $V^{-1}\operatorname{Var}\bigl(\sum_{v=1}^{\lfloor \tau V \rfloor} s_{v,H}(\theta_H^\dagger)\bigr) \to \tau \Lambda_H$. By the multivariate invariance principle for heterogeneous $\alpha$-mixing processes \citep[Theorem~2.11]{WW88} (see also \citealp[Theorem~29.6]{Davidson94}), the partial-sum process satisfies
\[
\mathcal{S}_V(\tau) := \frac{1}{\sqrt{V}}\sum_{v=1}^{\lfloor \tau V \rfloor} s_{v,H}(\theta_H^\dagger) \;\Rightarrow\; \Lambda_H^{1/2} W_p(\tau) \qquad \text{in } D[0,1]^p.
\]
Next, consider the estimated partial-sum process $\hat{\mathcal{S}}_V(\tau) := V^{-1/2}\sum_{v=1}^{\lfloor \tau V \rfloor} \tilde{s}_{v,H}$, where $\tilde{s}_{v,H} = s_{v,H}(\hat\theta_H)$ on the event (having probability approaching one) that $\hat\theta_H \in \mathrm{int}(\Theta)$ solves $\sum_{v=1}^V s_{v,H}(\hat\theta_H) = 0$. Applying the fundamental theorem of calculus to each vintage score along $\theta(u) = \theta_H^\dagger + u(\hat\theta_H - \theta_H^\dagger)$ gives
\[
\hat{\mathcal{S}}_V(\tau)
= \mathcal{S}_V(\tau) + \left(\frac{1}{V}\sum_{v=1}^{\lfloor \tau V \rfloor} \bar{h}_{v,H}\right)\sqrt{V}(\hat\theta_H - \theta_H^\dagger),
\qquad
\bar{h}_{v,H} := \int_0^1 \nabla_{\theta\theta}^2 \ell_{v,H}(\theta(u))\,du.
\]
Using the Lipschitz envelope $B_{v,3}$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:mixing_moment_inheritance}(b), $\sup_{\tau\in[0,1]}\bigl\|V^{-1}\sum_{v=1}^{\lfloor \tau V \rfloor} (\bar{h}_{v,H} - \nabla_{\theta\theta}^2 \ell_{v,H}(\theta_H^\dagger))\bigr\| \le \bigl(V^{-1}\sum_{v=1}^V B_{v,3}\bigr)\|\hat\theta_H - \theta_H^\dagger\| = O_p(V^{-1/2})$, while the uniform LLN on partial sums and Assumption~A5 yield $\sup_{\tau\in[0,1]}\bigl\|V^{-1}\sum_{v=1}^{\lfloor \tau V \rfloor} \nabla_{\theta\theta}^2 \ell_{v,H}(\theta_H^\dagger) - \tau \mathcal{H}_H\bigr\| \xrightarrow{p} 0$. Substituting $\sqrt{V}(\hat\theta_H - \theta_H^\dagger) = -\mathcal{H}_H^{-1}\mathcal{S}_V(1) + o_p(1)$ yields uniformly over $\tau \in [0,1]$,
\[
\hat{\mathcal{S}}_V(\tau)
= \mathcal{S}_V(\tau) - \tau\,\mathcal{S}_V(1) + o_p(1)
\;\Rightarrow\; \Lambda_H^{1/2}\bigl(W_p(\tau) - \tau W_p(1)\bigr) = \Lambda_H^{1/2} B_p(\tau),
\]
jointly with $\mathcal{S}_V(1) \Rightarrow \Lambda_H^{1/2} W_p(1)$.

Now compare the calendar-lag Bartlett estimator $\hat\Lambda_H(b)$ with the regular index-lag estimator $\hat\Lambda_H^{\mathrm{idx}}(b) := V^{-1}\sum_{v,w=1}^V K(|v-w|/S)\,\tilde{s}_{v,H}\tilde{s}_{w,H}^\top$.
Embed the $V$ observed vintages onto the regular annual grid $\{y_1, y_1+1, \dots, y_V\}$ of length $V_{\mathrm{cal}} := y_V - y_1 + 1$ by placing zero scores at the $G_V := V_{\mathrm{cal}} - V$ empty calendar years.
By the sparse-gap condition $G_V / V \to 0$ (A5), $V_{\mathrm{cal}}/V \to 1$ and the calendar-time partial-sum process $\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{V}^{\mathrm{cal}}(\tau) := V_{\mathrm{cal}}^{-1/2}\sum_{y=y_1}^{y_1+\lfloor \tau V_{\mathrm{cal}}\rfloor} \tilde{s}_{y,H}^{\mathrm{pad}}$ differs from $\hat{\mathcal{S}}_V(\tau)$ only by a uniform time-change perturbation $|\phi_V(\tau) - \tau| \le G_V/V_{\mathrm{cal}} \to 0$; by continuity of the limiting Brownian bridge sample paths, $\hat{\mathcal{S}}_V^{\mathrm{cal}}(\cdot) \Rightarrow \Lambda_H^{1/2} B_p(\cdot)$ in $D[0,1]^p$ with the same bandwidth ratio $S/V_{\mathrm{cal}} \to b$.
Writing $\hat{S}_t^{\mathrm{cal}} := \sum_{j=1}^t \tilde{s}_{y_1+j-1,H}^{\mathrm{pad}}$ for the unnormalized padded partial sums (so $V_{\mathrm{cal}}^{-1/2}\hat{S}_{\lfloor r V_{\mathrm{cal}}\rfloor}^{\mathrm{cal}} = \hat{\mathcal{S}}_V^{\mathrm{cal}}(r) \Rightarrow \Lambda_H^{1/2} B_p(r)$ and $\hat{S}_{V_{\mathrm{cal}}}^{\mathrm{cal}} = 0$), applying the exact Bartlett summation-by-parts identity of \citet[Theorem~1 and Theorem~2]{KV05} on the padded regular grid $\{y_1,\dots,y_V\}$ and the continuous mapping theorem gives
\begin{align*}
\hat\Lambda_H(b)
&= \frac{V_{\mathrm{cal}}}{V}\left[\frac{2}{S V_{\mathrm{cal}}}\sum_{t=1}^{V_{\mathrm{cal}}} \hat{S}_t^{\mathrm{cal}}(\hat{S}_t^{\mathrm{cal}})^\top - \frac{1}{S V_{\mathrm{cal}}}\sum_{t=1}^{V_{\mathrm{cal}}-S}\Bigl(\hat{S}_t^{\mathrm{cal}}(\hat{S}_{t+S}^{\mathrm{cal}})^\top + \hat{S}_{t+S}^{\mathrm{cal}}(\hat{S}_t^{\mathrm{cal}})^\top\Bigr)\right] + o_p(1) \\
&\Rightarrow\; \Lambda_H^{1/2}\,\Xi_p(b)\,\Lambda_H^{1/2}.
\end{align*}
Because $\hat{\mathcal{H}}_H \xrightarrow{p} \mathcal{H}_H$ and $\sqrt{V}(R\hat\theta_H - r_0) = -R\mathcal{H}_H^{-1}\mathcal{S}_V(1) + o_p(1)$ jointly with $\hat\Lambda_H(b)$, putting $\Sigma_R := R\mathcal{H}_H^{-1}\Lambda_H\mathcal{H}_H^{-1} R^\top \succ 0$ and using rotational invariance of standard Brownian motion yields $W_V(R) \Rightarrow W_q(1)^\top \Xi_q(b)^{-1} W_q(1)$, and $t_V(a) \Rightarrow t_{\mathrm{KV}}(b)$ for $q = 1$.

\emph{Part (ii).} At the true parameter $\theta_0$, $\mathbb{E}[g_{v,H}(\theta_0)] = 0$ by Condition~(P) (at $H=0$) or Condition~(X) (at $H>0$; Theorem~\ref{thm:finite_pool}(T2)). Since $g_{v,H}(\theta_0)$ is a measurable function of the same finite calendar block of length $L_H$, Lemma~\ref{lem:mixing_moment_inheritance}(a) implies it is 1D $\alpha$-mixing of size $-\lambda < -(2+\delta)/\delta$. By \citet[Theorem~2.11]{WW88}, $V^{-1/2}\sum_{v=1}^{\lfloor \tau V \rfloor} g_{v,H}(\theta_0) \Rightarrow \Omega_g^{1/2} W_1(\tau)$, so the centered partial-sum process $V^{-1/2}\sum_{v=1}^{\lfloor \tau V \rfloor}(g_{v,H}(\theta_0) - \bar{g}_H(\theta_0)) \Rightarrow \Omega_g^{1/2} B_1(\tau)$. Repeating the sparse-gap embedding and \citet[Theorem~1]{KV05} Bartlett identity on the centered scores yields $\hat\Omega_g(\theta_0; b) \Rightarrow \Omega_g\,\Xi_1(b)$ jointly with $\sqrt{V}\bar{g}_H(\theta_0) \Rightarrow \Omega_g^{1/2} W_1(1)$, giving $t_V^{\mathrm{AR}}(\theta_0) \Rightarrow W_1(1)/\sqrt{\Xi_1(b)} = t_{\mathrm{KV}}(b)$ without inverting a Hessian or moment Jacobian \citep{AR49,SW00}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{LLSW bandwidth, sparse calendar gaps, and boundary conventions}
\label{sec:llsw_bandwidth_app}

In finite vintage panels ($V = 23$--$27$), classical HAC inference with normal critical values severely over-rejects because the Bartlett estimator $\hat\Lambda_H$ is pulled toward zero by score centering ($\sum_v \tilde{s}_{v,H} = 0$). Following \citet{LLSW18} and \citet{KV05}, we set the Bartlett truncation lag to $S = \lceil 1.3\sqrt{V}\rceil$ ($S=7$ for $V\in\{23,25,27\}$) and evaluate $t$- and Wald statistics against the fixed-$b$ limit distribution at $b = S/V$ (two-sided 5\% critical values $2.790$ for $V=25$, $2.867$ for $V=23$, and $2.722$ for $V=27$, computed via the conditional Rao--Blackwell method with $10^6$ draws).\footnote{The cubic approximation of \citet[Table~1]{KV05} for the Bartlett $97.5\%$ quantile, $1.9600 + 2.9694b + 0.4160b^2 - 0.5324b^3$, gives $2.812$, $2.887$, and $2.749$ at $b = 7/25$, $7/23$, and $7/27$, that is $0.020$--$0.027$ above our values. The gap is not due to the cubic form: refitting the same constrained cubic to our simulated grid $b = 0.02, 0.04, \dots, 1$ reproduces our values within $0.023$, whereas the \citet{KV05} cubic lies above that grid everywhere (by $0.027$ on average). Our values agree within $0.003$ across two methods (the conditional method and direct quantiles from $10^5$ draws); \citet{KV05} fitted their cubic to critical values from $50{,}000$ replications per $b$. With the \citet{KV05} values, no Buyout or VC $U_{v,H}$ test in the main sample, for any model or horizon, would change its 5\% decision; in the all-funds and NAV-trimmed robustness samples five tests would, none of which the paper reports as a test, and the AR sets of main-text \eqref{eq:ar_set} would widen slightly (script \texttt{results/fixedb\_kv05\_comparison.R}).} With empty calendar years, the padded representation in the proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:proof_fixed_b_ar} suggests the calendar-span ratio $S/V_{\mathrm{cal}}$ instead ($7/26$ for Buyout and for VC $U_{v,H}$, $7/29$ for VC L2). Both ratios share the same limit under sparse gaps; we use $b = S/V$, whose critical values exceed those at $S/V_{\mathrm{cal}}$ ($2.753$ at $7/26$, $2.665$ at $7/29$), so this choice is conservative, most of all for VC $U_{v,H}$ ($2.867$ instead of $2.753$). Whenever an estimate lies on an active boundary constraint of $\Theta$, interior asymptotic normality does not apply; such cells are marked with $\dagger$ and their standard errors are suppressed. Because L1-loss estimates sit at sample kinks where numerical Hessians shrink with step size and the delete-one jackknife is inconsistent for median-type functionals \citep{Efron79}, L1 estimates are treated as descriptive only with no SEs or tests.

\begin{remark}[Calendar-lag versus index-lag Bartlett weighting under missing early vintages]
\label{rem:calendar_vs_index_shac}
In our empirical Preqin panels, a few early calendar years are empty or contain a single fund: Buyout misses 1989 ($V=25$ across 26 calendar years, gap share $3.8\%$); VC L2 misses 1983--1984 ($V=27$ across 29 years, $6.9\%$); and VC $U_{v,H}$ excludes four singleton vintages (1982, 1988, 1989, 1991; $V=23$ across 26 years 1985--2010, $11.5\%$).
Comparing our calendar-distance Bartlett kernel $K(|y_v - y_w|/S)$ against the integer index-lag Bartlett kernel $K(|v - w|/S)$ (script \texttt{results/calendar\_vs\_index\_shac.R}, output \texttt{calendar\_vs\_index\_shac.csv}) shows that the inception ($H=0$) conclusions are unchanged:
at $H = 0$, Buyout $U_{v,0}$ SEs shift by only $+0.00004$ EW ($0.1180 \to 0.1181$, $p_1 = 0.325$ unchanged) and $+0.0027$ FW ($0.1426 \to 0.1454$, $p_1$: $0.280 \to 0.288$), while AR lower bounds shift by $-0.005$ (EW) and $-0.008$ (FW).
In Venture Capital $U_{v,0}$, index-lag weighting pulls the 1987 vintage artificially closer to 1990--1993 and lowers the SE by $0.031$ ($0.325 \to 0.294$ EW, $p_1$: $0.247 \to 0.206$; $0.342 \to 0.310$ FW, $p_1$: $0.116 \to 0.089$), while VC AR lower bounds shift by $-0.018$ (EW) and $-0.023$ (FW).
Thus calendar-distance weighting is slightly more conservative in VC, and no $H=0$ test decision at the 5\% level changes. Across all 40 estimator--horizon cells ($U_{v,H}$ and L2 at $H\in\{0,5,10,15,30\}$, both series and weightings), five $p$-values cross a conventional threshold under index-lag weighting: VC FW $U_{v,0}$ $p_1$ ($0.116 \to 0.089$, across 10\%); the $\beta=0$ $p$-values of VC EW $U_{v,15}$ ($0.021 \to 0.007$), VC FW $U_{v,10}$ ($0.011 \to 0.007$) and VC FW $U_{v,15}$ ($0.019 \to 0.007$), each moving from $p<0.05$ to $p<0.01$; and Buyout FW L2 $p_1$ at 30 years ($0.067 \to 0.046$, across 5\%).
\end{remark}

\subsection{Diagnosis of large-$V$ studentization distortion and low $N_{\mathrm{eff}}$}
\label{sec:v400_studentization_app}

In Table~\ref{tab:mc_size_additions}, equal-vintage $U_{v,H}$ ($q_v=1$) on i.i.d.\ resampled factor paths still rejects $\beta_0 = 1$ in $6.8\%$--$10.8\%$ of draws at $V = 400$ with low median $N_{\mathrm{eff}} = 13\text{--}27$ and a pronounced left-tail excess (left/right counts $22/12$ to $38/16$), whereas replacing the random sandwich SE with the Monte Carlo SD removes the excess.
Table~\ref{tab:mc_v400_studentization} (\texttt{simulation/mc\_v400\_diagnostic.R}, 500 replications per design) resolves this puzzle cleanly through mechanism ablations at $V=400$ (Panel~A) and increasing-domain scaling through $V=1{,}600$ (Panel~B):
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{Consistent with asymptotic validity, but convergence is slow because score kurtosis is large.} At $H = 0$, $U_{v,0}$ rejection declines to nominal $4.4\%$ ($n_v=20$, $\mathrm{SE}/\mathrm{SD}=1.00$, $N_{\mathrm{eff}}=69.4$) and $5.0\%$ ($n_v=5$, $\mathrm{SE}/\mathrm{SD}=0.96$, $N_{\mathrm{eff}}=27.5$) by $V = 1{,}600$, while the estimator SD shrinks at close to the $V^{-1/2}$ rate ($0.0668 \to 0.0289 \to 0.0146 \to 0.0105 \to 0.0067$ across $V = 25, 100, 400, 800, 1{,}600$ at $n_v=20$, i.e.\ $\mathrm{SD}\cdot\sqrt{V} = 0.33, 0.29, 0.29, 0.30, 0.27$), and the kurtosis of $\hat\beta - 1$ is already $2.97$--$3.47$ at $V=400$, against $4.6$--$85.6$ at $V \le 100$ (\texttt{simulation/mc\_v400\_error\_moments.R}, output \texttt{v400\_error\_moments.csv}). With 500 replications per cell the Monte Carlo SE of a 5\% rejection rate is about 1 point, so single-cell differences of 2--3 points are only marginally significant.
  \item \emph{Channel~1 ($H=0$ left-tail excess): compounded lognormal deal skewness.} In the calibrated deal model ($\sigma = 0.130$/mo over up to $138$ months, log-variance $k s^2 \le 2.31$), rare right-skewed payoff multiples generate strongly left-skewed vintage scores $g_v(\beta_0)$ (median skewness $-1.81$ at $n_v=20$ and $-3.19$ at $n_v=5$; the largest-magnitude score is negative in $87\%$--$90\%$ of draws). Because $\hat\beta - \beta_0 \approx -\mathcal{H}^{-1}\bar g(\beta_0)$ with $\mathcal{H}>0$, negative score outliers push $\hat\beta$ upward while inflating $\hat\Psi$, inducing $\mathrm{Corr}(\hat\beta-\beta_0, \widehat{\mathrm{SE}}) = +0.28$ ($n_v=20$) to $+0.47$ ($n_v=5$) and shrinking $\widehat{\mathrm{SE}}$ in the left tail. Replacing resampled historical returns with i.i.d.\ Gaussian returns leaves $N_{\mathrm{eff}}$ ($28.1$ vs.\ $27.2$) and skewness ($-1.84$ vs.\ $-1.81$) unchanged---ruling out non-Gaussian equity crashes---whereas replacing lognormal deal shocks with symmetric shocks (an ablation outside Assumption~\ref{ass:deal_level_dgp}: $22\%$ of the variance-matched symmetric payoffs are negative, $37\%$ for deals held at least 11 years; Table~\ref{tab:mc_v400_studentization}), lowering $\sigma$ to $0.05$, or shortening deal lives to $2/2$~years immediately eliminates $\mathrm{Corr}(\hat\beta-\beta_0, \widehat{\mathrm{SE}})$, doubles $N_{\mathrm{eff}}$ to $45$--$52$, and brings $H=0$ rejection to $3.2\%$--$6.0\%$.
  \item \emph{Channel~2 (extra $H=15$ distortion): stochastic benchmark compounding $A_{v,H}^2$.} Even under symmetric light-tailed deal shocks, extending the horizon from $H=0$ to $H=15$~yr cuts $N_{\mathrm{eff}}$ from $50.4$ to $19.2$, raises score kurtosis from $7.9$ to $21.3$ (because $s_{v,15}^4$ scales with $A_{v,15}^8$; cf.\ \citealp{Martin12}), lowers $\mathrm{SE}/\mathrm{SD}$ from $1.00$ to $0.88$, and lifts Wald rejection from $3.2\%$ to $9.0\%$ (symmetric $21/24$). Lowering monthly market volatility to $5\%$/yr eliminates this $H=15$ distortion completely ($N_{\mathrm{eff}} = 81.4$, kurtosis $4.9$, $\mathrm{SE}/\mathrm{SD}=0.97$, rejection $4.0\%$).
  \item \emph{Practical diagnostic and null-evaluated score check.} Evaluating the centered fixed-$b$ score statistic at the null $\beta_0$ (column \texttt{Score\,\%}, avoiding plug-in leverage shrinkage and Hessian estimation noise) eliminates the $H=15$ over-rejection already at $V=400$ ($4.8\%$ at $n_v=20$, $3.6\%$ at $n_v=5$) and remains calibrated at $V=1{,}600$ ($4.2\%$--$5.4\%$), although at $H=0$ it still rejects $6.0\%$--$7.8\%$ at $V=400$--$800$. In empirical practice, we therefore recommend reporting $N_{\mathrm{eff}}$ alongside fixed-$b$ Wald SEs (our empirical $H=0$ Preqin $U_{v,0}$ panels have $N_{\mathrm{eff}} = 5.3$ BO EW, $8.9$ BO FW, $4.5$ VC EW, and $4.9$ VC FW---consistently higher than L2's $5.1, 8.4, 2.9, 1.9$), preferring $H=0$ when $c_v=0$, and pairing Wald intervals with the identification-robust AR moment set \eqref{eq:ar_set} whenever $N_{\mathrm{eff}}$ is modest.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Preqin sample filtering, fund-size imputation, terminal NAVs, and $hv$-block cross-validation}
\label{sec:appendix_data_engineering}

\paragraph{Constant-composition complete-history filter and sample sensitivity.}
The empirical panel uses the Preqin cash-flow export as of April~14, 2022, restricted to North American USD-denominated Buyout (BO) and Venture Capital (VC) funds to match the U.S.\ $q^5$ factors (\url{http://global-q.org/factors.html}; \citealp{HXZ15,hou2021augmented}).
To prevent survivor and reporting-horizon distortions, a fund enters the baseline complete-history sample only if its last Preqin record lies more than five years after January~1 of its vintage year, or if it is still reporting at the export end (last record within 12 months of March~31, 2022); 218 of 1{,}033 North American Buyout and VC funds with vintages up to 2010 stop reporting earlier (predominantly a vendor reporting artifact) and are excluded rather than having an early stale NAV booked as a liquidating cash flow.
In the seasoned baseline ($V \le 2010$), this yields 383 Buyout funds across 25 vintages (1985--2010; 1989 absent, all 25 vintages with $n_v \ge 2$) and 432 Venture Capital funds across 27 vintages (1982--2010; 1983--1984 absent; four singleton vintages 1982, 1988, 1989, and 1991 with $n_v=1$, leaving 23 eligible vintages for $U_{v,H}$).
Table~\ref{tab:robustness_samples} verifies stability across (i) the complete-history baseline, (ii) an \emph{all-funds} sample retaining truncated histories (495 BO / 538 VC funds), and (iii) a \emph{20\% terminal-NAV-threshold} sample dropping funds that stop reporting after age five but before March~31, 2021 with a final NAV exceeding 20\% of total value (328 BO / 344 VC funds).

\paragraph{Fund-size imputation.}
In the baseline $V \le 2010$ panel, fund size is missing for 10 of 383 Buyout funds (2.6\%) and 15 of 432 VC funds (3.5\%), heavily concentrated in early small vintages (1985--1995 in BO; 1982--1994 in VC).
Imputing the pooled cross-vintage mean (\$1{,}842.24M Buyout; \$379.43M VC) assigns 94.9\% weight to a single missing-size 1986 Buyout fund, 92.8\% to two 1992 Buyout funds, 51.5\% to two 1988 Buyout funds, 98.8\% to two 1985 VC funds, and 87.3\% to two 1986 VC funds.
Our baseline FW rule therefore imputes the \emph{within-vintage median} observed fund size (expanding symmetrically to the nearest adjacent vintages with observed sizes only for singleton 1982 VC, which receives the 1985 median of \$9M), yielding $\mathrm{SD}(\log S) = 1.185$ (BO) and $0.941$ (VC)---matching our FW Monte Carlo Design~B ($\sigma_{\log S} = 1.19$ BO, $0.90$ VC).
Table~\ref{tab:fw_imputation_sensitivity} compares within-vintage median imputation, pooled cross-vintage mean imputation, and dropping missing-size funds (which shrinks Buyout $U_{v,H}$ from 25 to 22 vintages while L2 keeps 25): Buyout FW $U_{v,0}$ moves only from $1.197$ (median) to $1.205$ (mean) and $1.226$ (drop), whereas pooled-mean imputation exaggerates Buyout FW L2's 0--30~yr decline from $0.427$ to $0.634$ and flips the inception $\mathrm{L2}-U$ gap from $+0.028$ to $-0.013$.

\paragraph{Terminal NAV haircuts and $hv$-block cross-validation.}
In $V \le 2010$, median vintage-level booked terminal NAV shares are $8.1\%$ (BO EW), $5.4\%$ (BO FW), $10.8\%$ (VC EW), and $8.1\%$ (VC FW), with 2006--2010 averages of $12.2\%/10.7\%$ in Buyout and $27.1\%/24.2\%$ in VC (Table~\ref{tab:terminal_nav_shares} and Figure~\ref{fig:terminal_nav_shares}).
Across five constant terminal-NAV haircuts from 0\% (full NAV) to 100\% (complete write-off), single-factor inception L2 betas move smoothly and modestly: $1.283 \to 1.170$ (BO EW), $1.224 \to 1.133$ (BO FW), $1.925 \to 1.877$ (VC EW), and $2.287 \to 2.245$ (VC FW; Figure~\ref{fig:nav_disc_BOVC_MKT}), providing a transparent nonparametric envelope without imposing a parametric residual-value model \citep{JLRS20,brown2023nowcasting}.
To provide a descriptive stability check alongside fixed-$b$ inference, Table~\ref{tab:appendix_e_cv_folds} reports rolling $hv$-block cross-validation \citep{R00} with 3-year validation blocks buffered by 3-year exclusion zones on each side (8 folds in $V \le 2010$), evaluating every horizon's fold estimates on the common held-out $H=0$ inception loss (minimized at $H=0$ for BO FW $0.117$, VC EW $0.303$, and VC FW $0.375$, and flat in BO EW at $0.109$ vs.\ $0.106$ at $H=20$).
