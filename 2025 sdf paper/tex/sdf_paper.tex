\begin{center}
  {\Large\bfseries Cash-Flow Pricing of Non-Traded Assets: \\[0.5ex] Finite-Pool Identification, Horizon Selection, and Inference\par}
\end{center}
\vspace{0.5em}

\begin{abstract}
\singlespacing
\noindent Non-traded assets---including private equity, venture capital, private credit, real estate, and infrastructure---generate irregular, pooled cash-flow streams grouped into finite vintage cohorts rather than periodic traded returns.
We show that estimating nonlinear cash-flow discounting models by minimizing squared pooled net present values suffers from a structural finite-pool identification failure: within-vintage cash-flow noise introduces a parameter-dependent variance term that tilts the population target upward even at inception.
Averaging pricing errors across post-inception compounding horizons multiplies each vintage error by a parameter-dependent scale factor, generating a downward scale-Jacobian pull that displays rather than removes the bias.
We introduce a within-vintage cross-fund pair-product criterion that eliminates idiosyncratic variance tilt under bounded pool sizes, establish an exact multi-deal duration-matching theory for optimal horizon selection under shared and cross-vintage shocks, prove that cross-vintage leave-band-out pair products collapse to rank one, and develop primitive one-dimensional fixed-$b$ and identification-robust inference.
Monte Carlo simulations confirm finite-pool debiasing, compounding-kurtosis studentization dynamics, and horizon coercivity.
An application to North American Buyout and Venture Capital cash flows yields market betas of 1.15--1.20 and 1.49--1.74, while revealing that abnormal performance and second-factor loadings are only weakly identified.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Benchmark deflator; Fixed-$b$ inference; Non-traded cash flows; Private equity; Stochastic discount factor; U-statistics.

\noindent\textbf{JEL classification:} C14, C23, C58, G12, G24.

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
%% =============================================================================

Across private equity, venture capital, private credit, commercial real estate, infrastructure, collateralized loan obligations, and project finance, researchers rarely observe periodic traded returns.
Instead, empirical panels consist of irregular, pooled cash-flow streams grouped into finite vintage cohorts ($n_v < \infty$ funds or projects per vintage $v$) whose active lives overlap in calendar time.
Estimating nonlinear present-value discounting parameters $\theta = (\alpha, \beta)$ directly from such cash flows by minimizing squared pooled pricing errors creates an econometric problem with no counterpart in linear traded-return factor regressions: both the discounting factors and the conditional variance of discounted payoffs depend nonlinearly on $\theta$, while vintage counts $V$ are modest and per-vintage pool sizes $n_v$ are finite.

This paper develops a unified identification, bias-correction, horizon-selection, and inference framework for nonlinear cash-flow pricing models of pooled non-traded assets.
Although our empirical application examines Buyout and Venture Capital funds---building on the reciprocal benchmark-deflator paradigm pioneered by \citet{DLP12}---the econometric mechanisms apply to any nonlinear discounting M-estimator operating on finite cohorts of irregular cash flows.
We make four main contributions.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{Finite-pool identification failure and exact horizon rescaling (Theorem~\ref{thm:finite_pool}, T1--T6).}
  Minimizing squared vintage-portfolio net present values (NPVs)---the inception criterion of \citet{DLP12}---targets $\mathbb{E}[PV_v(\theta)^2] = \operatorname{Var}(PV_v(\theta)) + (\mathbb{E}[PV_v(\theta)])^2$.
  Because higher discount rates shrink random future payouts toward zero, the variance term varies with $\theta$ and tilts the population minimizer upward even at inception ($H=0$), where the mean pricing restriction $\mathbb{E}[PV_v(\theta_0)] = 0$ holds exactly; consistency of squared loss holds only in the infill limit $n_v \to \infty$ with no shared vintage shocks.
  Averaging pricing errors over post-inception compounding horizons $H > 0$ adds no independent observations: we prove the exact algebraic factorization $\bar\epsilon_{v,H}(\theta) = A_{v,H}(\theta)\,PV_v(\theta)$.
  Under inception pricing~(P) alone, multiplying by the random post-inception scale $A_{v,H}(\theta_0)$ creates a first-moment covariance wedge; even under strict factor-path exogeneity~(X), differentiating $A_{v,H}(\theta)^2$ introduces a downward \emph{Scale-Jacobian pull} that moves the squared-loss target along a DGP-dependent curve rather than removing noise bias.

  \item \emph{Cross-fund pair-product criterion $U_{v,H}$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:finite_pool}, T7--T8).}
  To remove the own-unit diagonal variance directly under bounded pool sizes ($n_v \ge 2$), we introduce the within-vintage cross-fund pair-product criterion $U_{v,H}(\theta)$, which averages distinct-fund products $\bar\epsilon_{i,H}(\theta)\bar\epsilon_{j,H}(\theta)$ ($i \neq j$) across vintages with equal vintage weights ($q_v = 1$).
  Under strict factor-path exogeneity~(X), conditional independence across funds within a vintage, and absent shared vintage shocks ($c_v = 0$), the diagonal variance term cancels identically and $U_{v,H}$ targets $\theta_0$ at \emph{every} horizon---including inception ($H=0$)---as $V \to \infty$ with fixed $n_v \ge 2$.
  When shared vintage shocks are present ($c_v > 0$), $U_{v,H}$ still removes all $O(1/n_v)$ idiosyncratic noise while inheriting a shared-shock tilt and non-coercivity at $\alpha \to +\infty$ at $H = 0$.

  \item \emph{Optimal horizon theory and impossibility of cross-vintage pair products (Proposition~\ref{prop:optimal_H}).}
  We formalize horizon choice across three error-dependence regimes: i.i.d.\ fund noise ($c_v = 0$), independent shared vintage shocks ($c_v > 0$), and locally dependent cross-vintage shocks ($c_{v,w} \neq 0$ for nearby vintages $|y_v - y_w| \le L$).
  Whenever $\sigma_{\mathrm{eff},v}^2 > 0$, setting $H$ to match the compounding sensitivity $\bar{S}_{v,H}^{\mathrm{comp}}$ to the covariance-weighted payoff duration $T_{\mathrm{eff}}$ yields a unique zero-bias horizon $H_0^\ast \in (T_{\mathrm{eff}}, \min(2T_{\mathrm{eff}}, T_{\mathrm{eff}} + 1/r))$ that shrinks with benchmark drift and volatility, remains invariant to stationary AR(1) cohort persistence $\rho_W$, lengthens strictly under overlapping calendar-time shocks $e_u$, and governs a finite-$V$ MSE expansion $H_{\mathrm{MSE}}^\ast(V) = H_0^\ast - O(V^{-1})$.
  By contrast, attempting to eliminate shared or cross-vintage shocks at $H = 0$ via leave-band-out cross-vintage pair products $\widetilde{U}_{H,m}^{\mathrm{cross}}$ collapses asymptotically to the rank-1 squared linear mean moment and is indefinite in finite $V$; in our Monte Carlo its minimizers lie on the boundary in $42\%$--$100\%$ of draws.

  \item \emph{Primitive 1D increasing-domain asymptotics, fixed-$b$ inference, and AR sets (Proposition~\ref{prop:consistency_clt} and Corollary~\ref{cor:fixed_b_ar}).}
  Because vintage overlap operates strictly along one-dimensional calendar time $y_v \in \mathbb{Z}$, we establish consistency, asymptotic normality, and fixed-$b$ Bartlett HAC / Anderson--Rubin (AR) moment-inversion inference \citep{KV05,LLSW18,AR49,SW00} directly from primitive bounded-lifetime mixing and moment conditions (Assumptions~A1--A5 and Lemma~\ref{lem:mixing_moment_inheritance} in the Online Supplement).
  Across 72 calibrated Monte Carlo designs and a large-$V$ ablation study up to $V = 1{,}600$, we verify finite-pool debiasing, diagnose why studentized Wald tests converge slowly under compounded lognormal payoff and return-scale kurtosis, and show that null-evaluated fixed-$b$ score tests remove the long-horizon Wald over-rejection.
\end{enumerate}

Applying our framework to a cleaned North American USD Preqin panel (383 Buyout funds across 25 vintages and 432 Venture Capital funds across 27 vintages, $V \le 2010$) illustrates every theoretical prediction.
Equal-vintage $U_{v,0}$ yields economically coherent single-factor market betas of $1.147$ (SE $0.118$) equal-weighted (EW) and $1.197$ (SE $0.143$) fund-size weighted (FW) for Buyout, and $1.493$ (SE $0.325$) EW and $1.742$ (SE $0.342$) FW for Venture Capital---none distinguishable from unity ($p_1 \in [0.116, 0.325]$)---while squared-loss L2 declines sharply with $H$ and turns negative at 30 years in Venture Capital via the Scale-Jacobian term.
Identification-robust 95\% AR sets on the inception mean moment avoid fragile multiple-root point estimation, cover both $\beta=1$ and $U_{v,0}$, and rule out very low Buyout betas ($<0.95$ EW, $<0.81$ FW).
By contrast, abnormal performance $\alpha$ and non-market $q^5$ factor loadings are only weakly identified: Buyout $(\alpha,\beta)$ estimates trade off tightly ($\widehat{\mathrm{Corr}} \approx -0.8$), Venture Capital inception estimates sit on the $+2\%$ monthly alpha ceiling solely due to the 1993--1997 cohort, and non-market $q^5$ loadings fluctuate or reverse sign across horizons.

\paragraph{Related literature.}
Methodologically, our cross-fund pair-product criterion $U_{v,H}$ connects directly to the econometric literature on leave-one-out / jackknife quadratic-form debiasing and U-statistic M-estimation, including jackknife instrumental variables \citep{AIK99,CSHNW12}, leave-out variance-component estimation in two-way and high-dimensional linear models \citep{CJN18,KSS20}, jackknife Anderson--Rubin weak-identification inference \citep{MS22}, and pairwise-difference extremum estimators \citep{HP94}.
Pooled cash-flow discounting differs sharply from linear jackknife IV and variance-component settings in four structural respects: (a) the pricing error is nonlinear in $\theta$ and compounded by a parameter-dependent horizon scale $A_{v,H}(\theta)^2$; (b) at $H=0$, dropping diagonal squares does not prevent non-coercive boundary collapse as $\alpha \to +\infty$ under shared vintage shocks; (c) shared and overlapping calendar-time shocks interact with compounding through an exact multi-deal duration-matching equation (Proposition~\ref{prop:optimal_H}(i)--(iii)); and (d) extending leave-out pairing across distinct vintages destroys full-rank factor-path matching, collapsing to rank one and producing finite-$V$ indefiniteness (Proposition~\ref{prop:optimal_H}(iv)).

In empirical financial economics, we clarify the structural boundary between cash-flow-identified reciprocal benchmark deflators \citep{DLP12}---applied across private equity, real estate, and human capital \citep{kroencke2013return,kim2015estimation,farrelly2019risk,lee2019new}---and public-calibrated stochastic discount factor (SDF) evaluation frameworks \citep{KN16,GSW20,GN18,andonov2021institutional,riddiough2022private,cordell2023clo,erel2024risk,jeffers2024risk,KN24,korteweg2024private}.
We complement \citet{GSW20} on NPV versus per-period return biases and \citet{burnside2016identification} on SDF identification, while linking our empirical diagnostics to parametric private-equity models \citep{FNP12,ACGP18}, tailored PME benchmarks \citep{HJK14,RS16}, secondary-market pricing \citep{boyer2023discount}, NAV smoothing \citep{BGK19,JLRS20,brown2023nowcasting}, and drift-uncertainty bounds \citep{Merton80}.

\section{Model and Finite-Pool Identification}
\label{sec:model_id}
%% =============================================================================

\subsection{Pooled cash-flow pricing restrictions}
\label{sec:asset_pricing_pooled}

Non-traded investment funds deploy capital across multiple quarters, so any fund-level pricing restriction aggregates deal-level cash flows initiated at different dates.
Throughout, the deal-level structure serves solely as a latent accounting device justifying fund-level moments; only pooled fund cash flows enter estimation.
For fund $i$, let $CF_{i,t}$ denote the observed net cash flow at month $t$, and define fund inception as $t_i^0 := \min_j t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Inv}}$.

\begin{assume}[Deal-level decomposition]
\label{ass:deal_level_dgp}
For each fund $i$, there exist $J_i$ underlying deals such that:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Deal $j$ has a single negative investment cash flow $\mathrm{Inv}_{i,j} < 0$ at date $t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Inv}} \in \{0,1,\dots,T-1\}$.
  \item Deal $j$ generates a finite stream of positive divestments $(\mathrm{Div}_{i,j,k})_{k=1}^{K_{i,j}}$ with $\mathrm{Div}_{i,j,k} > 0$ and $t_{i,j,k}^{\mathrm{Div}} > t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Inv}}$.
  \item Observed fund cash flows equal the cross-deal sum $CF_{i,t} = \sum_{j=1}^{J_i} cf_{i,j,t}$ for all $i,t$, with $CF_{i,t} = 0$ for $t < t_i^0$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{assume}

Let $\Psi_t = \prod_{u=1}^t M_u$ ($\Psi_0 = 1$) denote the cumulative deflator discounting a time-$t$ cash flow to time $0$, and write $\Psi_{\tau,t} = \Psi_t / \Psi_\tau$.
Under exact deal-level pricing at acquisition, the deal-level pricing error
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:deal_pricing_error}
\delta_{i,j} := \mathrm{Inv}_{i,j} + \sum_{k=1}^{K_{i,j}} \frac{\Psi_{t_{i,j,k}^{\mathrm{Div}}}}{\Psi_{t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Inv}}}}\,\mathrm{Div}_{i,j,k}
\end{equation}
satisfies the conditional zero-moment restriction
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:deal_pricing}
\mathbb{E}\!\left[\delta_{i,j} \,\middle|\, \mathcal{F}_{t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Inv}}}\right] = 0 \qquad \forall\, i,j.
\end{equation}
In our reciprocal benchmark specification $M_u(\theta) = G_u(\theta)^{-1} = (1 + r_{f,u} + \alpha + \beta^\top F_u)^{-1}$, the cumulative deflator $\Psi_t(\theta)$ is an \emph{asset-class-specific reciprocal benchmark deflator}: a valid strictly positive SDF on the private-market cash-flow space in incomplete markets \citep{HR87}, rather than a public-calibrated pricing kernel imposing equity and Treasury pricing moments \citep{KN16,KN24}.
As proved in Online Supplement Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_sdf}, estimating distinct loadings $\beta_0$ across asset classes remains fully compatible with a single economy-wide public SDF $M_u^\ast$: whenever $M_u^\ast$ conditionally prices the traded benchmark $R_u^0 = 1 + r_{f,u} + \beta_0^\top F_u$ each period and multiplicative idiosyncratic noise has mean one given the factor/SDF paths and random exit stopping time $b$, optional stopping implies that $M_u^\ast$ also prices the deal when $\alpha_0 = 0$, while $\alpha_0 > 0$ ($\alpha_0 < 0$) maps strictly to positive (negative) public-SDF NPV.

\begin{lemma}[Deal-level timing]
\label{lem:deal_level_timing}
Suppose Equation~\eqref{eq:deal_pricing} holds and all products below are integrable (proof in Online Supplement Appendix~\ref{sec:proof_timing}).
\begin{enumerate}
  \item If $\tau \le t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Inv}}$, then
  \begin{equation}
  \label{eq:zero_deal_expectation}
  \mathbb{E}\!\left[\frac{\Psi_{t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Inv}}}}{\Psi_\tau}\,\delta_{i,j} \,\middle|\, \mathcal{F}_\tau\right] = 0.
  \end{equation}
  \item If $\tau > t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Inv}}$, then
  \begin{equation}
  \label{eq:non_zero_deal_expectation}
  \mathbb{E}\!\left[\frac{\Psi_{t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Inv}}}}{\Psi_\tau}\,\delta_{i,j} \,\middle|\, \mathcal{F}_\tau\right]
  = \frac{\Psi_{t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Inv}}}}{\Psi_\tau}\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[\delta_{i,j} \,\middle|\, \mathcal{F}_\tau\right],
  \end{equation}
  which generally differs from zero because payoffs realized by date $\tau$ are $\mathcal{F}_\tau$-measurable.
  \item If $\tau \le t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Inv}} < t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Future}}$, compounding forward to $t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Future}}$ defines the net-future-value moment $m_{i,j}^{\mathrm{NFV}}(\tau,t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Future}}) := (\Psi_{t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Inv}}}/\Psi_{t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Future}}})\,\delta_{i,j}$, whose conditional expectation given $\mathcal{F}_\tau$ equals
  \begin{equation}
  \label{eq:future_value_bias_derivation}
  \mathbb{E}\!\left[m_{i,j}^{\mathrm{NFV}}\!\left(\tau,t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Future}}\right) \,\middle|\, \mathcal{F}_\tau\right]
  = \mathbb{E}\!\left[\mathrm{Cov}\!\left(\frac{\Psi_{t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Inv}}}}{\Psi_{t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Future}}}},\,\delta_{i,j} \,\middle|\, \mathcal{F}_{t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Inv}}}\right) \,\middle|\, \mathcal{F}_\tau\right],
  \end{equation}
  which is generically nonzero whenever $\delta_{i,j}$ covaries with the forward compounding factor $\Psi_{t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Inv}}}/\Psi_{t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Future}}} = \prod_{u=t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Inv}}+1}^{t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Future}}} G_u$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

Aggregating Part~(1) across deals yields the pooled fund-level inception pricing restriction (proof in Online Supplement Appendix~\ref{sec:proof_timing}).

\begin{proposition}[Price of a pooled cash-flow stream at or before inception]
\label{theo:inception_value}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:deal_level_dgp} and Equation~\eqref{eq:deal_pricing}, if $\tau \le t_i^0$, then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:price_pooled_cash_flows}
\mathbb{E}\!\left[\sum_{t=\tau}^{T} \frac{\Psi_t}{\Psi_\tau}\,CF_{i,t} \,\middle|\, \mathcal{F}_\tau\right] = 0.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\subsection{Finite-pool identification, horizon rescaling, and the $U_{v,H}$ criterion}
\label{sec:fundwise_lmd_estimator}

For any unit $i$ (an individual fund or a vintage-year portfolio $v$) and candidate $\theta \in \Theta \subset \mathbb{R}^p$, define the time-$\tau$ revaluation of the cash-flow stream and its horizon average over $\mathcal{T}_{i,H} = \{t_i^0, t_i^0+1, \dots, \min(t_i^0 + 12H, T_i^{\max})\}$ ($H \ge 0$ in years) as
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:average_pricing_error}
\epsilon_{\tau,i}(\theta) := \sum_{t=0}^{T} \frac{\Psi_t(\theta)}{\Psi_\tau(\theta)}\,CF_{i,t},
\qquad
\bar\epsilon_{i,H}(\theta) := \frac{1}{|\mathcal{T}_{i,H}|} \sum_{\tau \in \mathcal{T}_{i,H}} \epsilon_{\tau,i}(\theta),
\end{equation}
with inception NPV $PV_i(\theta) := \epsilon_{t_i^0,i}(\theta) = \bar\epsilon_{i,0}(\theta)$ (Online Supplement Figure~\ref{fig:npvs}).
The squared-loss Least-Mean-Distance (L2 VYP) estimator solves
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:estimator_H}
\hat\theta_{H}^{\mathrm{L2}} := \arg\min_{\theta \in \Theta} \frac{1}{V} \sum_{v=1}^{V} \bar\epsilon_{v,H}(\theta)^2,
\end{equation}
which coincides with the identity-weighted cross-sectional GMM criterion of \citet{DLP12} at $H = 0$.

\paragraph{Motivating one-deal algebra.}
Consider a single-deal unit investing $1$ at $t=0$ and paying $G_1(\theta_0)\cdots G_T(\theta_0)\,N$ at $t=T$, where $N \ge 0$ is an idiosyncratic shock independent of the factor path with $\mathbb{E}[N]=1$ and $\operatorname{Var}(N)=\sigma_N^2 > 0$.
Over $K = |\mathcal{T}_H|$ valuation dates, exact factorization gives $\bar\epsilon_H(\theta) = A_H(\theta)\,PV(\theta)$ with $A_H(\theta) = K^{-1}\sum_{\tau=0}^{K-1} \Psi_\tau(\theta)^{-1}$ and $PV(\theta) = (\Psi_T(\theta)/\Psi_T(\theta_0))N - 1$.
Hence
\[
\mathbb{E}\!\left[\bar\epsilon_H(\theta)^2 \,\middle|\, \mathcal{F}_T^{\mathrm{pub}}\right]
= A_H(\theta)^2\!\left[\left(\frac{\Psi_T(\theta)}{\Psi_T(\theta_0)} - 1\right)^2 + \sigma_N^2\left(\frac{\Psi_T(\theta)}{\Psi_T(\theta_0)}\right)^2\right].
\]
Differentiating with respect to $\theta$ and evaluating at $\theta = \theta_0$ immediately yields
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:one_deal_gradient}
\left.\nabla_\theta\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[\bar\epsilon_H(\theta)^2 \,\middle|\, \mathcal{F}_T^{\mathrm{pub}}\right]\right|_{\theta=\theta_0}
= 2\,\sigma_N^2\,A_H(\theta_0)^2\!\left[\nabla_\theta \log \Psi_T(\theta_0) + \nabla_\theta \log A_H(\theta_0)\right].
\end{equation}
At $H=0$ ($A_0 \equiv 1$, $\nabla_\theta \log A_0 = 0$), the gradient is nonzero whenever $\sigma_N^2 > 0$, tilting the inception squared-loss target toward higher discount rates.
As $H$ increases, $\nabla_\theta \log A_H(\theta_0)$ opposes $\nabla_\theta \log \Psi_T(\theta_0)$ and pulls the target downward (for $\alpha$ under $G_u \equiv 1$, crossing zero at $K = 2T + 1$ valuation dates).
Replacing $\bar\epsilon_H(\theta)^2$ with a cross-product of two independent units $\bar\epsilon_{1,H}(\theta)\bar\epsilon_{2,H}(\theta)$ annihilates the $\sigma_N^2$ term identically at every $H \ge 0$.

\paragraph{General finite-pool identification and $U_{v,H}$.}
Consider $V$ vintages; vintage $v$ contains $n_v \ge 1$ funds $i \in \mathcal{I}_v$ with positive fund weights $\omega_{v,i}$ summing to $1$ ($\omega_{v,i} = 1/n_v$ under EW; $\omega_{v,i} = S_{v,i}/\sum_{j \in \mathcal{I}_v} S_{v,j}$ under FW), sharing a common vintage origin $t_v^0$ and evaluation window $\mathcal{T}_{v,H}$, with vintage-pool cash flow $CF_{v,t} = \sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_v} \omega_{v,i} CF_{i,t}$ and $PV_v(\theta) = \sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_v}\omega_{v,i} PV_i(\theta)$.
Maintain two benchmark pricing conditions:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Condition~(P) (Inception pricing):} $\mathbb{E}[PV_i(\theta_0) \mid \mathcal{F}_{t_v^0}] = 0$ for every fund $i \in \mathcal{I}_v$, as guaranteed by Proposition~\ref{theo:inception_value}.
  \item \emph{Condition~(X) (Strict factor-path exogeneity):} $\mathbb{E}[PV_i(\theta_0) \mid \mathcal{G}_v] = 0$, where $\mathcal{G}_v := (\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{pub}}, \mathcal{W}_v)$ collects the public factor path, vintage origin $t_v^0$, eligibility ($n_v \ge 2$), and fund sizes $\{S_{v,i}\}$. Condition~(X) holds automatically for multiplicative payoffs $I_a (\prod_{u=a+1}^b G_u(\theta_0)) N$ with $\mathbb{E}[N \mid \mathcal{G}_v] = 1$, and fails when payoff shocks correlate with the realized factor path during the holding window (Design~A$'$ in Section~\ref{sec:simulation_study}).
\end{itemize}

For eligible vintages ($n_v \ge 2$) with $\mathcal{W}_v$-measurable weights $\omega_{v,i}$, define the \emph{cross-fund pair-product criterion}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:u_criterion}
\widehat{U}_H(\theta) := \frac{\sum_{v:\, n_v \ge 2} q_v\, U_{v,H}(\theta)}{\sum_{v:\, n_v \ge 2} q_v},
\qquad
U_{v,H}(\theta) := \frac{\sum_{i \neq j \in \mathcal{I}_v} \omega_{v,i}\omega_{v,j}\,\bar\epsilon_{i,H}(\theta)\bar\epsilon_{j,H}(\theta)}{1 - \sum_{i \in \mathcal{I}_v}\omega_{v,i}^2},
\end{equation}
with equal vintage weights $q_v = 1$ as our baseline ($q_v = n_v$ concentrates effective sample size in large late vintages and severely distorts fixed-$b$ inference; Section~\ref{sec:simulation_study}).
The exact $O(n_v)$ identity
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:u_identity}
U_{v,H}(\theta) = \frac{\bar\epsilon_{v,H}(\theta)^2 - \sum_{i \in \mathcal{I}_v}\omega_{v,i}^2\,\bar\epsilon_{i,H}(\theta)^2}{1 - \sum_{i \in \mathcal{I}_v}\omega_{v,i}^2}
= A_{v,H}(\theta)^2\,\frac{PV_v(\theta)^2 - \sum_{i \in \mathcal{I}_v}\omega_{v,i}^2 PV_i(\theta)^2}{1 - \sum_{i \in \mathcal{I}_v}\omega_{v,i}^2}
\end{equation}
shows that $U_{v,H}$ drops own-fund diagonal squares directly from fund-level NPVs (unlike L2, $U_{v,H}$ is unrestricted in sign in finite samples, so a negative fitted value does not by itself rule out shared vintage effects).

\begin{theorem}[Finite-pool identification of L2 and $U_{v,H}$]
\label{thm:finite_pool}
Suppose all funds in vintage $v$ share origin $t_v^0$ and evaluation set $\mathcal{T}_{v,H}$, with $\mathcal{W}_v$-measurable fund weights $\omega_{v,i} > 0$ summing to $1$, and assume the moment envelopes of Appendix~\ref{sec:proofs_asymptotics_H} hold (proofs in Online Supplement Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_theory}).
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[\textbf{T1}] \emph{(Exact rescaling identity.)} For every unit $i$ (or vintage portfolio $v$) and horizon $H \ge 0$,
  \[
  \bar\epsilon_{v,H}(\theta) = A_{v,H}(\theta)\,PV_v(\theta),
  \qquad
  A_{v,H}(\theta) := \frac{1}{|\mathcal{T}_{v,H}|}\sum_{\tau \in \mathcal{T}_{v,H}} \frac{\Psi_{t_v^0}(\theta)}{\Psi_\tau(\theta)}.
  \]
  \item[\textbf{T2}] \emph{(Mean preservation under (X) vs.\ compounding wedge under (P) alone.)} Under~(X), $\mathbb{E}[\bar\epsilon_{v,H}(\theta_0) \mid \mathcal{G}_v] = 0$ and $\mathbb{E}[\bar\epsilon_{v,H}(\theta_0)] = 0$ for all $H \ge 0$. Under~(P) alone, however, $\mathbb{E}[\bar\epsilon_{v,H}(\theta_0) \mid \mathcal{F}_{t_v^0}] = \mathrm{Cov}(A_{v,H}(\theta_0),\,PV_v(\theta_0) \mid \mathcal{F}_{t_v^0}) =: b_{v,H}$, retaining a first-moment compounding wedge whenever pricing errors covary with post-inception factor realizations.
  \item[\textbf{T3}] \emph{(Inception L2 variance tilt.)} Under~(P) with $\mathcal{F}_{t_v^0}$-measurable weights, at $H = 0$,
  \[
  \nabla_\theta\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[PV_v(\theta)^2\right]\Big|_{\theta=\theta_0}
  = \nabla_\theta\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[\operatorname{Var}\!\left(PV_v(\theta) \,\middle|\, \mathcal{F}_{t_v^0}\right)\right]\Big|_{\theta=\theta_0}
  = 2\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[PV_v(\theta_0)\,\nabla_\theta PV_v(\theta_0)\right],
  \]
  which is generically nonzero whenever $\operatorname{Var}(PV_v(\theta)\mid \mathcal{F}_{t_v^0})$ varies with $\theta$.
  \item[\textbf{T4}] \emph{(Finite-$n_v$ L2 decomposition.)} Under~(X) with conditionally independent funds of common conditional variance $\sigma_v^2(\theta) := \operatorname{Var}(PV_i(\theta)\mid \mathcal{G}_v)$ and equal weights $\omega_{v,i} = 1/n_v$ (replace $n_v^{-1}$ by $\sum_i \omega_{v,i}^2$ under general weights),
  \[
  \mathbb{E}\!\left[\bar\epsilon_{v,H}(\theta)^2\right]
  = \mathbb{E}\!\left[A_{v,H}(\theta)^2\,\mu_v(\theta)^2\right]
  + \mathbb{E}\!\left[A_{v,H}(\theta)^2\,n_v^{-1}\,\sigma_v^2(\theta)\right],
  \]
  where $\mu_v(\theta) := \mathbb{E}[PV_i(\theta)\mid \mathcal{G}_v]$ vanishes at $\theta_0$.
  \item[\textbf{T5}] \emph{(Horizon gradient decomposition.)} At any $\theta \in \Theta$,
  \[
  \nabla_\theta\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[\bar\epsilon_{v,H}(\theta)^2\right]
  = \underbrace{2\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[A_{v,H}(\theta)^2\,PV_v(\theta)\,\nabla_\theta PV_v(\theta)\right]}_{\text{reweighted inception score (Fix-}A\text{)}}
  + \underbrace{2\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[A_{v,H}(\theta)\,\nabla_\theta A_{v,H}(\theta)\,PV_v(\theta)^2\right]}_{\text{Scale-Jacobian pull}}.
  \]
  \item[\textbf{T6}] \emph{(Horizon displays the tilt; it does not remove it.)} Under the conditions of T4,
  \[
  \nabla_\theta\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[\bar\epsilon_{v,H}(\theta)^2\right]\Big|_{\theta=\theta_0}
  = \mathbb{E}\!\left[n_v^{-1}A_{v,H}(\theta_0)^2\left(\nabla_\theta\sigma_v^2(\theta_0) + 2\,\sigma_v^2(\theta_0)\,\nabla_\theta\log A_{v,H}(\theta_0)\right)\right].
  \]
  Varying $H$ traces a DGP-dependent target curve $\theta_H^\dagger$; any horizon achieving $\theta_H^\dagger = \theta_0$ reflects cancellation of the variance slope and Scale-Jacobian pull rather than elimination of finite-pool noise.
  \item[\textbf{T7}] \emph{(Identification of $U_{v,H}$.)} Suppose~(X) holds and funds $i \neq j \in \mathcal{I}_v$ are conditionally independent given $\mathcal{G}_v$ with no shared vintage shock ($c_v = 0$), so $\mathbb{E}[PV_i(\theta)PV_j(\theta)\mid \mathcal{G}_v] = \mu_{v,i}(\theta)\mu_{v,j}(\theta)$ with $\mu_{v,i}(\theta_0)=0$. Then for any $\mathcal{W}_v$-measurable weights $q_v > 0$,
  \[
  \mathbb{E}\!\left[q_v\,U_{v,H}(\theta)\right] = \mathbb{E}\!\left[q_v\,A_{v,H}(\theta)^2\,\frac{\sum_{i \neq j}\omega_{v,i}\omega_{v,j}\,\mu_{v,i}(\theta)\mu_{v,j}(\theta)}{1 - \sum_i \omega_{v,i}^2}\right],
  \]
  which vanishes at $\theta_0$ for all $H \ge 0$. Under a common conditional mean $\mu_{v,i}(\theta) = \mu_v(\theta)$, it reduces to $\mathbb{E}[q_v A_{v,H}(\theta)^2 \mu_v(\theta)^2] \ge 0$, so $\theta_0$ is a global minimizer (with size-dependent means, global identification must be assumed). Under the conditions of Section~\ref{sec:asymptotic_framework}, $\hat\theta_H^U := \arg\min_{\theta\in\Theta}\widehat{U}_H(\theta) \xrightarrow{p} \theta_0$ for every fixed $H \ge 0$ with bounded $n_v \ge 2$.
  \item[\textbf{T8}] \emph{(Shared vintage shocks, $c_v \neq 0$.)} Under a common conditional mean $\mu_v(\theta)$ and pairwise conditional covariance $\operatorname{Cov}(PV_i(\theta),PV_j(\theta)\mid\mathcal{G}_v) = c_v(\theta) \ge 0$ ($i \neq j$),
  \[
  \mathbb{E}\!\left[q_v\,U_{v,H}(\theta)\right] = \mathbb{E}\!\left[q_v\,A_{v,H}(\theta)^2\left(\mu_v(\theta)^2 + c_v(\theta)\right)\right].
  \]
  Thus $U_{v,H}$ eliminates all idiosyncratic $O(1/n_v)$ noise while retaining a shared-shock tilt with the same structure as T6 ($n_v^{-1}\sigma_v^2$ replaced by $c_v$), including potential exploding-$\alpha$ boundary hits at $H=0$ because $U_{v,0}$ is non-coercive in $\alpha$ (Online Supplement Lemma~\ref{lem:coercivity} and Figure~\ref{fig:exploding_alpha}).
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{Admissible parameter domain $\Theta$.}
Because $M_u(\theta) = G_u(\theta)^{-1}$ requires strictly positive gross benchmark returns at every month $u$ spanned by sample cash flows, estimation minimizes over the compact domain
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:admissible_domain}
\Theta := \left\{\theta = (\alpha,\beta) :\; \alpha \in [-0.02, 0.02],\ \beta_{\mathrm{MKT}} \in [-1, 4],\ \min_{u \in \mathcal{U}} G_u(\theta) \ge \underline{G}\right\},
\end{equation}
with gross-return floor $\underline{G} = 0.08$ and box bounds preventing the exploding-$\alpha$ boundary degeneracy \citep[Footnote~8]{DLP12} illustrated in Online Supplement Figure~\ref{fig:exploding_alpha} (second-factor loadings are bounded implicitly on both sides by $\min_u G_u(\theta) \ge \underline{G}$).
Whenever a minimizer lies on an active boundary of $\Theta$, interior sandwich standard errors are invalid and suppressed (marked with $^{\dagger}$).

\subsection{Optimal horizon theory and cross-vintage dependence}
\label{sec:optimal_H_theory}

Whenever a squared-error or pair-product criterion retains nonzero effective noise variance---$c_v(\theta) + (\sum_i \omega_{v,i}^2)\sigma_{\nu,v}^2(\theta)$ for L2 VYP, or $c_v(\theta)$ alone for $U_{v,H}$---horizon averaging pits backward discounting of random future payoffs against forward compounding through $A_{v,H}(\theta)^2$.
We formalize this trade-off across three progressively richer dependence regimes: (1)~i.i.d.\ fund noise ($c_v = 0$, $c_{v,w} = 0$), (2)~independent shared vintage shocks ($c_v > 0$, $c_{v,w} = 0$ for $v \neq w$), and (3)~nearby cross-vintage dependence ($c_{v,w} \neq 0$ for $0 < |y_v - y_w| \le L$), generated either by persistent AR(1) cohort shocks $W_v$ or by an overlapping calendar-time private-market shock $e_u$ accumulated over active deal lives.

Under Condition~(X), decompose fund inception NPVs as
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:three_regime_decomp}
PV_i(\theta) = \mu_v(\theta) + \eta_v(\theta) + \nu_i(\theta),
\qquad i \in \mathcal{I}_v,
\end{equation}
where $\nu_i(\theta)$ is fund-idiosyncratic with conditional variance $\sigma_{\nu,v}^2(\theta)$ given $\mathcal{G}_v$ and $\eta_v(\theta)$ is a zero-mean cohort/calendar shock with conditional covariance $c_{v,w}(\theta) := \operatorname{Cov}(\eta_v(\theta), \eta_w(\theta) \mid \mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{pub}}, \mathcal{W})$ and $c_v(\theta) := c_{v,v}(\theta)$.
Writing $\mathcal{L}_{v,H}(\theta)$ for the vintage-$v$ criterion contribution ($q_v \bar\epsilon_{v,H}(\theta)^2$ for L2; $q_v U_{v,H}(\theta)$ for pair products) and $Q_H(\theta) := \lim_{V\to\infty} V^{-1}\sum_{v=1}^V \mathbb{E}[\mathcal{L}_{v,H}(\theta)]$, both estimators share $\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{L}_{v,H}(\theta) \mid \mathcal{G}_v] = q_v A_{v,H}(\theta)^2(\mu_v(\theta)^2 + \sigma_{\mathrm{eff},v}^2(\theta))$.
With cash-flow innovations $e_{i,t} := CF_{i,t} - \mathbb{E}[CF_{i,t}\mid \mathcal{G}_v]$, discount factors $D_{v,t}(\theta) := R^b_{t_v^0,t}(\theta)^{-1}$, effective covariance matrix $\Sigma_v^{(s,t)}$ ($\operatorname{Cov}(e_{v,s}, e_{v,t}\mid\mathcal{G}_v)$ for L2; $\operatorname{Cov}(e_{i,s}, e_{j,t}\mid\mathcal{G}_v)$, $i \neq j$, for $U_{v,H}$), and cumulative log-return sensitivity $S_{v,t}(\theta) := \nabla_\theta \log R^b_{t_v^0,t}(\theta) = \sum_{u=t_v^0+1}^t G_u(\theta)^{-1}\nabla_\theta G_u(\theta)$, we obtain the exact optimal-horizon characterization.

\begin{proposition}[Optimal horizon theory and cross-vintage dependence]
\label{prop:optimal_H}
Suppose Condition~(X) and Assumptions~\ref{ass:asymptotics_H}(A1)--(A5) of Section~\ref{sec:asymptotic_framework} hold (proofs in Online Supplement Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_optimal_H}).
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[\textbf{(i)}] \emph{(Multi-deal duration matching and global bounds.)}
  Whenever $\sigma_{\mathrm{eff},v}^2(\theta_0) > 0$, the population score at $\theta_0$ equals
  \begin{equation}
  \label{eq:duration_matching_score}
  \nabla_\theta Q_H(\theta_0)
  = 2 \lim_{V\to\infty}\frac{1}{V}\sum_{v=1}^V \mathbb{E}\!\left[q_v A_{v,H}(\theta_0)^2\,\sigma_{\mathrm{eff},v}^2(\theta_0)\,\Big(\bar{S}_{v,H}^{\mathrm{comp}}(\theta_0) - \bar{S}_v^{\mathrm{eff}}(\theta_0)\Big)\right],
  \end{equation}
  where
  \[
  \bar{S}_{v,H}^{\mathrm{comp}}(\theta_0) := \frac{\sum_{\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{v,H}} R^b_{t_v^0,\tau}(\theta_0)\,S_{v,\tau}(\theta_0)}{\sum_{\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{v,H}} R^b_{t_v^0,\tau}(\theta_0)},
  \qquad
  \bar{S}_v^{\mathrm{eff}}(\theta_0) := \frac{\sum_{s,t\ge t_v^0} D_{v,s}(\theta_0)D_{v,t}(\theta_0)\,\Sigma_v^{(s,t)}\,S_{v,t}(\theta_0)}{\sum_{s,t\ge t_v^0} D_{v,s}(\theta_0)D_{v,t}(\theta_0)\,\Sigma_v^{(s,t)}}.
  \]
  For scalar $\theta$ ($\alpha$, or $\beta$ under constant excess return) with constant gross return $G = e^r$ ($r \ge 0$), \eqref{eq:duration_matching_score} vanishes at a \emph{unique} zero-bias horizon $H_0^\ast \in (0,\infty)$; averaging over continuous $\tau \in [0,H]$, it solves
  \begin{equation}
  \label{eq:exact_tau_bar}
  \bar\tau(H_0^\ast;\, r) := H_0^\ast - \frac{1}{r} + \frac{H_0^\ast}{e^{r H_0^\ast} - 1} = T_{\mathrm{eff}}
  \qquad\bigl(\bar\tau(H;0) = H/2 \implies H_0^\ast = 2\,T_{\mathrm{eff}}\bigr),
  \end{equation}
  where $T_{\mathrm{eff}}$ is the covariance-weighted payoff exit date. On the monthly grid $\mathcal{T}_{v,H}$ the integrals become sums; over the grid of Online Supplement Table~\ref{tab:H_star_lookup}, the monthly root lies within half a month of the continuous one. For $r > 0$, $H_0^\ast$ is strictly decreasing in $r$ and satisfies
  \begin{equation}
  \label{eq:H_star_bounds}
  T_{\mathrm{eff}} \;<\; H_0^\ast \;<\; \min\!\left(2\,T_{\mathrm{eff}},\; T_{\mathrm{eff}} + \frac{1}{r}\right).
  \end{equation}
  Under stochastic i.i.d.\ lognormal returns $r_u \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_r,\sigma_r^2)$ with arithmetic drift $r_{\mathrm{arith}} = \mu_r + \frac{1}{2}\sigma_r^2$, squaring $A_{v,H}$ adds a volatility-compounding tilt $\exp(\sigma_r^2 \min(\tau_1,\tau_2))$ that shrinks $H_0^\ast$ strictly below the deterministic root of \eqref{eq:exact_tau_bar} at $r = r_{\mathrm{arith}}$.

  \item[\textbf{(ii)}] \emph{(Cross-vintage dependence: $O(1)$ invariance, calendar-shock shift, and $O(1/V)$ amplification.)}
  Under local cross-vintage dependence ($c_{v,w} \neq 0$ for $0 < |y_v - y_w| \le L$):
  (a)~within-vintage $U_{v,H}$ still eliminates $\sigma_{\nu,v}^2/n_v$ identically and off-diagonal $c_{v,w}$ ($v \neq w$) do not enter $Q_H(\theta)$ or $H_0^\ast$, though positive semidefiniteness forces $c_v > 0$ so $U_{v,0}$ retains a tilt;
  (b)~$H_0^\ast$ is invariant to stationary AR(1) cohort persistence $\rho_W$ at fixed $c_v$, whereas an overlapping calendar-time shock $e_u$ weights deal pairs by active-life overlap $|[a_1,b_1]\cap[a_2,b_2]|$, placing greater weight on late exits and strictly lengthening $T_{\mathrm{eff}}$ and $H_0^\ast$;
  (c)~off-diagonal $c_{v,w} > 0$ inflates both the long-run score variance $\Lambda_H = \Lambda_H^{\mathrm{indep}} + \Lambda_H^{\mathrm{cross}}$ and the $O(1/V)$ finite-sample M-estimator bias, even though quadratic-shock score covariances ($\propto c_{v,w}^2$) vanish near $H_0^\ast$.

  \item[\textbf{(iii)}] \emph{(MSE-optimal horizon and multi-parameter trade-off.)}
  In dimension $p \ge 2$, a single scalar $H$ cannot generically zero all $p$ coordinates of $\nabla_\theta Q_H(\theta_0)$ simultaneously. For $\mathrm{MSE}_V(H) := \|B_0(H)\|^2 + V^{-1}\mathrm{tr}(\mathcal{H}_H^{-1}\Lambda_H\mathcal{H}_H^{-1})$ with $B_0(H) := \theta_H^\dagger - \theta_0$: when $\sigma_{\mathrm{eff},v}^2 \equiv 0$ ($U_{v,H}$, $c_v = 0$) and homoscedastic $q_v = 1$, $H_{\mathrm{MSE}}^\ast(V) = 0$ for every $V$; when $\sigma_{\mathrm{eff},v}^2 > 0$ for scalar $\theta$ with $\mathcal{V}'(H_0^\ast) > 0$,
  \begin{equation}
  \label{eq:H_mse_expansion}
  H_{\mathrm{MSE}}^\ast(V) = H_0^\ast - \frac{\mathcal{V}'(H_0^\ast)}{2\,V\,[B_0'(H_0^\ast)]^2} + O(V^{-2}) \;<\; H_0^\ast,
  \qquad \lim_{V\to\infty} H_{\mathrm{MSE}}^\ast(V) = H_0^\ast,
  \end{equation}
  where positive cross-vintage dependence shifts $H_{\mathrm{MSE}}^\ast(V)$ further inward whenever the cross-vintage variance derivative $(\mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{cross}})'(H_0^\ast)$ is positive (whereas when $H=0$ is non-coercive in $\alpha$, moderate $H \approx H_0^\ast$ cuts both bias and variance).

  \item[\textbf{(iv)}] \emph{(Rank-1 collapse and indefiniteness of cross-vintage pair products.)}
  The leave-band-out cross-vintage criterion $\widetilde{U}_{H,m}^{\mathrm{cross}}(\theta) := |\mathcal{P}_m|^{-1}\sum_{|y_v-y_w|>m}\bar\epsilon_{v,H}(\theta)\bar\epsilon_{w,H}(\theta)$ removes $c_v$ and nearby $c_{v,w}$ ($|y_v-y_w|\le m$) in expectation, but as $V\to\infty$ with fixed $m$ it converges uniformly to $\big(\mathbb{E}[\bar\epsilon_{v,H}(\theta)]\big)^2$, whose Hessian has rank~$1$ (destroying $p \ge 2$ identification and restoring multiple roots for scalar $\beta$; Online Supplement Lemma~\ref{lem:multiple_roots}). Moreover, for finite $V$ and any $m \ge 0$, the adjacency matrix $M_m = [\mathbf{1}_{|y_v-y_w|>m}]$ has zero trace and strictly negative eigenvalues (e.g., at $V=25, m=7$, $\lambda_{\min} = -9.51$ vs.\ $\lambda_{\max} = 12.62$), so $\widetilde{U}_{H,m}^{\mathrm{cross}}$ is an indefinite quadratic form in the vintage pricing errors. Indefiniteness alone does not place the minimizer on $\partial\Theta$; in the Monte Carlo it lies there in $42\%$--$100\%$ of draws (Online Supplement Table~\ref{tab:mc_regimes_verification}, Panel~C).
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

Figure~\ref{fig:optimal_H_theory} and Table~\ref{tab:regime_summary} summarize Proposition~\ref{prop:optimal_H}.
In our core Preqin deal calibration (5-year investment window, 0.25--11.5-year holding lives, historical annualized benchmark return $g = 13.3\%$, volatility $\sigma_m = 15.4\%$), $H_0^\ast$ falls from $2T_{\mathrm{eff}} \approx 16\text{--}20$~years under flat returns to $12.65$~years under a static vintage shock $W_v$ ($T_{\mathrm{eff}} = 8.09$~yr) and $14.78\text{--}15.02$~years under an overlapping calendar-time shock $e_u$ or idiosyncratic L2 deal noise ($T_{\mathrm{eff}} = 9.76\text{--}9.96$~yr; Online Supplement Tables~\ref{tab:H_star_lookup}--\ref{tab:mc_regimes_verification}).
Thus within-vintage $U_{v,H}$ paired with moderate horizon averaging ($H \approx 12.5\text{--}15$~yr) remains effective under nearby cross-vintage dependence, whereas cross-vintage pair products fail structurally.

\subsection{Identification-robust inception moment diagnostic}
\label{sec:ar_diagnostic}

Unlike quadratic criteria, the linear inception pricing moment
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:iv_moment}
\bar{g}_H(\theta) := \frac{1}{V}\sum_{v=1}^{V} z_{v,H}\,PV_v(\theta),
\qquad
z_{v,H} := \frac{A_{v,H}(1)}{A_{v,0}(1)},
\end{equation}
reduces at $H = 0$ ($z_{v,0} \equiv 1$) to $V^{-1}\sum_v PV_v(\theta)$, which has exact mean zero under Condition~(P) alone---even when $c_v \neq 0$ or strict exogeneity~(X) fails.
Solving $\bar{g}_0(\beta) = 0$ for a point estimate is fragile, however: the nonlinear pricing moment can admit multiple roots on $\Theta$ (including the public growth-optimal exposure; Online Supplement Lemma~\ref{lem:multiple_roots}), can lose all roots in finite samples, and faces boundary/collinearity subtleties in two-parameter $(\alpha,\beta)$ systems (Online Supplement Remark~\ref{rem:iv_alpha}).

To bypass root selection and weak-slope division, we invert the centered Bartlett fixed-$b$ moment test \citep{AR49,SW00,KV05} across all $\beta \in \Theta$: with $s_v(\beta) = z_{v,H} PV_v(\beta)$ and centered long-run variance $\hat\Omega_g(\beta) = V^{-1}\sum_{v,w} K(|y_v - y_w|/S)\,(s_v(\beta)-\bar{g}_H(\beta))(s_w(\beta)-\bar{g}_H(\beta))$, collect all $\beta \in \Theta$ satisfying
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ar_set}
\left|\frac{\sqrt{V}\,\bar{g}_H(\beta)}{\sqrt{\hat\Omega_g(\beta)}}\right| \le c_{0.05}^{\mathrm{fb}}(b),
\qquad
b = \frac{S}{V}.
\end{equation}
While \eqref{eq:ar_set} is fully robust to multiple roots, zero-root samples, and near-zero slopes, its finite-$V$ coverage under cross-vintage persistence still reflects the small-$V$ Bartlett approximation (Section~\ref{sec:simulation_study}), so we report AR sets as nominal, calibrated-by-design companions to $U_{v,0}$.

\subsection{Primitive increasing-domain asymptotics and fixed-$b$ inference}
\label{sec:asymptotic_framework}

Let $\mathcal{L}_{v,H}(\theta)$ denote the vintage-$v$ criterion contribution ($\bar\epsilon_{v,H}(\theta)^2$ for L2 VYP; $q_v U_{v,H}(\theta)$ for pair products over $n_v \ge 2$) on strictly increasing calendar years $y_1 < \dots < y_V$ in $\mathbb{Z}$, and let $\theta_H^\dagger := \arg\min_{\theta \in \Theta} \lim_{V\to\infty} V^{-1}\sum_{v=1}^V \mathbb{E}[\mathcal{L}_{v,H}(\theta)]$.
Because fund overlap across vintages arises strictly along one-dimensional calendar time (rather than a two-dimensional spatial random field), increasing-domain asymptotics ($V \to \infty$ with bounded $n_v$) follow directly from five primitive DGP assumptions stated formally in Online Supplement Appendix~\ref{sec:primitive_assumptions} (Assumption~\ref{ass:asymptotics_H}, A1--A5):
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[\textbf{A1}] \emph{Compact positive-return domain and global identification:} $\Theta \subset \mathbb{R}^p$ compact and convex, factor returns with bounded support such that $\inf_{u,\theta\in\Theta} G_u(\theta) \ge \underline{G} > 0$ a.s., and unique population minimizer $\theta_H^\dagger \in \Theta$.
  \item[\textbf{A2}] \emph{Bounded lifetimes and pool structure:} bounded active fund lives $t - t_v^0 \le L_{\max} < \infty$, bounded fund counts $n_v \le \bar{n}$ ($n_v \ge 2$ for $U_{v,H}$), bounded vintage weights $0 < \underline{q} \le q_v \le \bar{q}$, non-degenerate off-diagonal denominator $\inf_v(1 - \sum_i \omega_{v,i}^2) \ge \underline{d} > 0$, and bounded calendar spacing $\sup_v(y_{v+1}-y_v) \le \bar{\Delta}$.
  \item[\textbf{A3}] \emph{1D calendar-time weak dependence:} the annual calendar process $Z_y$ collecting monthly public factors $F_u$, calendar shocks $e_u$, and cohort shocks $\eta_y$ is $\alpha$-mixing along $\mathbb{Z}$ with mixing size $-\lambda < -(2+\delta)/\delta$ ($\delta > 0$), while fund-idiosyncratic shocks $\nu_{v,i}$ are generated from vintage $v$'s local calendar block and mutually independent innovations independent of $\{Z_y\}$.
  \item[\textbf{A4}] \emph{Primitive moment envelopes:} uniform $(16 + 8\delta)$-th moments on single-vintage compounded gross return/discount envelopes $M_{v,H}^{(R)}$, factor norms $M_v^{(F)}$, and gross cash flows $M_v^{(CF)}$ over $L_H := \max(L_{\max}, 12H)$ months.
  \item[\textbf{A5}] \emph{Interior curvature and partial-sum homogeneity:} $\theta_H^\dagger \in \mathrm{int}(\Theta)$, positive definite limiting Hessian $\mathcal{H}_H$ and long-run score covariance $\Lambda_H$, homogeneous partial-sum variance/Hessian limits for fixed-$b$ FCLTs, and sparse empty calendar years $(y_V - y_1 + 1 - V)/V \to 0$.
\end{enumerate}

Online Supplement Lemma~\ref{lem:mixing_moment_inheritance} verifies explicitly from A1--A4 that because each vintage depends only on a finite calendar block of at most $m_H = \lceil L_H/12 \rceil + 1$ years, $(\mathcal{L}_{v,H}, s_{v,H}, \nabla_{\theta\theta}^2 \mathcal{L}_{v,H})$ inherit 1D $\alpha$-mixing with coefficients $\tilde\alpha(k) \le \alpha_Z(\max(k - m_H, 0))$, smooth uniform $L_{2+\delta}$ envelopes and $L_{1+\delta/2}$ Lipschitz bounds via the Leibniz rule and H\"older's inequality, and stochastic equicontinuity / uniform laws of large numbers \citep{Andrews92,Davidson94,PP97}.
Notice also how A4 foreshadows our finite-sample simulation diagnostics: because $\mathcal{L}_{v,H}$ is a fourth-order nonlinear product of compounded returns and cash flows, controlling score moments requires high finite return/payoff moments whose finite-sample kurtosis grows sharply with lifetime and horizon $H$.

\begin{proposition}[Consistency and asymptotic normality]
\label{prop:consistency_clt}
Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:asymptotics_H}(A1)--(A4), $\hat\theta_H \xrightarrow{p} \theta_H^\dagger$ as $V \to \infty$ (with $\theta_H^\dagger = \theta_0$ for $U_{v,H}$ under Theorem~\ref{thm:finite_pool}(T7)). If Assumption~\ref{ass:asymptotics_H}(A5) also holds with $\mathbb{E}[s_{v,H}(\theta_H^\dagger)] = 0$ for every $v$, then
\[
\sqrt{V}\left(\hat\theta_H - \theta_H^\dagger\right) \xrightarrow{d} \mathcal{N}\!\left(0, \mathcal{H}_H^{-1}\Lambda_H\mathcal{H}_H^{-1}\right).
\]
\end{proposition}

The per-vintage centering condition holds for $U_{v,H}$ at $\theta_0$ under the conditions of Theorem~\ref{thm:finite_pool}(T7) for any profile of pool sizes, because every vintage's expected criterion is stationary at $\theta_0$. It fails for L2 when $n_v$ varies across vintages, because the variance tilt of Theorem~\ref{thm:finite_pool}(T3--T6) moves each vintage's own minimizer (Online Supplement Remark~\ref{rem:per_vintage_centering}); we therefore attach fixed-$b$ significance markers only to $U_{v,H}$.

For finite-$V$ inference, let $\tilde{s}_{v,H} := s_{v,H}(\hat\theta_H) - \bar{s}_H(\hat\theta_H)$ be the centered vintage score and form the 1D calendar-distance Bartlett long-run variance estimator \citep{C99,KS11,LLSW18}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:shac_H}
\hat\Lambda_H(b) = \frac{1}{V}\sum_{v=1}^V\sum_{w=1}^V K\!\left(\frac{|y_v - y_w|}{S}\right)\tilde{s}_{v,H}\tilde{s}_{w,H}^\top,
\qquad
K(x) = \max(1 - |x|, 0),
\qquad
S = \left\lceil 1.3\sqrt{V} \right\rceil,
\end{equation}
with sandwich matrix $\widehat{\mathrm{Avar}}(\hat\theta_H) = V^{-1}\hat{\mathcal{H}}_H^{-1}\hat\Lambda_H(b)\hat{\mathcal{H}}_H^{-1}$ and bandwidth ratio $b = S/V$.
Because $b \approx 0.26$--$0.30$ in our $V = 23$--$27$ panels, normal critical values ($1.96$) severely over-reject ($13.3\%$--$14.1\%$ even on independent Gaussian draws; Online Supplement Table~\ref{tab:mc_size}).
Holding $b \in (0,1]$ fixed yields the \citet{KV05} reference distribution (proof in Online Supplement Appendix~\ref{sec:proofs_prop3_cor1}). That limit is pivotal only under the homogeneous partial sums of A5, which our pools violate: $n_v$ rises from 2 in the 1980s to 41 (Buyout) and 56 (VC) in the 2000s. We therefore use the \citet{KV05} critical values as an approximation and check their size by design: in the core Monte Carlo designs, which reproduce the observed vintage calendars and fund counts, $U_{v,0}$ rejects a true $\beta$ in $4.6\%$ (Buyout) and $6.4\%$ (VC) of draws (Table~\ref{tab:mc_core}).

\begin{corollary}[One-dimensional fixed-$b$ Wald/$t$ and identification-robust AR asymptotics]
\label{cor:fixed_b_ar}
Fix $b \in (0,1]$ with $S = \lfloor b V \rfloor$, and let $t_{\mathrm{KV}}(b) := W_1(1)/\sqrt{\Xi_1(b)}$ denote the pivotal \citet{KV05} Bartlett fixed-$b$ distribution (where $\Xi_k(b)$ is the Bartlett functional of a $k$-dimensional Brownian bridge $B_k$).
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[\textbf{(i)}] Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:asymptotics_H}(A1)--(A5) with $\mathbb{E}[s_{v,H}(\theta_H^\dagger)] = 0$ for every $v$, $\hat\Lambda_H(b) \Rightarrow \Lambda_H^{1/2}\Xi_p(b)\Lambda_H^{1/2}$ as $V \to \infty$, so any scalar contrast $t$-statistic for $H_0: a^\top\theta_H^\dagger = r_0$ converges to $t_{\mathrm{KV}}(b)$ (and a $q$-restriction Wald statistic converges to $W_q(1)^\top \Xi_q(b)^{-1} W_q(1)$).
  \item[\textbf{(ii)}] Under Condition~(P) at $H=0$ (or Condition~(X) at $H>0$) together with Assumptions~A2--A3, $L_{2+\delta}$ integrability, homogeneous long-run variance $\Omega_g > 0$, and sparse gaps, the centered AR statistic in \eqref{eq:ar_set} satisfies $\sqrt{V}\bar{g}_H(\theta_0)/\sqrt{\hat\Omega_g(\theta_0;b)} \Rightarrow t_{\mathrm{KV}}(b)$ without requiring unique roots, interior optimization, or a nonzero moment Jacobian \citep{AR49,SW00}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

In practice, we evaluate all $p$-values and 95\% intervals using $c_{0.05}^{\mathrm{fb}}(b)$ at $b = \lceil 1.3\sqrt{V}\rceil / V$ ($2.790$ for $V=25$; $2.867$ for $V=23$; $2.722$ for $V=27$).
Online Supplement Remark~\ref{rem:calendar_vs_index_shac} shows that index-lag weighting $K(|v - w|/S)$ changes Buyout $U_{v,0}$ SEs by at most $0.003$ and lowers VC $U_{v,0}$ SEs by $0.031$: calendar-distance weighting is slightly more conservative, no $H=0$ test decision at the 5\% level changes, and five of 80 $p$-values cross a conventional threshold.

\subsection{Relation to benchmark-deflator and public-SDF paradigms}
\label{sec:comparison_to_similar_estimators}

Within the cash-flow-identified reciprocal benchmark-deflator paradigm, L2 VYP at $H = 0$ nests \citet{DLP12} exactly, while Theorem~\ref{thm:finite_pool} explains why their portfolio-infill consistency ($n_v \to \infty$ inside fixed vintages; Online Supplement Lemma~\ref{lem:infill}) fails at finite $n_v$ and how $U_{v,H}$ restores identification under increasing-$V$ asymptotics.
Relative to \citet{DLP09wp}, who stabilize inception NPV via log-ratio or PME transforms after portfolio averaging, horizon averaging acts directly on net cash flows without requiring separate investment/dividend labeling while revealing why horizon shifts cannot remove squared-loss bias in general.

By contrast, \citet{KN16}---together with public-kernel and cash-flow-strip approaches such as \citet{GSW20} and \citet{GN18}---occupy a distinct paradigm: they calibrate an economy-wide SDF strictly to traded public-equity and Treasury moments (assigning zero weight to PE cash flows during estimation) and evaluate PE cash flows ex post to obtain a Generalized PME readout.
\citet{KN24} emphasize this distinction and bridge the two paradigms under lognormal return structure by using an asset-specific benchmark beta to remove realized factor-path noise while anchoring abnormal performance to a public-calibrated SDF (Online Supplement Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_sdf} and Table~\ref{tab:comparison}).
Because public-calibrated SDF procedures and cash-flow-identified benchmark deflators estimate different population objects from disjoint moments, our Monte Carlo study below benchmarks estimators targeting the \emph{same} cash-flow-identified parameter $\theta_0$: individual-fund L2, pooled L2 VYP (\citealp{DLP12} at $H=0$), cross-fund $U_{v,H}$, inception linear IV/AR sets, and leave-band-out cross-vintage pair products $\widetilde{U}_{H,m}^{\mathrm{cross}}$.

\subsection{Calibrated cash-flow DGP and design architecture}
\label{sec:mc_design}

We evaluate estimator bias, precision, horizon dynamics, and fixed-$b$ test calibration across 72 data-shaped Monte Carlo designs (1,456 summary rows; 250--1,000 replications per design, 1,000 in the two cores and fund-size Design~B, 500 in the path-shock, growing-$V$, and Optimal-$H$ Regime~1--3b suites) using common deal draws across estimators and horizons.
In the two \emph{core designs}, we match the exact North American Preqin Buyout (25 vintages, 383 funds) and Venture Capital (27 vintages, 432 funds) calendars and fund counts through 2010, the historical U.S.\ $q^5$ factor path, 15 deals per fund with quarterly cash flows (5-year investment window, holding lives up to 11.5 years), and exact pricing at $\beta_{\mathrm{MKT}} = 1$ ($\alpha = 0$) with mean-one multiplicative lognormal deal shocks calibrated to match empirical within-vintage cross-sectional dispersion (monthly $\sigma = 0.130$ Buyout, $0.154$ VC).
Because deal shocks are multiplicative and mean-one, both Condition~(P) and Condition~(X) hold exactly in the cores (unlike additive shocks with default truncation; Online Supplement Appendix~\ref{sec:dlp_simulation}).

\subsection{Single-factor core results, bias decomposition, and design margins}
\label{sec:mc_core}

Table~\ref{tab:mc_core} and Figure~\ref{fig:mc_decomposition} present the core single-factor results and yield four main conclusions:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{Individual-fund L2 is severely biased across horizons} ($+0.249$ at $H=0$; $-0.190$ BO / $-0.370$ VC at $H=30$), rejecting $\beta=1$ in up to $79.6\%$ of draws.
  \item \emph{Vintage-portfolio L2 reduces but does not eliminate finite-pool bias.} At $H=0$, L2 VYP is biased upward by $+0.075$ (BO) and $+0.177$ (VC), crosses near zero at 15 years ($-0.007$ / $-0.028$), and drops to $-0.111$ (BO) and $-0.375$ (VC) at 30 years. Figure~\ref{fig:mc_decomposition} confirms that almost this entire horizon slope stems from the population target shift $\theta_H^\dagger - \theta_0$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:finite_pool}(T3--T6)); L2 VYP rejects $\beta=1$ in only $4.1\%/6.3\%$ of draws at $H=0$ because positive target shift and negative finite-sample bias partially offset, whereas against its own target $\theta_0^\dagger$ it rejects in $13.1\%$ (BO) and $34.9\%$ (VC) of draws (Online Supplement Table~\ref{tab:mc_hardening}).
  \item \emph{Equal-vintage $U_{v,H}$ ($q_v=1$) eliminates finite-pool bias at every horizon and minimizes RMSE at $H = 0$.} At $H=0$, $U_{v,0}$ has near-zero bias ($-0.007$ BO, $-0.017$ VC), the lowest RMSE among estimators with near-nominal tests ($0.084$ BO, $0.107$ VC---less than half L2 VYP's $0.296$ in VC), and near-nominal fixed-$b$ rejection ($4.6\%$ BO, $6.4\%$ VC). Extending $H$ leaves $U_{v,H}$ nearly unbiased while monotonically inflating SD ($0.084 \to 0.186$ BO; $0.106 \to 0.273$ VC).
  \item \emph{Fund-count vintage weighting ($q_v = n_v$) ruins fixed-$b$ size.} Because late 2005--2008 vintages hold far more funds than 1985--1995 vintages, $q_v = n_v$ concentrates effective sample size in a handful of overlapping vintages and rejects $\beta=1$ in $20.3\%$ (BO) and $20.0\%$ (VC) of draws at $H=0$, although its RMSE is lower ($0.056$ BO, $0.086$ VC). A leave-one-vintage-out jackknife with $t_{V-1}$ critical values brings the Buyout rejection down to $10.3\%$, still twice nominal (Online Supplement Table~\ref{tab:mc_se_study}). We therefore use $q_v = 1$ for all empirical inference.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Hardening designs and large-$V$ studentization diagnosis}
\label{sec:mc_hardening}

Online Supplement Tables~\ref{tab:mc_size}--\ref{tab:mc_hardening}, \ref{tab:mc_v400_studentization}, and Figure~\ref{fig:mc_growing_v} test harder departures from the core design:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Heterogeneous fund sizes (Design~B, Table~\ref{tab:mc_hardening}, Panel~A):} Calibrated lognormal fund sizes ($\sigma_{\log S} = 1.19$ BO, $0.90$ VC) leave FW $U_{v,0}$ nearly unbiased ($-0.013$ BO, $-0.018$ VC) with mild over-rejection at $H = 0$ ($8.1\%$ BO, $8.5\%$ VC), which we flag as a qualification on FW markers in Table~\ref{tab:u_l2_beta}.
  \item \emph{Path-correlated shocks and AR replay (Designs~A$'$ and~A, Table~\ref{tab:mc_hardening}, Panels~B--C):} When deal payoffs depend on realized factor paths in a way that preserves (P) while violating (X) (Design~A$'$), L2 and $U_{v,H}$ stay biased as $V$ grows from $25$ to $100$ ($+0.15$ to $+0.30$), whereas $H=0$ IV approaches $\beta_0 = 1$. Replaying the centered fixed-$b$ AR test \eqref{eq:ar_set} at $\beta = 1$ yields $4.0\%/3.4\%$ rejection in the independent BO/VC cores, $9.4\%/11.1\%$ under persistent AR(1) M4 shocks ($\rho_W = 0.6$, $c/2c$), and $19.2\%/8.8\%$ ($c$) and $21.8\%/9.8\%$ ($2c$) in Design~A$'$ ($V = 25/100$), underscoring that root robustness and finite-$V$ persistence coverage are distinct properties.
  \item \emph{Large-$V$ studentization and score-concentration diagnosis (Tables~\ref{tab:mc_size_additions} and~\ref{tab:mc_v400_studentization}):} On resampled paths at $V = 400$, equal-vintage $U_{v,H}$ still rejects $\beta_0 = 1$ in $6.8\%$--$10.8\%$ of draws with low effective vintage count ($N_{\mathrm{eff}} = 13$--$27$) and a left-tail excess, even though $\hat\beta - 1$ itself is already near-Gaussian (kurtosis $3.0$--$3.5$) and its SD shrinks at close to the $V^{-1/2}$ rate from $V=100$ on. Our ablation and scaling study through $V = 1{,}600$ (Online Supplement Appendix~\ref{sec:v400_studentization_app}, Table~\ref{tab:mc_v400_studentization}) is consistent with asymptotic validity---by $V = 1{,}600$, $H = 0$ Wald rejection reaches nominal $4.4\%$ ($n_v=20$, $\mathrm{SE}/\mathrm{SD}=1.00$) and $5.0\%$ ($n_v=5$, $\mathrm{SE}/\mathrm{SD}=0.96$)---and isolates two distinct compounding-kurtosis channels explaining slow finite-$V$ studentization convergence:
  (1)~at $H=0$, compounded lognormal deal payoffs ($k s^2 \le 2.31$) generate left-skewed vintage scores (median skewness $-1.81$ to $-3.19$) and positive error--SE correlation ($\mathrm{Corr}(\hat\beta-1, \widehat{\mathrm{SE}}) = +0.28$ to $+0.47$), shrinking $\widehat{\mathrm{SE}}$ in the left tail; replacing historical returns with i.i.d.\ Gaussian returns leaves $N_{\mathrm{eff}}$ unchanged ($28.1$ vs.\ $27.2$), ruling out crash outliers, whereas symmetric deal shocks (an ablation outside Assumption~\ref{ass:deal_level_dgp} that makes $22\%$ of payoffs negative) or low-$\sigma$ lognormal shocks eliminate the correlation and restore nominal $3.2\%$--$6.0\%$ rejection immediately;
  (2)~at $H=15$~yr, compounding scores by $A_{v,15}^2$ inflates fourth-moment score kurtosis through $A_{v,15}^8$ \citep[cf.][]{Martin12}, cutting $N_{\mathrm{eff}}$ in half even under symmetric light-tailed deals ($50.4 \to 19.2$, rejection $3.2\% \to 9.0\%$) until market volatility is reduced ($N_{\mathrm{eff}} = 81.4$, rejection $4.0\%$).
  Evaluating the centered fixed-$b$ score test at the null $\beta_0$ avoids plug-in leverage shrinkage: it rejects $3.6\%$--$4.8\%$ at $H=15$ already at $V=400$ and $4.2\%$--$5.4\%$ at $V=1{,}600$, but still $6.0\%$--$7.8\%$ at $H=0$ for $V=400$--$800$. Practically, this motivates reporting $N_{\mathrm{eff}}$ alongside fixed-$b$ Wald SEs, preferring $H = 0$ when $c_v = 0$, and pairing Wald tests with null-evaluated score or AR diagnostics whenever $N_{\mathrm{eff}}$ is modest.
  \item \emph{Reconciliation with \citet{DLP12} (Online Supplement Appendix~\ref{sec:dlp_simulation}):} Table~\ref{tab:mc_grid} isolates the $n_v \times V$ infill noise effect within our simulator, while Table~\ref{tab:driessen_comparison} documents the remaining scenario differences relative to \citet[Table~1]{DLP12}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Data, constant-composition pooling, and implementation}
\label{sec:empirical_data}

We illustrate the methodology on the Preqin cash-flow dataset (exported April~14, 2022) restricted to North American USD-denominated Buyout (BO) and Venture Capital (VC) funds matched to U.S.\ $q^5$ factors (\url{http://global-q.org/factors.html}; \citealp{HXZ15,hou2021augmented}).
To construct \emph{constant-composition} vintage portfolios free of early-reporting artifacts, our seasoned baseline ($V \le 2010$) retains funds with complete histories (last record $> 5$ years after vintage start or still reporting near March~31, 2022), yielding \textbf{383 Buyout funds across 25 vintages} (1985--2010; 1989 absent, all 25 with $n_v \ge 2$) and \textbf{432 Venture Capital funds across 27 vintages} (1982--2010; 1983--1984 absent; 23 eligible vintages with $n_v \ge 2$ for $U_{v,H}$ after excluding four singleton vintages 1982, 1988, 1989, and 1991).
Cash flows are aligned on a monthly calendar from each vintage's first observed cash flow $t_v^0$, with final reported NAV booked as a terminal distribution for non-liquidated funds.

Online Supplement Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_data_engineering} documents three key data-construction safeguards:
(i)~missing fund sizes ($10/383$ BO, $15/432$ VC) cluster in tiny 1980s--1990s vintages, so naive pooled-mean imputation assigns up to $94.9\%$ (BO) and $98.8\%$ (VC) portfolio weight to single early funds and exaggerates Buyout FW L2's 0--30~yr decline ($0.634$ vs.\ $0.427$); we therefore use \emph{within-vintage median imputation} as baseline (giving $\mathrm{SD}(\log S) = 1.185$ BO / $0.941$ VC, matching Design~B, while leaving Buyout FW $U_{v,0}$ stable across $1.197$--$1.226$ under all three rules; Table~\ref{tab:fw_imputation_sensitivity});
(ii)~across five constant terminal-NAV haircuts from $0\%$ to $100\%$ write-off, inception L2 betas shift smoothly and modestly ($1.283 \to 1.170$ BO EW, $1.224 \to 1.133$ BO FW, $1.925 \to 1.877$ VC EW, $2.287 \to 2.245$ VC FW; Figure~\ref{fig:nav_disc_BOVC_MKT}, Table~\ref{tab:terminal_nav_shares});
and (iii)~single-factor $U_{v,0}$ estimates remain stable across complete histories, all funds (495 BO / 538 VC), and a 20\%-NAV early-stopper filter (328 BO / 344 VC; Table~\ref{tab:robustness_samples}), while rolling 8-fold $hv$-block cross-validation \citep{R00} confirms that held-out $H=0$ loss is minimized or flat at $H=0$ across all four series (Table~\ref{tab:appendix_e_cv_folds}).
We examine six parsimonious reciprocal linear specifications---single-factor \{MKT\} and two-factor \{MKT + $\alpha$, ME, IA, ROE, EG\}---leading with equal-vintage $U_{v,0}$ ($q_v=1$) and using L2 VYP, horizon profiles, $N_{\mathrm{eff}}$, and fixed-$b$ AR sets as structured diagnostics.

\subsection{Single-factor market exposure and AR diagnostics}
\label{sec:empirical_single_bovc}

Figure~\ref{fig:u_beta_profiles} and Table~\ref{tab:u_l2_beta} report single-factor market betas across horizons (Panel~A) alongside inception ($H=0$) estimator gaps, IV root diagnostics, $N_{\mathrm{eff}}(U_{v,0})$, and nominal 95\% AR sets (Panel~B; full 12-horizon and four-horizon tables appear in Online Supplement Tables~\ref{tab:u_l2_beta_all} and~\ref{tab:iv_comparison}).

\paragraph{Buyout.}
At inception ($H = 0$), headline equal-vintage $U_{v,0}$ delivers $\hat\beta_{\mathrm{MKT}} = 1.147$ (SE $0.118$, $N_{\mathrm{eff}} = 5.3$) under EW and $1.197$ (SE $0.143$, $N_{\mathrm{eff}} = 8.9$) under FW.
Both reject $\beta = 0$ ($p < 0.001$; noting Design~B's $8.1\%$ FW finite-sample size), yet neither rejects unit market beta $\beta_{\mathrm{MKT}} = 1$ ($p_1 = 0.325$ EW, $0.280$ FW).
Through $H = 10$ years, $U_{v,H}$ stays remarkably flat ($1.147 \to 1.158$ EW; $1.197 \to 1.213$ FW) before drifting modestly to $1.309$ / $1.428$ at 30 years.
By contrast, squared-loss L2 behaves exactly as Theorem~\ref{thm:finite_pool}(T3--T6) predicts: starting above $U_{v,0}$ ($1.283$ EW, $1.224$ FW) and falling to $1.108$ / $0.989$ at 15 years and $0.933$ / $0.798$ at 30 years.
In the Fix-$A$ decomposition (Online Supplement Table~\ref{tab:fixA_jacobian}), holding $A_{v,H}$ fixed at $\hat\beta_{U,0}$ moves Buyout L2 only to $1.266$ (EW) and $1.106$ (FW) at 30 years, so the Scale-Jacobian term accounts for $-0.334$ of the $-0.351$ EW drop and $-0.308$ of the $-0.427$ FW drop.

\paragraph{Venture Capital.}
Inception $U_{v,0}$ estimates higher systematic risk with wider dispersion: $\hat\beta_{\mathrm{MKT}} = 1.493$ (SE $0.325$, $N_{\mathrm{eff}} = 4.5$) EW and $1.742$ (SE $0.342$, $N_{\mathrm{eff}} = 4.9$) FW, both rejecting $\beta = 0$ at 1\% while remaining indistinguishable from $\beta = 1$ ($p_1 = 0.247$ EW, $0.116$ FW).
VC L2 starts far above $U_{v,0}$ at inception ($1.925$ EW, $2.287$ FW---an observed gap of $+0.432$ EW / $+0.546$ FW, or $+0.435 / +0.551$ on the exact 23 matched $n_v \ge 2$ vintages, roughly twice the core MC expectation) and collapses to negative values at 30 years ($-0.373$ EW, $-0.573$ FW); holding $A_{v,H}$ fixed would push VC L2 \emph{upward} to $2.256$ / $2.680$, so the Scale-Jacobian term ($-2.629$ EW, $-3.253$ FW) drives more than the entire fall.
Meanwhile, VC $U_{v,H}$ peaks at 5 years ($1.992$ / $2.290$) and declines toward $0.760$ / $-0.076$ by 30 years, consistent with shared vintage effects ($c_v \neq 0$, T8) or cohort heterogeneity across the 1993--1997 boom and 1999--2000 bust (Online Supplement Figure~\ref{fig:vintage_residuals}).
Four-year overlapping vintage-block bootstrap 95\% intervals corroborate fixed-$b$ bands at $H=0$ for BO EW ($[0.929, 1.508]$ vs.\ $[0.818, 1.476]$), VC EW ($[0.745, 1.996]$ vs.\ $[0.562, 2.425]$), and VC FW ($[0.707, 2.284]$ vs.\ $[0.762, 2.722]$), while flagging upper-tail sensitivity in BO FW ($4.6\%$ of resamples hit $3.994$; Table~\ref{tab:block_bootstrap}).

\paragraph{IV root fragility and identification-robust AR sets.}
Panel~B of Table~\ref{tab:u_l2_beta} highlights why root-based IV point estimates are unreliable in Buyout: at $H = 0$, the mean pricing moment admits two interior roots ($2.146; 2.577$ EW and $2.023; 2.659$ FW), and deleting a single vintage destroys all roots in \textbf{11/25} (EW) and \textbf{10/25} (FW) refits.
Inverting the centered fixed-$b$ moment test \eqref{eq:ar_set} avoids root selection entirely and yields nominal 95\% confidence sets of $[0.949, 3.994]$ (BO EW), $[0.805, 3.994]$ (BO FW), $[-0.244, 3.994]$ (VC EW), and $[-0.549, 3.994]$ (VC FW).
All four sets cover both $\beta = 1$ and $U_{v,0}$ and reach the upper ceiling because the mean moment flattens at high discount rates, ruling out very low Buyout betas while offering minimal upper-tail identification.
Notice also that $N_{\mathrm{eff}}(U_{v,0})$ ($4.5$--$8.9$) exceeds L2's effective vintage count in every series (especially VC: $4.5/4.9$ vs.\ $2.9/1.9$), yet remains modest enough that pairing fixed-$b$ Wald SEs with AR sets is essential.

In Buyout, freeing $\alpha$ shifts inception $U_{v,0}$ market beta sharply down---from $1.147$ to $0.362$ (SE $0.207$) EW and from $1.197$ to $0.439$ (SE $0.178$) FW---while assigning $\hat\alpha = 0.605\%$ (SE $0.145\%$) and $0.568\%$ (SE $0.192\%$) per month; by 30 years, $U_{v,30}$ hits the lower beta bound in EW ($\hat\beta = -1.000^\dagger$).
Online Supplement Table~\ref{tab:alpha_beta_identification} and Figure~\ref{fig:alpha_beta_contours} trace this trade-off to a large positive Hessian cross-term ($0.646$ EW, $0.609$ FW normalized) and severe Bartlett sampling correlation ($\widehat{\mathrm{Corr}}(\hat\alpha,\hat\beta) = -0.783$ EW, $-0.810$ FW at $H=0$; $-0.99$ at $H=15$).
Over a 37-year factor span ($\sigma_{\mathrm{MKT}} = 15.4\%$/yr), \citet{Merton80} drift uncertainty alone imposes a $2.54\%$/yr ($0.21\%$/mo) lower bound on mean-return precision, illustrating why separating constant $\alpha$ from $\beta\,\mathbb{E}[F_{\mathrm{MKT}}]$ is inherently fragile on $V = 25$ vintages.

In Venture Capital, both $U_{v,0}$ and L2 collapse onto the $+2\%$ monthly alpha ceiling at inception ($\hat\beta = -0.080^\dagger$ EW, $0.368^\dagger$ FW for $U_{v,0}$), mirroring the shared-shock mechanism in Table~\ref{tab:mc_alpha_beta}, Panel~B.
Extending to $H > 0$ restores coercivity (Lemma~\ref{lem:coercivity}) while reversing $\hat\alpha$ from $+2.00\%$ ($H=0$) to $-0.47\% / -0.56\%$ ($H=15$) and $-1.00\% / -1.13\%$ ($H=30$) as $\hat\beta$ climbs above $2.0$.
Excluding a single five-year cohort---vintages \textbf{1993--1997}---removes the $+2\%$ boundary hit completely and yields smooth interior $U_{v,H}$ profiles ($\hat\beta$: $1.666 \to 1.958$ EW, $1.607 \to 1.881$ FW; $\hat\alpha$ consistently negative at $-0.50\%$ to $-0.81\%$/mo; Online Supplement Table~\ref{tab:vc_without_1993_1997}).

Two-factor $q^5$ specifications pairing MKT with ME, IA, ROE, or EG similarly exhibit weak identification (Online Supplement Figures~\ref{fig:empirical_BO_q_factors}--\ref{fig:empirical_VC_q_factors}).
In Buyout, interior $U_{v,0}$ models keep $\hat\beta_{\mathrm{MKT}} \in [0.93, 1.10]$ while non-market loadings are noisy and attenuate toward zero by 15 years (and Buyout FW MKT+ME hits the return floor at $H=0$, $\hat\beta_{\mathrm{MKT}} = 2.837^\dagger, \hat\beta_{\mathrm{ME}} = 3.605^\dagger$, before returning to $1.352 / 0.394$ at $H=15$).
In VC, every non-market $U_{v,H}$ loading swings from large positive values at $H=0$ (EG $1.59 / 2.10$, IA $2.59 / 2.67$, ROE $1.36 / 7.27$) to near zero or negative at $H=15$ ($-0.61$ to $+0.04$).
Placed beside representative studies (Online Supplement Table~\ref{tab:published_estimates}), our Buyout $U_{v,0}$ betas ($1.15$--$1.20$) align with cash-flow estimates ($0.91$ in \citealp{KN24} to $1.22$--$1.32$ in \citealp{ACGP18,DLP12,FNP12}) and lie below secondary-market indices ($1.76$--$1.79$, \citealp{boyer2023discount}), while our VC $U_{v,0}$ betas ($1.49$--$1.74$) sit below squared-loss $2.73$ \citep{DLP12} and $2.37$ \citep{KN24} and close to $1.72$--$1.80$ in \citet{ACGP18}.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
%% =============================================================================

Finite-pool cross-sectional noise fundamentally alters the identification and horizon behavior of nonlinear cash-flow discounting estimators for non-traded assets.
Squared pooled-NPV criteria embed a parameter-dependent variance term that tilts the population target upward even at inception, and averaging across compounding horizons $H > 0$ rescales every vintage by $A_{v,H}(\theta)$---introducing a downward Scale-Jacobian pull that displays finite-pool bias rather than eliminating it.

Our cross-fund pair-product criterion $U_{v,H}$ ($q_v = 1$) removes idiosyncratic variance tilt directly at bounded pool sizes ($n_v \ge 2$), restoring exact inception identification under strict factor-path exogeneity and zero shared vintage shocks.
When shared or nearby cross-vintage shocks are present ($c_v > 0$), moderate post-inception horizons matching our multi-deal duration equation $H_0^\ast$ restore coercivity and cancel first-order bias, whereas extending leave-out pairing across vintages fails via rank-1 collapse and finite-$V$ indefiniteness.
Paired with primitive one-dimensional fixed-$b$ Bartlett inference, the score-concentration diagnostic $N_{\mathrm{eff}}$, and identification-robust Anderson--Rubin moment inversion, the framework cleanly separates precisely identified single-factor market exposure from weakly identified abnormal performance and multi-factor loadings in private-equity cash-flow panels.
Promising directions for future work include extending duration-matched pair-product criteria to dynamic state-space cash-flow models, developing studentization-robust score-bootstrap refinements for small-$N_{\mathrm{eff}}$ panels, and applying the framework to private credit, infrastructure, and commercial real estate cohorts.
