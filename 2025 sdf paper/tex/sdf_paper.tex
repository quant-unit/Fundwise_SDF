\begin{center}
  {\Large\bfseries Cash-Flow Pricing of Non-Traded Assets: \\[0.5ex] Finite-Pool Identification, Horizon Selection, and Inference\par}
\end{center}
\vspace{0.5em}

\begin{abstract}
\singlespacing
\noindent Private companies, private capital funds, real assets, and illiquid credit represent a vast share of global wealth, yet lack reliable market prices, making irregular cash flows a primary basis for measuring their risk and performance.
Standard methods estimate risk exposures from pooled or cohort-grouped cash flows by minimizing squared net present values.
We show that nonlinear discounting creates a finite-pool bias: because higher discount rates mechanically shrink payoff dispersion, squared-loss estimators can remain biased even as the number of cohorts grows large.
We introduce a cross-unit pair-product estimator that eliminates idiosyncratic variance bias with as few as two units per cohort.
Under shared cohort shocks, pooling alone cannot eliminate the bias; we show how forward compounding to a duration-matched horizon cancels first-order bias and prevents boundary collapse.
We establish valid small-sample (fixed-$b$) and identification-robust inference, and demonstrate substantial bias reduction in simulations and North American private equity cash flows.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Benchmark deflator; Horizon selection; Non-traded cash flows; Private equity; Stochastic discount factor; U-statistics.

\noindent\textbf{JEL classification:} C14, C23, C58, G12, G24.

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
%% =============================================================================

Empirical asset pricing overwhelmingly studies traded securities whose periodic market prices allow researchers to compute one-period returns and estimate risk exposures linearly.
Even a listed corporation is a collection of staggered, untraded investment projects---factories, research pipelines, and acquisitions---whose cash flows the stock market continuously aggregates into a share price.
Without a public exchange, a vast share of global wealth is valued without reliable periodic prices, relying at best on stale appraisals \citep{BGK19,JLRS20,brown2023nowcasting} and often on no intermediate valuation at all.

Across this non-traded economy, irregular cash-flow streams are the primary economic record, and pooling arises naturally at two levels.
First, many non-traded claims are \emph{natively pooled} entities whose cash flows aggregate staggered underlying projects.
Prominent examples include private companies---which represent the vast majority of firms and a large share of household wealth \citep{MV02}---closed-end private capital funds managing over \$13 trillion \citep{McKinsey2024,DLP12,farrelly2019risk,andonov2021institutional,riddiough2022private,erel2024risk,jeffers2024risk}, illiquid CLO equity tranches \citep{cordell2023clo}, and portfolios of life settlements, litigation claims, or royalties \citep{BGS12}.
Second, even when cash flows are observed for individual assets---such as direct commercial real estate \citep{lee2019new}, unlisted infrastructure, whole loans, or human-capital contracts \citep{kroencke2013return}---high idiosyncratic dispersion leads researchers to group units into origination cohorts (``vintages'').

Estimating risk exposures and abnormal performance directly from cash flows creates an econometric problem absent in linear return regressions.
In the workhorse benchmark-deflator framework of \citet{DLP12}, parameters minimize squared cohort net present values (NPVs) discounted back to cohort inception.
Because discounting divides future cash flows by a parameter-dependent return factor, raising the discount rate mechanically compresses the dispersion of discounted payoffs.
Minimizing squared pooled NPVs therefore creates a \emph{finite-pool variance trap}: the optimizer can lower squared pricing errors simply by inflating discount parameters to shrink cash-flow noise.
Evaluating individual assets without pooling ($n_v = 1$) produces the severest variance bias, which is why researchers form cohorts.
Yet finite cohorts retain a non-vanishing distortion, and even massive cohorts ($n_v \to \infty$) remain biased when units share vintage or calendar shocks.
Formalizing the volatility bias noted by \citet[p.~517 fn.~8; Table~1, p.~522]{DLP12}, we prove that squared pooled NPVs can converge to a shifted population target even as the number of cohorts grows without bound at fixed cohort sizes (Theorem~\ref{thm:finite_pool}, T3--T4); absent shared shocks, consistency requires diffuse within-cohort infill (Online Supplement Lemma~\ref{lem:infill}).

To break this finite-pool trap without requiring large cohorts, we exploit a structural feature shared by all cohort cash-flow panels: every unit originating in the same vintage experiences the identical public factor path.
Replacing each cohort's squared average NPV with the normalized average of \emph{distinct-unit} pair products $U_{v,H}$ removes all own-unit diagonal squares identically, adapting leave-one-out quadratic-form debiasing and pairwise U-statistic M-estimation to nonlinear cash-flow discounting \citep{HP94,AIK99,CSHNW12,CJN18,KSS20,MS22}.
This tuning-free upgrade computes in $O(n_v)$ operations via a simple algebraic identity, scaling effortlessly from tiny to massive cohorts.
Under conditional independence across units given the public factor path and sign-aligned conditional means, $U_{v,H}$ restores exact identification at inception---and at any compounding horizon---with as few as two units per cohort (Theorem~\ref{thm:finite_pool}, T7).

In many non-traded markets, however, units in the same cohort also share unobserved vintage or calendar-time shocks---such as a venture boom, a real-estate cycle, or a credit downturn---that survive within-cohort pairing (Theorem~\ref{thm:finite_pool}, T8) and persist even as $n_v \to \infty$.
Under shared shocks, the inception objective retains a shared-shock variance tilt and remains non-coercive as abnormal return grows large, risking boundary collapse (``exploding $\alpha$''; Online Supplement Lemma~\ref{lem:coercivity}).
A tempting remedy is to extend leave-out pairing across distant cohorts.
We prove that \emph{uninstrumented} cross-vintage pair products fail structurally: they collapse asymptotically to rank one, breaking multi-parameter identification, and form an indefinite finite-sample criterion that pushes estimates to boundaries (Proposition~\ref{prop:optimal_H}(iv)).

This impossibility result explains why \emph{forward-horizon compounding}---introduced empirically by \citet[eq.~4]{TP24}, who average revalued pricing errors across post-inception valuation dates before squaring---is genuinely useful, and how it must be disciplined.
Through an exact algebraic factorization (Theorem~\ref{thm:finite_pool}, T1), averaging pricing errors over a horizon $H > 0$ multiplies each cohort's inception NPV by a common, parameter-dependent compounding scale $A_{v,H}(\theta)$.
That compounding factor performs two constructive roles.
First, at sufficiently late horizons it causes the criterion to diverge as $\alpha \to +\infty$, restoring coercivity and eliminating boundary degeneracy (Online Supplement Lemma~\ref{lem:coercivity}(b)).
Second, under strict factor-path exogeneity, differentiating $A_{v,H}(\theta)$ generates a \emph{Scale-Jacobian pull} opposing the backward-discounting variance tilt (Theorem~\ref{thm:finite_pool}, T5--T6; under inception pricing alone, post-inception revaluation also introduces a first-moment covariance wedge, T2).
Rather than acting as an arbitrary smoother---where too short a horizon leaves inception bias and too long a horizon overshoots downward---horizon length becomes a precision tuning lever.
We derive an exact \emph{multi-deal duration-matching score} whose zero-score root $H_0^\ast$ equates forward compounding sensitivity to covariance-weighted payoff duration, canceling first-order shared-shock bias (Proposition~\ref{prop:optimal_H}(i)).
In a constant-return benchmark, we establish tight global bounds for $H_0^\ast$, characterize how it responds to return drift, cohort persistence, and overlapping calendar shocks, and embed it in a local finite-vintage mean-squared-error expansion (Proposition~\ref{prop:optimal_H}(ii)--(iii)).

Addressing bias is only half the econometric challenge when empirical panels span roughly 25 overlapping cohorts.
Because vintage overlap runs along one-dimensional calendar time rather than a two-dimensional spatial field, we establish consistency, asymptotic normality, and valid small-sample (fixed-$b$) Bartlett inference \citep{KV05,LLSW18} from primitive bounded-lifetime mixing conditions (Proposition~\ref{prop:consistency_clt}, Corollary~\ref{cor:fixed_b_ar}).
To guard against multiple or missing roots in finite samples (Online Supplement Lemma~\ref{lem:multiple_roots}), we pair $U_{v,H}$ with centered fixed-$b$ Anderson--Rubin (AR) confidence sets \citep{AR49,SW00} valid under inception pricing alone.
We also introduce an effective-vintage diagnostic ($N_{\mathrm{eff}}$) that explains slow finite-sample studentization under heavy-tailed compounding.

To evaluate our framework, we combine a 72-design calibrated Monte Carlo study (plus an increasing-vintage ablation through $V = 1{,}600$) with an empirical laboratory of North American Buyout and Venture Capital cash flows from Preqin (383 Buyout funds across 25 vintages; 432 Venture Capital funds across 27 vintages, 23 pair-eligible).
Closed-end funds provide an ideal testbed: unlike ongoing private companies, they deliver complete birth-to-liquidation cash flows spanning both modest-dispersion Buyout cohorts and highly skewed, shared-shock-prone Venture Capital cohorts across pool sizes from two to over fifty funds.
In simulations, equal-vintage $U_{v,0}$ eliminates finite-pool bias with near-nominal fixed-$b$ size when shared shocks are absent, while duration-matched horizons remove boundary collapse and cut RMSE sharply when shared shocks are present.
Empirically, with $\alpha$ fixed at zero, Buyout exhibits modest finite-pool bias and stable market exposures just above unity across horizons.
In Venture Capital, removing diagonal squares lowers inception estimates substantially, prevents long-horizon squared-loss collapse, and yields coherent market exposures under both inception and shared-shock ($12.5$-year) sensitivity horizons.
Under inception pricing alone, AR sets rule out low Buyout exposures but remain wide for Venture Capital; across both strategies, abnormal performance ($\alpha$) and multi-factor exposures remain poorly identified.

\label{sec:model_id}

\subsection{Pooled cash-flow pricing restrictions}
\label{sec:asset_pricing_pooled}

Because non-traded funds (like private companies, securitized loan pools, or project portfolios) deploy capital across multiple quarters, unit-level pricing aggregates staggered underlying projects; we use this deal structure solely as a latent accounting device justifying pooled moments, while estimation uses only observed unit cash flows.
For fund $i$, let $CF_{i,t}$ denote the observed net cash flow at month $t$, with inception $t_i^0 := \min_j t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Inv}}$.

\begin{assume}[Deal-level decomposition]
\label{ass:deal_level_dgp}
For each fund $i$, there exist $J_i$ underlying deals (with bounded or integrable adapted participation indicators $\mathbf{1}_{\{j \le J_i\}}$ measurable with respect to $\mathcal{F}_{t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Inv}}}$) such that:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Deal $j$ is acquired at an $\mathcal{F}_t$-stopping time $t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Inv}} \in \{0,1,\dots,T-1\}$ with a single negative $\mathcal{F}_{t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Inv}}}$-measurable investment cash flow $\mathrm{Inv}_{i,j} < 0$.
  \item Deal $j$ generates a finite stream of positive divestments $(\mathrm{Div}_{i,j,k})_{k=1}^{K_{i,j}}$ with $\mathrm{Div}_{i,j,k} > 0$ and $t_{i,j,k}^{\mathrm{Div}} > t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Inv}}$.
  \item Observed fund cash flows equal the cross-deal sum $CF_{i,t} = \sum_{j=1}^{J_i} cf_{i,j,t}$ for all $i,t$, with $CF_{i,t} = 0$ for $t < t_i^0$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{assume}

Let $\Psi_t = \prod_{u=1}^t M_u$ ($\Psi_0 = 1$) denote the cumulative deflator discounting a time-$t$ cash flow to time $0$, and write $\Psi_{\tau,t} = \Psi_t / \Psi_\tau$.
Under exact deal-level pricing at acquisition, the deal-level pricing error
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:deal_pricing_error}
\delta_{i,j} := \mathrm{Inv}_{i,j} + \sum_{k=1}^{K_{i,j}} \frac{\Psi_{t_{i,j,k}^{\mathrm{Div}}}}{\Psi_{t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Inv}}}}\,\mathrm{Div}_{i,j,k}
\end{equation}
satisfies the conditional zero-moment restriction
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:deal_pricing}
\mathbb{E}\!\left[\delta_{i,j} \,\middle|\, \mathcal{F}_{t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Inv}}}\right] = 0 \qquad \forall\, i,j.
\end{equation}
In our reciprocal benchmark specification $M_u(\theta) = G_u(\theta)^{-1} = (1 + r_{f,u} + \alpha + \beta^\top F_u)^{-1}$, the parameter $\beta$ is the factor loading of the fitted cash-flow benchmark (equating it to the beta of a traded total-return series requires an auxiliary return-generating model).
The positive cumulative deflator $\Psi_t(\theta)$ acts as a private-claim pricing kernel only on the set of claims for which \eqref{eq:deal_pricing} holds; market incompleteness \citep{HR87} permits asset-class-specific kernels but does not by itself establish \eqref{eq:deal_pricing}, nor does $\Psi_t(\theta)$ impose public equity and Treasury pricing moments \citep{KN16,GSW20,GN18,KN24}.
As proved in Online Supplement Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_sdf} (see Table~\ref{tab:comparison} for a paradigm taxonomy), estimating distinct loadings $\beta_0$ across asset classes remains compatible with a single economy-wide public SDF $M_u^\ast$ whenever $M_u^\ast$ conditionally prices the traded benchmark $R_u^0 = 1 + r_{f,u} + \beta_0^\top F_u$ each period, exits are bounded stopping times $b$, and multiplicative idiosyncratic noise has mean one jointly given $\mathcal{F}_a$, the factor/SDF paths, and $b$: optional stopping then implies that $M_u^\ast$ also prices the deal when $\alpha_0 = 0$, while $\alpha_0 > 0$ ($\alpha_0 < 0$) maps strictly to positive (negative) public-SDF NPV.

\begin{lemma}[Deal-level timing]
\label{lem:deal_level_timing}
Suppose Equation~\eqref{eq:deal_pricing} holds and all products below are integrable (proof in Online Supplement Appendix~\ref{sec:proof_timing}).
\begin{enumerate}
  \item If $\tau \le t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Inv}}$, then
  \begin{equation}
  \label{eq:zero_deal_expectation}
  \mathbb{E}\!\left[\frac{\Psi_{t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Inv}}}}{\Psi_\tau}\,\delta_{i,j} \,\middle|\, \mathcal{F}_\tau\right] = 0.
  \end{equation}
  \item If $\tau > t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Inv}}$, then
  \begin{equation}
  \label{eq:non_zero_deal_expectation}
  \mathbb{E}\!\left[\frac{\Psi_{t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Inv}}}}{\Psi_\tau}\,\delta_{i,j} \,\middle|\, \mathcal{F}_\tau\right]
  = \frac{\Psi_{t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Inv}}}}{\Psi_\tau}\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[\delta_{i,j} \,\middle|\, \mathcal{F}_\tau\right],
  \end{equation}
  which generally differs from zero because payoffs realized by date $\tau$ are $\mathcal{F}_\tau$-measurable.
  \item If $\tau \le t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Inv}} < t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Future}}$, compounding forward to $t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Future}}$ defines the net-future-value moment $m_{i,j}^{\mathrm{NFV}}(\tau,t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Future}}) := (\Psi_{t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Inv}}}/\Psi_{t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Future}}})\,\delta_{i,j}$, whose conditional expectation given $\mathcal{F}_\tau$ equals
  \begin{equation}
  \label{eq:future_value_bias_derivation}
  \mathbb{E}\!\left[m_{i,j}^{\mathrm{NFV}}\!\left(\tau,t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Future}}\right) \,\middle|\, \mathcal{F}_\tau\right]
  = \mathbb{E}\!\left[\mathrm{Cov}\!\left(\frac{\Psi_{t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Inv}}}}{\Psi_{t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Future}}}},\,\delta_{i,j} \,\middle|\, \mathcal{F}_{t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Inv}}}\right) \,\middle|\, \mathcal{F}_\tau\right],
  \end{equation}
  which is generically nonzero whenever $\delta_{i,j}$ covaries with the forward compounding factor $\Psi_{t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Inv}}}/\Psi_{t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Future}}} = \prod_{u=t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Inv}}+1}^{t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Future}}} G_u$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

Aggregating Part~(1) across deals yields the pooled fund-level inception pricing restriction (proof in Online Supplement Appendix~\ref{sec:proof_timing}).

\begin{proposition}[Price of a pooled cash-flow stream at or before inception]
\label{theo:inception_value}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:deal_level_dgp} and Equation~\eqref{eq:deal_pricing}, if $\tau \le t_i^0$, then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:price_pooled_cash_flows}
\mathbb{E}\!\left[\sum_{t=\tau}^{T} \frac{\Psi_t}{\Psi_\tau}\,CF_{i,t} \,\middle|\, \mathcal{F}_\tau\right] = 0.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\subsection{Finite-pool identification, horizon rescaling, and the $U_{v,H}$ criterion}
\label{sec:fundwise_lmd_estimator}

For any unit $i$ (fund or vintage portfolio $v$, with cash flows $CF_{i,t}$ per unit of commitment in both EW and FW) and candidate $\theta \in \Theta \subset \mathbb{R}^p$, define the time-$\tau$ revaluation and its horizon average over $\mathcal{T}_{i,H} = \{t_i^0, t_i^0+1, \dots, \min(t_i^0 + 12H, T^{\max})\}$ ($H \ge 0$ in years, predetermined administrative end date $T^{\max}$) as
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:average_pricing_error}
\epsilon_{\tau,i}(\theta) := \sum_{t=0}^{T} \frac{\Psi_t(\theta)}{\Psi_\tau(\theta)}\,CF_{i,t},
\qquad
\bar\epsilon_{i,H}(\theta) := \frac{1}{|\mathcal{T}_{i,H}|} \sum_{\tau \in \mathcal{T}_{i,H}} \epsilon_{\tau,i}(\theta),
\end{equation}
with inception NPV $PV_i(\theta) := \epsilon_{t_i^0,i}(\theta) = \bar\epsilon_{i,0}(\theta)$ (Online Supplement Figure~\ref{fig:npvs}).
The squared-loss Least-Mean-Distance (L2 VYP) estimator solves
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:estimator_H}
\hat\theta_{H}^{\mathrm{L2}} := \arg\min_{\theta \in \Theta} \frac{1}{V} \sum_{v=1}^{V} \bar\epsilon_{v,H}(\theta)^2,
\end{equation}
matching \citet[eq.~4]{TP24} for $H > 0$ and the identity-weighted vintage-NPV objective of \citet[eqs.~3--4]{DLP12} at $H = 0$ for the same vintage-portfolio input.
With fund sizes $s_{v,i}$ (USD~mn), dollar flows $C_{i,t} = s_{v,i} CF_{i,t}$, and $S_v = \sum_{k \in \mathcal{I}_v} s_{v,k}$, FW uses $PV_v^{\mathrm{FW}} = S_v^{-1}\sum_i s_{v,i} PV_i = \sum_i \omega_{v,i} PV_i$ ($\omega_{v,i} = s_{v,i}/S_v$), matching the inverse-FoF-size dollar-portfolio weighting of \citet[Table~7, p.~533]{DLP12}; EW ($n_v^{-1}\sum_i PV_i$) averages per-dollar fund NPVs equally and defines a different portfolio when sizes differ, while squaring unnormalized $\sum_i C_i$ scales vintage squared error by $S_v^2$ (Online Supplement Table~\ref{tab:notation_asset_pricing}).

\paragraph{One vintage, two funds: squared average versus pair product.}
Consider one vintage containing $n = 2$ equally weighted funds, each investing $1$ at $t=0$ and paying $G_1(\theta_0)\cdots G_T(\theta_0)\,N_i$ at $t=T$, where $N_1, N_2 \ge 0$ are independent shocks independent of the factor path with $\mathbb{E}[N_i]=1$ and $\operatorname{Var}(N_i)=\sigma_N^2 > 0$.
Over $K = |\mathcal{T}_H|$ valuation dates, exact factorization gives $\bar\epsilon_{i,H}(\theta) = A_H(\theta)\,PV_i(\theta)$ with $A_H(\theta) = K^{-1}\sum_{\tau=0}^{K-1} \Psi_\tau(\theta)^{-1}$ and $PV_i(\theta) = (\Psi_T(\theta)/\Psi_T(\theta_0))N_i - 1$.
Conditioning on the public factor path $\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{pub}}$ (or $\mathcal{F}_{\max(T,K-1)}^{\mathrm{pub}}$, so $A_H(\theta)$ is known even when $K-1 > T$), the squared vintage-portfolio average $\bar\epsilon_{v,H}(\theta)^2 = A_H(\theta)^2(\frac{PV_1 + PV_2}{2})^2$ satisfies
\[
\mathbb{E}\!\left[\bar\epsilon_{v,H}(\theta)^2 \,\middle|\, \mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{pub}}\right]
= A_H(\theta)^2\!\left[\left(\frac{\Psi_T(\theta)}{\Psi_T(\theta_0)} - 1\right)^2 + \frac{\sigma_N^2}{2}\left(\frac{\Psi_T(\theta)}{\Psi_T(\theta_0)}\right)^2\right]
\]
(with $\sigma_N^2/2$ replaced by $\sigma_N^2/n$ for an equal-weighted $n$-fund pool; at $H=0$ and constant per-period gross return $R_0$, writing $z = (R_0/R)^T$ gives $R^\dagger = R_0(1 + \sigma_N^2/n)^{1/T}$---or $R^\dagger = R_0(1 + \sigma_N^2/n)$ for the cumulative holding-period return over $[0,T]$ or $T = 1$---a population distortion that survives adding vintages at fixed $n$).
Differentiating with respect to $\theta$ and evaluating at $\theta = \theta_0$ gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:one_deal_gradient}
\left.\nabla_\theta\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[\bar\epsilon_{v,H}(\theta)^2 \,\middle|\, \mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{pub}}\right]\right|_{\theta=\theta_0}
= \sigma_N^2\,A_H(\theta_0)^2\!\left[\nabla_\theta \log \Psi_T(\theta_0) + \nabla_\theta \log A_H(\theta_0)\right].
\end{equation}
At $H=0$ ($A_0 \equiv 1$, $\nabla_\theta \log A_0 = 0$), the score is nonzero whenever $\sigma_N^2 > 0$ (tilting $\alpha$ upward, and $\beta$ upward under positive excess returns).
As $H$ increases, $\nabla_\theta \log A_H(\theta_0)$ opposes $\nabla_\theta \log \Psi_T(\theta_0)$ (for $\alpha$ under $G_u \equiv 1$, crossing zero at $K = 2T + 1$ valuation dates).
Replacing the squared average with the distinct-fund product $\bar\epsilon_{1,H}(\theta)\bar\epsilon_{2,H}(\theta)$ yields $\mathbb{E}[\bar\epsilon_{1,H}(\theta)\bar\epsilon_{2,H}(\theta)\mid\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{pub}}] = A_H(\theta)^2(\Psi_T(\theta)/\Psi_T(\theta_0) - 1)^2$, removing the $\sigma_N^2$ term at every $H \ge 0$ and targeting $\theta_0$ at fixed $n = 2$.

\paragraph{General finite-pool identification and $U_{v,H}$.}
Consider $V$ vintages; vintage $v$ contains $n_v \ge 1$ funds $i \in \mathcal{I}_v$ with positive fund weights $\omega_{v,i}$ summing to $1$ ($\omega_{v,i} = 1/n_v$ under EW; $\omega_{v,i} = s_{v,i}/S_v$ under FW), sharing a common vintage origin $t_v^0$ and evaluation window $\mathcal{T}_{v,H}$, with per-commitment vintage-pool cash flow $CF_{v,t} = \sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_v} \omega_{v,i} CF_{i,t}$ and $PV_v(\theta) = \sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_v}\omega_{v,i} PV_i(\theta)$.
Maintain two benchmark pricing conditions defined on the actual selected and per-commitment weighted empirical population (taking complete-history reporting eligibility and fund sizes as non-informative or conditioned on in the design):
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Condition~(P) (Inception pricing):} $\mathbb{E}[PV_v(\theta_0) \mid \mathcal{F}_{t_v^0}] = 0$ with inception-measurable weights and selection (when eligibility uses future reporting, as in the complete-history filter, this imposes an additional pricing restriction on the selected cohort), as implied by Proposition~\ref{theo:inception_value}. Under persistent cohort shocks, $\mathcal{F}_{t_v^0}$ is the investor's inception information set (excluding unrealized outcomes of adjacent cohorts), so we distinguish unconditional $\mathbb{E}[PV_v(\theta_0)] = 0$ from conditional mean zero given earlier realized cohort outcomes.
  \item \emph{Condition~(X) (Strict factor-path exogeneity):} $\mathbb{E}[PV_i(\theta_0) \mid \mathcal{G}_v] = 0$, where $\mathcal{G}_v := (\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{pub}}, \mathcal{W}_v)$ collects the public factor path, vintage origin $t_v^0$, eligibility ($n_v \ge 2$), and fund sizes $\{s_{v,i}\}$. For a deal entered at random date $a$ with outlay $I_a$ and payoff $I_a (\prod_{u=a+1}^b G_u(\theta_0)) N$ (contributing $D_{t_v^0,a} I_a (N - 1)$ to $PV_i(\theta_0)$), $\mathbb{E}[N \mid \mathcal{G}_v, a, I_a] = 1$ is sufficient for Condition~(X) (the weighted orthogonality $\mathbb{E}[D_{t_v^0,a} I_a (N-1)\mid \mathcal{G}_v] = 0$ can hold under weaker assumptions), which fails when payoff shocks correlate with realized factor paths during the holding window (Design~A$'$ in Section~\ref{sec:simulation_study}).
\end{itemize}

For eligible vintages ($n_v \ge 2$) with $\mathcal{W}_v$-measurable weights $\omega_{v,i}$, define the \emph{cross-fund pair-product criterion}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:u_criterion}
\widehat{U}_H(\theta) := \frac{\sum_{v:\, n_v \ge 2} q_v\, U_{v,H}(\theta)}{\sum_{v:\, n_v \ge 2} q_v},
\qquad
U_{v,H}(\theta) := \frac{\sum_{i \neq j \in \mathcal{I}_v} \omega_{v,i}\omega_{v,j}\,\bar\epsilon_{i,H}(\theta)\bar\epsilon_{j,H}(\theta)}{1 - \sum_{i \in \mathcal{I}_v}\omega_{v,i}^2},
\end{equation}
with equal vintage weights $q_v = 1$ as our baseline ($q_v = n_v$ concentrates effective sample size in large late vintages and severely distorts fixed-$b$ inference; Section~\ref{sec:simulation_study}).
Writing $h_v := \sum_{i \in \mathcal{I}_v}\omega_{v,i}^2$, the exact $O(n_v)$ identity
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:u_identity}
U_{v,H}(\theta) = \frac{\bar\epsilon_{v,H}(\theta)^2 - \sum_{i \in \mathcal{I}_v}\omega_{v,i}^2\,\bar\epsilon_{i,H}(\theta)^2}{1 - h_v}
= A_{v,H}(\theta)^2\,\frac{PV_v(\theta)^2 - \sum_{i \in \mathcal{I}_v}\omega_{v,i}^2 PV_i(\theta)^2}{1 - h_v}
\end{equation}
shows that moving from pooled L2 ($\bar\epsilon_{v,H}^2$) to $U_{v,H}$ drops own-fund diagonal squares and rescales the surviving off-diagonal products by $(1 - h_v)^{-1}$ ($2$ for $n_v = 2$ vs.\ $10/9$ for $n_v = 10$ under EW, and analogously by size concentration under FW): this preserves identical unit weight on the common vintage signal $\mu_v^2 + c_v$ relative to L2 while scaling realized cross-fund noise and within-vintage heterogeneity in small or concentrated cohorts (unlike L2, $U_{v,H}$ is sign-unrestricted in finite samples, so a negative fitted value does not rule out shared vintage effects).
For $n_v = 2$, size weights cancel identically ($U_{v,H} = \bar\epsilon_{1,H}\bar\epsilon_{2,H}$), and for $n_v > 2$ fund $i$'s marginal pair weight scales with $\omega_{v,i}(1 - \omega_{v,i})$ rather than $\omega_{v,i}$, giving ``fund-size weighted'' a distinct meaning under heterogeneous exposures.

\begin{theorem}[Finite-pool identification of L2 and $U_{v,H}$]
\label{thm:finite_pool}
Suppose all funds in vintage $v$ share origin $t_v^0$ and evaluation set $\mathcal{T}_{v,H}$, with $\mathcal{W}_v$-measurable fund weights $\omega_{v,i} > 0$ summing to $1$, and assume the moment envelopes of Appendix~\ref{sec:proofs_asymptotics_H} hold (proofs in Online Supplement Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_theory}).
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[\textbf{T1}] \emph{(Exact rescaling identity.)} For every unit $i$ (or vintage portfolio $v$) and horizon $H \ge 0$,
  \[
  \bar\epsilon_{v,H}(\theta) = A_{v,H}(\theta)\,PV_v(\theta),
  \qquad
  A_{v,H}(\theta) := \frac{1}{|\mathcal{T}_{v,H}|}\sum_{\tau \in \mathcal{T}_{v,H}} \frac{\Psi_{t_v^0}(\theta)}{\Psi_\tau(\theta)}.
  \]
  \item[\textbf{T2}] \emph{(Mean preservation under (X) vs.\ compounding wedge under (P) alone.)} Under~(X), $\mathbb{E}[\bar\epsilon_{v,H}(\theta_0) \mid \mathcal{G}_v] = 0$ and $\mathbb{E}[\bar\epsilon_{v,H}(\theta_0)] = 0$ for all $H \ge 0$. Under~(P) alone, however, $\mathbb{E}[\bar\epsilon_{v,H}(\theta_0) \mid \mathcal{F}_{t_v^0}] = \mathrm{Cov}(A_{v,H}(\theta_0),\,PV_v(\theta_0) \mid \mathcal{F}_{t_v^0}) =: b_{v,H}$, retaining a first-moment compounding wedge whenever pricing errors covary with post-inception factor realizations.
  \item[\textbf{T3}] \emph{(Inception L2 variance tilt.)} Under~(P) with $\mathcal{F}_{t_v^0}$-measurable weights, at $H = 0$,
  \[
  \nabla_\theta\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[PV_v(\theta)^2\right]\Big|_{\theta=\theta_0}
  = \nabla_\theta\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[\operatorname{Var}\!\left(PV_v(\theta) \,\middle|\, \mathcal{F}_{t_v^0}\right)\right]\Big|_{\theta=\theta_0}
  = 2\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[PV_v(\theta_0)\,\nabla_\theta PV_v(\theta_0)\right],
  \]
  which displaces interior $\theta_0$ whenever vintage-avg.\ $\nabla_\theta\,\mathbb{E}[\operatorname{Var}(PV_v(\theta)\mid \mathcal{F}_{t_v^0})]|_{\theta_0} \neq 0$.
  \item[\textbf{T4}] \emph{(Finite-$n_v$ L2 decomposition.)} Under~(X) with conditionally independent funds of common conditional variance $\sigma_v^2(\theta) := \operatorname{Var}(PV_i(\theta)\mid \mathcal{G}_v)$ and equal weights $\omega_{v,i} = 1/n_v$ (replace $n_v^{-1}$ by $\sum_i \omega_{v,i}^2$ under general weights),
  \[
  \mathbb{E}\!\left[\bar\epsilon_{v,H}(\theta)^2\right]
  = \mathbb{E}\!\left[A_{v,H}(\theta)^2\,\mu_v(\theta)^2\right]
  + \mathbb{E}\!\left[A_{v,H}(\theta)^2\,n_v^{-1}\,\sigma_v^2(\theta)\right],
  \]
  where $\mu_v(\theta) := \mathbb{E}[PV_v(\theta)\mid \mathcal{G}_v]$ is the conditional mean of the weighted pool and vanishes at $\theta_0$.
  \item[\textbf{T5}] \emph{(Horizon gradient decomposition.)} At any $\theta \in \Theta$,
  \[
  \nabla_\theta\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[\bar\epsilon_{v,H}(\theta)^2\right]
  = \underbrace{2\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[A_{v,H}(\theta)^2\,PV_v(\theta)\,\nabla_\theta PV_v(\theta)\right]}_{\text{reweighted inception score (Fix-}A\text{)}}
  + \underbrace{2\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[A_{v,H}(\theta)\,\nabla_\theta A_{v,H}(\theta)\,PV_v(\theta)^2\right]}_{\text{Scale-Jacobian pull}}.
  \]
  \item[\textbf{T6}] \emph{(Horizon displays the tilt; it does not remove it.)} Under the conditions of T4,
  \[
  \nabla_\theta\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[\bar\epsilon_{v,H}(\theta)^2\right]\Big|_{\theta=\theta_0}
  = \mathbb{E}\!\left[n_v^{-1}A_{v,H}(\theta_0)^2\left(\nabla_\theta\sigma_v^2(\theta_0) + 2\,\sigma_v^2(\theta_0)\,\nabla_\theta\log A_{v,H}(\theta_0)\right)\right].
  \]
  Varying $H$ traces a DGP-dependent target curve $\theta_H^\dagger$; any horizon achieving $\theta_H^\dagger = \theta_0$ reflects cancellation of variance slope and Scale-Jacobian pull, not removal of noise.
  \item[\textbf{T7}] \emph{(Identification of $U_{v,H}$.)} Suppose~(X) holds and funds $i \neq j \in \mathcal{I}_v$ are conditionally independent given $\mathcal{G}_v$ with no shared vintage shock ($c_v = 0$), so $\mathbb{E}[PV_i(\theta)PV_j(\theta)\mid \mathcal{G}_v] = \mu_{v,i}(\theta)\mu_{v,j}(\theta)$ with $\mu_{v,i}(\theta_0)=0$. Then for any $\mathcal{W}_v$-measurable weights $q_v > 0$,
  \[
  \mathbb{E}\!\left[q_v\,U_{v,H}(\theta)\right] = \mathbb{E}\!\left[q_v\,A_{v,H}(\theta)^2\,\frac{\sum_{i \neq j}\omega_{v,i}\omega_{v,j}\,\mu_{v,i}(\theta)\mu_{v,j}(\theta)}{1 - \sum_i \omega_{v,i}^2}\right],
  \]
  which vanishes at $\theta_0$ for all $H \ge 0$. Conditional independence alone yields this expectation identity (with opposing fund slopes, $\theta_0$ can be a local maximum). Under sign-aligned proportionality $\mu_{v,i}(\theta) = a_{v,i}(\theta)\mu_v(\theta)$ with $a_{v,i}(\theta) > 0$ (nesting a common conditional mean $\mu_v$), $\mathbb{E}[q_v U_{v,H}(\theta)] \ge 0$ and $\theta_0$ is a global minimizer; for a common mean with $J_v := \nabla_\theta \mu_v(\theta_0)$, the local Hessian is $2\mathbb{E}[q_v A_{v,H}(\theta_0)^2 J_v J_v^\top]$ (up to normalization), requiring factor-path realizations whose sensitivity vectors $J_v$ span $\mathbb{R}^p$ (e.g., two distinct factor realizations in one period separate $\alpha$ from $\beta$), while global uniqueness requires $\mu_v(\theta) \neq 0$ on a set of positive weight for every $\theta \neq \theta_0$ (A1). Under Section~\ref{sec:asymptotic_framework}, $\hat\theta_H^U := \arg\min_{\theta\in\Theta}\widehat{U}_H(\theta) \xrightarrow{p} \theta_0$ for every fixed $H \ge 0$ with bounded $n_v \ge 2$ (though an unbiased population criterion can still have a biased finite-$V$ minimizer).
  \item[\textbf{T8}] \emph{(Shared vintage shocks, $c_v \neq 0$.)} Under a common conditional mean $\mu_v(\theta)$ and pairwise conditional covariance $\operatorname{Cov}(PV_i(\theta),PV_j(\theta)\mid\mathcal{G}_v) = c_v(\theta) \ge 0$ ($i \neq j$),
  \[
  \mathbb{E}\!\left[q_v\,U_{v,H}(\theta)\right] = \mathbb{E}\!\left[q_v\,A_{v,H}(\theta)^2\left(\mu_v(\theta)^2 + c_v(\theta)\right)\right].
  \]
  Thus $U_{v,H}$ removes the idiosyncratic diagonal contribution ($h_v$; $n_v^{-1}$ EW) while retaining a shared-shock score term as in T6 ($n_v^{-1}\sigma_v^2$ replaced by $c_v$), including $H=0$ exploding-$\alpha$ boundary hits via non-coercivity (Supplement Lemma~\ref{lem:coercivity}, Figure~\ref{fig:exploding_alpha}).
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{Admissible parameter domain $\Theta$.}
Because $M_u(\theta) = G_u(\theta)^{-1}$ requires strictly positive gross benchmark returns at every month $u$ spanned by sample cash flows, estimation minimizes over the compact domain
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:admissible_domain}
\Theta := \left\{\theta = (\alpha,\beta) :\; \alpha \in [-0.02, 0.02],\ \beta_{\mathrm{MKT}} \in [-1, 4],\ \min_{u \in \mathcal{U}} G_u(\theta) \ge \underline{G}\right\},
\end{equation}
with gross-return floor $\underline{G} = 0.08$ and box bounds preventing the exploding-$\alpha$ boundary degeneracy \citep[Footnote~8]{DLP12} illustrated in Online Supplement Figure~\ref{fig:exploding_alpha} (second-factor loadings are bounded implicitly on both sides by $\min_u G_u(\theta) \ge \underline{G}$).
Whenever a minimizer hits an active boundary of $\Theta$, interior sandwich SEs are invalid and suppressed ($^{\dagger}$).

\subsection{Optimal horizon theory and cross-vintage dependence}
\label{sec:optimal_H_theory}

Whenever a squared-error or pair-product criterion retains nonzero effective noise variance---$c_v(\theta) + (\sum_i \omega_{v,i}^2)\sigma_{\nu,v}^2(\theta)$ for L2 VYP, or $c_v(\theta)$ alone for $U_{v,H}$---horizon averaging pits backward discounting of random future payoffs against forward compounding through $A_{v,H}(\theta)^2$.
We analyze three dependence regimes: (1)~i.i.d.\ fund noise ($c_v = c_{v,w} = 0$), (2)~independent vintage shocks ($c_v > 0$, $c_{v,w} = 0$ for $v \neq w$), and (3)~nearby cross-vintage dependence ($c_{v,w} \neq 0$ for $0 < |y_v - y_w| \le L$) from persistent AR(1) cohort shocks $W_v$ or overlapping calendar-time shocks $e_u$.

Under Condition~(X), decompose fund inception NPVs as
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:three_regime_decomp}
PV_i(\theta) = \mu_v(\theta) + \eta_v(\theta) + \nu_i(\theta),
\qquad i \in \mathcal{I}_v,
\end{equation}
where $\nu_i(\theta)$ is fund-idiosyncratic with conditional variance $\sigma_{\nu,v}^2(\theta)$ given $\mathcal{G}_v$ and $\eta_v(\theta)$ is a zero-mean cohort/calendar shock with conditional covariance $c_{v,w}(\theta) := \operatorname{Cov}(\eta_v(\theta), \eta_w(\theta) \mid \mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{pub}}, \mathcal{W})$ and $c_v(\theta) := c_{v,v}(\theta)$.
Writing $\mathcal{L}_{v,H}(\theta)$ for vintage $v$'s term ($q_v \bar\epsilon_{v,H}^2$ for L2; $q_v U_{v,H}$ on the $V$ eligible vintages, conditioning on eligibility if random) and $Q_H(\theta) := \lim_{V\to\infty} V^{-1}\sum_{v=1}^V \mathbb{E}[\mathcal{L}_{v,H}(\theta)]$ (so $\widehat{U}_H$ in \eqref{eq:u_criterion} targets $Q_H/\mu_q$, $\mu_q := \lim V^{-1}\sum_v\mathbb{E}[q_v] > 0$, same minimizer), both satisfy $\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{L}_{v,H}(\theta) \mid \mathcal{G}_v] = q_v A_{v,H}(\theta)^2(\mu_v(\theta)^2 + \sigma_{\mathrm{eff},v}^2(\theta))$.
With cash-flow innovations $e_{i,t} := CF_{i,t} - \mathbb{E}[CF_{i,t}\mid \mathcal{G}_v]$, discount factors $D_{v,t}(\theta) := R^b_{t_v^0,t}(\theta)^{-1}$, symmetric effective covariance matrix $\Sigma_v^{(s,t)}$ ($\operatorname{Cov}(e_{v,s}, e_{v,t}\mid\mathcal{G}_v)$ for L2, and the pair-weighted average of $\operatorname{Cov}(e_{i,s}, e_{j,t}\mid\mathcal{G}_v)$ over ordered distinct pairs $i \neq j$ for $U_{v,H}$; Online Supplement Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_optimal_H}), and cumulative log-return sensitivity $S_{v,t}(\theta) := \nabla_\theta \log R^b_{t_v^0,t}(\theta) = \sum_{u=t_v^0+1}^t G_u(\theta)^{-1}\nabla_\theta G_u(\theta)$, we obtain the duration-matching score characterization.

\begin{proposition}[Duration-matching score, cross-vintage dependence, and uninstrumented cross-vintage pairs]
\label{prop:optimal_H}
Suppose Condition~(X) and Assumptions~\ref{ass:asymptotics_H}(A1)--(A5) hold (proofs and extensions in Online Supplement Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_optimal_H}).
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[\textbf{(i)}] \emph{(General duration-matching score and constant-return corollary.)}
  Whenever $\sigma_{\mathrm{eff},v}^2(\theta_0) > 0$, the population score at $\theta_0$ for arbitrary vintage-specific caps $\mathcal{T}_{v,H}$ is
  \begin{equation}
  \label{eq:duration_matching_score}
  \nabla_\theta Q_H(\theta_0)
  = 2 \lim_{V\to\infty}\frac{1}{V}\sum_{v=1}^V \mathbb{E}\!\left[q_v A_{v,H}(\theta_0)^2\,\sigma_{\mathrm{eff},v}^2(\theta_0)\,\Big(\bar{S}_{v,H}^{\mathrm{comp}}(\theta_0) - \bar{S}_v^{\mathrm{eff}}(\theta_0)\Big)\right],
  \end{equation}
  where
  \[
  \bar{S}_{v,H}^{\mathrm{comp}}(\theta_0) := \frac{\sum_{\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{v,H}} R^b_{t_v^0,\tau}(\theta_0)\,S_{v,\tau}(\theta_0)}{\sum_{\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{v,H}} R^b_{t_v^0,\tau}(\theta_0)},
  \qquad
  \bar{S}_v^{\mathrm{eff}}(\theta_0) := \frac{\sum_{s,t\ge t_v^0} D_{v,s}(\theta_0)D_{v,t}(\theta_0)\,\Sigma_v^{(s,t)}\,S_{v,t}(\theta_0)}{\sum_{s,t\ge t_v^0} D_{v,s}(\theta_0)D_{v,t}(\theta_0)\,\Sigma_v^{(s,t)}}.
  \]
  Under caps $T^{\max}$ or discrete grids, a root requires \eqref{eq:duration_matching_score} to cross zero over attainable $H$, and $\nabla_\theta Q_{H_0^\ast}(\theta_0)=0$ yields $\theta_{H_0^\ast}^\dagger = \theta_0$ only when $\theta_0$ is the global minimizer on $\Theta$ (not merely a stationary point).
  \emph{Constant-return corollary:} On an uncapped continuous window $[0,H]$ with $G_u(\theta_0) \equiv e^r$ ($r \ge 0$), scalar $\alpha$ (or $\beta$ under constant $\bar{F} \neq 0$), and $T_{\mathrm{eff}} > 0$ (guaranteed when discounted row sums $\sum_s D_{v,s}\Sigma_v^{(s,t)} \ge 0$ with positive weight on a future date, ruling out opposing-sign payment-timing shifts), \eqref{eq:duration_matching_score} vanishes at a unique score root $H_0^\ast \in (0,\infty)$ solving
  \begin{equation}
  \label{eq:exact_tau_bar}
  \bar\tau(H_0^\ast;\, r) := H_0^\ast - \frac{1}{r} + \frac{H_0^\ast}{e^{r H_0^\ast} - 1} = T_{\mathrm{eff}}
  \qquad\bigl(\bar\tau(H;0) = H/2 \implies H_0^\ast = 2\,T_{\mathrm{eff}}\bigr),
  \end{equation}
  which for $r > 0$ decreases strictly in $r$ at fixed $T_{\mathrm{eff}}$ (reinforced if $\Sigma^{(s,t)} \ge 0$) and satisfies
  \begin{equation}
  \label{eq:H_star_bounds}
  T_{\mathrm{eff}} \;<\; H_0^\ast \;<\; \min\!\left(2\,T_{\mathrm{eff}},\; T_{\mathrm{eff}} + \frac{1}{r}\right).
  \end{equation}
  Online Supplement Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_optimal_H} gives monthly-grid checks, a multiplicative-shift volatility illustration, and lookup tables; empirical $\beta$ uses additive $S_{v,t} = \sum_u F_u/G_u$ in \eqref{eq:duration_matching_score}.

  \item[\textbf{(ii)}] \emph{(Cross-vintage dependence.)}
  Under nearby dependence ($c_{v,w} \neq 0$ for $0 < |y_v - y_w| \le L$):
  (a)~$U_{v,H}$ eliminates the idiosyncratic diagonal contribution $h_v\sigma_{\nu,v}^2$ ($\sigma_{\nu,v}^2/n_v$ EW), and fixing the within-vintage marginal law leaves $Q_H$ and $H_0^\ast$ invariant to $c_{v,w}$ ($v \neq w$), while PSD forces $c_v(\theta_0) > 0$ (giving an inception score when vintage-avg.\ $\mathbb{E}[q_v\nabla_\theta c_v(\theta_0)] \neq 0$);
  (b)~$H_0^\ast$ is invariant to stationary AR(1) $\rho_W$ at fixed marginal law, whereas calendar shocks $e_u$ (overlap length for i.i.d.\ $e_u$ and equal outlays; double autocovariance sum under serial correlation) lengthen $T_{\mathrm{eff}}$ and $H_0^\ast$ strictly iff PV-weighted exit--overlap covariance is positive (nonnegative at common entry with nonnegative calendar autocovariances, strictly positive when exit variation alters overlap, and positive in our calibration, though late short deals can shorten $T_{\mathrm{eff}}$);
  (c)~$c_{v,w}$ enters the linear part of $\Lambda_H^{\mathrm{cross}}$ via $\mu_v'(\theta_0)\mu_w'(\theta_0)$ (total $\Lambda_H^{\mathrm{cross}}$ adds quadratic/mixed parts) and both signed stochastic terms of the $O(1/V)$ bias around the finite-$V$ target $\theta_{V,H}^\dagger$ (and around $\theta_H^\dagger$ under higher-order regularity if $V^{-1}\sum_v\mathbb{E}[s_{v,H}(\theta_H^\dagger)] = o(V^{-1})$, or $= \gamma_1(H)/V + o(V^{-1})$ adding $-\mathcal{H}_H^{-1}\gamma_1(H)/V$), while quadratic-shock covariances vanish at $H_0^\ast$ in homogeneous constant-return designs.

  \item[\textbf{(iii)}] \emph{(Multi-parameter trade-off, score variance, and MSE expansion.)}
  For $p \ge 2$, one scalar $H$ cannot generically zero all $p$ coordinates of \eqref{eq:duration_matching_score}. Under $\sigma_{\mathrm{eff},v}^2 \equiv 0$ (T7), $B_0(H) := \theta_H^\dagger - \theta_0 \equiv 0$, yet $s_{v,H}(\theta_0) := \nabla_\theta(q_v U_{v,H})|_{\theta_0} = A_{v,H}^2 s_{v,0}(\theta_0) + 2\,q_v A_{v,H}(\nabla_\theta A_{v,H})U_{v,0}(\theta_0)$ retains a random second term at finite $n_v$, so $H=0$ does not universally minimize variance (Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_optimal_H} two-fund counterexample; $H=0$ minimizes RMSE in our core simulations). When $\sigma_{\mathrm{eff},v}^2 > 0$ for scalar $\theta$ with simple zero $H_0^\ast$ of $B_0$ and smooth $\mathcal{V}(H) := \mathcal{H}_H^{-2}\Lambda_H$, the \emph{local} minimizer near $H_0^\ast$ of the asymptotic MSE proxy $B_0(H)^2 + V^{-1}\mathcal{V}(H)$ satisfies
  \begin{equation}
  \label{eq:H_mse_expansion}
  H_{\mathrm{MSE}}^{\ast,\mathrm{as}}(V) = H_0^\ast - \frac{\mathcal{V}'(H_0^\ast)}{2\,V\,[B_0'(H_0^\ast)]^2} + O(V^{-2}) \to H_0^\ast
  \end{equation}
  (global if $H_0^\ast$ is the unique globally separated zero of $B_0$ on the compact horizon domain; else competing zeros are ranked by $\mathcal{V}$, ties unresolved at $O(V^{-1})$); for actual finite-$V$ MSE under a uniformly differentiable $O(V^{-1})$ expansion with $\mathbb{E}[\hat\theta_H - \theta_H^\dagger] = b_1(H)/V + o(V^{-1})$ (including any $O(V^{-1})$ shift $\theta_{V,H}^\dagger - \theta_H^\dagger$ from~(ii)(c)), the local numerator becomes $\mathcal{V}'(H_0^\ast) + 2B_0'(H_0^\ast)b_1(H_0^\ast)$ (sign may flip), and discrete grids need discrete comparison.

  \item[\textbf{(iv)}] \emph{(Rank-1 collapse and indefiniteness of uninstrumented cross-vintage pairs.)}
  The \emph{uninstrumented} leave-band-out criterion $\widetilde{U}_{H,m}^{\mathrm{cross}}(\theta) := |\mathcal{P}_m|^{-1}\sum_{|y_v-y_w|>m}\bar\epsilon_{v,H}(\theta)\bar\epsilon_{w,H}(\theta)$ removes lag-$m$ covariances (AR(1) tails persist at every finite $m$ and vanish at $O(1/V)$), but as $V\to\infty$ with fixed $m$ converges uniformly to $(\mathbb{E}[\bar\epsilon_{v,H}(\theta)])^2$, whose Hessian at a zero has rank $\le 1$ (destroying regular local $p \ge 2$ identification; for scalar $\beta$, the limit can have multiple roots, as in Lemma~\ref{lem:multiple_roots} at $H=0$, whereas $A_{v,H}(\beta)$ at $H>0$ can shift or remove the growth-optimal root). In finite $V$ with retained pairs, $M_m = [\mathbf{1}_{|y_v-y_w|>m}]$ has zero trace and negative eigenvalues ($\lambda_{\min}=-9.51$ vs.\ $\lambda_{\max}=12.62$ at $V=25, m=7$), making $\widetilde{U}_{H,m}^{\mathrm{cross}}$ indefinite (42\%--100\% boundary hits in Monte Carlo; Table~\ref{tab:mc_regimes_verification}). By contrast, instrumented linear moments $\mathbb{E}[z_v PV_v(\theta_0)]=0$ (inception-measurable $z_v$ under~(P), or public-path $z_v$ under~(X)) remain valid under shared shocks and can achieve full rank \citep[cf.][]{DL04,MJK20}, requiring instrument choice and facing root fragility (Remark~\ref{rem:iv_alpha}).
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

Figure~\ref{fig:optimal_H_theory} and Online Supplement Table~\ref{tab:regime_summary} summarize Proposition~\ref{prop:optimal_H}.
Because $H_0^\ast$ depends on latent inputs ($\Sigma_v$, deal timing, benchmark dynamics), calibrating it requires a deal/shock model, and data-estimated $\hat H$ adds pre-estimation uncertainty.
In our Preqin calibration ($I=5$~yr, $h \in [0.25,11.5]$~yr, $g=13.3\%$, $\sigma_m=15.4\%$), the stylized static-shock duration gives a continuous-time zero-score horizon of $12.65$~yr ($14.78\text{--}15.02$~yr under calendar shocks $e_u$; Tables~\ref{tab:H_star_lookup}--\ref{tab:mc_regimes_verification}); because single-factor $\beta$ uses additive $S_{v,t} = \sum_u F_u/G_u$ in \eqref{eq:duration_matching_score}, $12.65$~yr motivates $H = 12.5$~yr and VC shared-shock MC ($-0.008$ bias for $U_{v,12.5}$) provides calibration.

\subsection{Identification-robust inception moment diagnostic}
\label{sec:ar_diagnostic}

Unlike quadratic criteria, the linear inception pricing moment
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:iv_moment}
\bar{g}_H(\theta) := \frac{1}{V}\sum_{v=1}^{V} z_{v,H}\,PV_v(\theta),
\qquad
z_{v,H} := \frac{A_{v,H}(1)}{A_{v,0}(1)},
\end{equation}
reduces at $H = 0$ ($z_{v,0} \equiv 1$) to $V^{-1}\sum_v PV_v(\theta)$, which has exact mean zero under Condition~(P) alone---even when $c_v \neq 0$ or strict exogeneity~(X) fails (at $H>0$, $z_{v,H}$ is a fixed reference-parameter instrument evaluated at $\beta=1$, so it does not grow with candidate $\alpha$; Online Supplement Remark~\ref{rem:iv_alpha}).
Solving $\bar{g}_0(\beta) = 0$ for a point estimate is fragile, however: the nonlinear pricing moment can admit multiple roots on $\Theta$ (including the public growth-optimal exposure; Online Supplement Lemma~\ref{lem:multiple_roots}), can lose all roots in finite samples, and faces boundary/collinearity subtleties in two-parameter $(\alpha,\beta)$ systems (Online Supplement Remark~\ref{rem:iv_alpha}; cf.\ \citealp{burnside2016identification} on weak identification in linear SDF models).

To bypass root selection and weak-slope division, we invert the centered Bartlett fixed-$b$ moment test \citep{AR49,SW00,KV05} across all $\beta \in \Theta$: with $s_v(\beta) = z_{v,H} PV_v(\beta)$ and centered long-run variance $\hat\Omega_g(\beta) = V^{-1}\sum_{v,w} K(|y_v - y_w|/S)\,(s_v(\beta)-\bar{g}_H(\beta))(s_w(\beta)-\bar{g}_H(\beta))$, collect all $\beta \in \Theta$ satisfying
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ar_set}
\left|\frac{\sqrt{V}\,\bar{g}_H(\beta)}{\sqrt{\hat\Omega_g(\beta)}}\right| \le c_{0.05}^{\mathrm{fb}}(b),
\qquad
b = \frac{S}{V}.
\end{equation}
While \eqref{eq:ar_set} is fully robust to multiple roots, zero-root samples, and near-zero slopes, its finite-$V$ coverage under cross-vintage persistence still reflects the small-$V$ Bartlett approximation (Section~\ref{sec:simulation_study}), so we report AR sets as nominal, calibrated-by-design companions to $U_{v,0}$.

\subsection{Primitive increasing-domain asymptotics and fixed-$b$ inference}
\label{sec:asymptotic_framework}

On strictly increasing vintage years $y_1 < \dots < y_V$ in $\mathbb{Z}$, let $\mathcal{L}_{v,H}(\theta)$ denote the vintage-$v$ term ($q_v\bar\epsilon_{v,H}^2$ with $q_v = 1$ for L2 VYP; $q_v U_{v,H}$ over the $V$ eligible vintages with $n_v \ge 2$), with target $\theta_H^\dagger := \arg\min_{\theta\in\Theta} Q_H(\theta)$ for $Q_H$ of Section~\ref{sec:optimal_H_theory} (equivalently $Q_H/\mu_q$ for \eqref{eq:u_criterion}).
Because fund overlap across vintages arises strictly along one-dimensional calendar time (rather than a two-dimensional spatial random field), increasing-domain asymptotics ($V \to \infty$ with bounded $n_v$) follow directly from five primitive DGP assumptions stated formally in Online Supplement Appendix~\ref{sec:primitive_assumptions} (Assumption~\ref{ass:asymptotics_H}, A1--A5):
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[\textbf{A1}] \emph{Compact positive-return domain and global identification:} $\Theta \subset \mathbb{R}^p$ compact and convex, factor returns with bounded support such that $\inf_{u,\theta\in\Theta} G_u(\theta) \ge \underline{G} > 0$ a.s., and unique population minimizer $\theta_H^\dagger \in \Theta$.
  \item[\textbf{A2}] \emph{Bounded lifetimes and pool structure:} bounded active fund lives $t - t_v^0 \le L_{\max} < \infty$, bounded fund counts $1 \le n_v \le \bar{n}$, bounded vintage weights $0 < \underline{q} \le q_v \le \bar{q}$, bounded calendar spacing $\sup_v(y_{v+1}-y_v) \le \bar{\Delta}$, and---for pair-eligible ($2 \le n_v \le \bar{n}$) vintages entering $U_{v,H}$---non-degenerate pair denominator $\inf_{v:\,n_v \ge 2}(1 - \sum_i \omega_{v,i}^2) \ge \underline{d} > 0$ (L2 and the linear AR moment have no pair denominator and accommodate singleton vintages $n_v = 1$).
  \item[\textbf{A3}] \emph{1D calendar-time weak dependence:} the annual calendar process $Z_y$ collecting monthly public factors $F_u$, calendar shocks $e_u$, and cohort shocks $\eta_y$ is $\alpha$-mixing along $\mathbb{Z}$ with mixing size $-\lambda < -(2+\delta)/\delta$ ($\delta > 0$), while fund-idiosyncratic shocks $\nu_{v,i}$ are generated from vintage $v$'s local calendar block and mutually independent innovations independent of $\{Z_y\}$.
  \item[\textbf{A4}] \emph{Primitive moment envelopes:} uniform $(16 + 8\delta)$-th moments on single-vintage compounded gross return/discount envelopes $M_{v,H}^{(R)}$, factor norms $M_v^{(F)}$, and gross cash flows $M_v^{(CF)}$ over $L_H := \max(L_{\max}, 12H)$ months.
  \item[\textbf{A5}] \emph{Interior curvature and partial-sum homogeneity:} $\theta_H^\dagger \in \mathrm{int}(\Theta)$, positive definite limiting Hessian $\mathcal{H}_H$ and long-run score covariance $\Lambda_H$, homogeneous partial-sum variance/Hessian limits for fixed-$b$ FCLTs, and sparse empty calendar years $(y_V - y_1 + 1 - V)/V \to 0$.
\end{enumerate}

Online Supplement Lemma~\ref{lem:mixing_moment_inheritance} verifies from A1--A4 that because each vintage spans a finite block of at most $m_H = \lceil L_H/12 \rceil + 1$ years, $(\mathcal{L}_{v,H}, s_{v,H}, \nabla_{\theta\theta}^2 \mathcal{L}_{v,H})$ inherit 1D $\alpha$-mixing with $\tilde\alpha(k) \le \alpha_Z(\max(k - m_H, 0))$, uniform $L_{2+\delta}$ envelopes and $L_{1+\delta/2}$ Lipschitz bounds, and ULLNs \citep{Andrews92,Davidson94,PP97}.
Assumption~A4 also foreshadows our simulations: because $\mathcal{L}_{v,H}$ is a fourth-order nonlinear product, score kurtosis grows sharply with deal lifetime and horizon $H$.

\begin{proposition}[Consistency and asymptotic normality]
\label{prop:consistency_clt}
Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:asymptotics_H}(A1)--(A4), $\hat\theta_H \xrightarrow{p} \theta_H^\dagger$ as $V \to \infty$ (with $\theta_H^\dagger = \theta_0$ for $U_{v,H}$ under Theorem~\ref{thm:finite_pool}(T7)). If Assumption~\ref{ass:asymptotics_H}(A5) also holds with $\mathbb{E}[s_{v,H}(\theta_H^\dagger)] = 0$ for every $v$, then
\[
\sqrt{V}\left(\hat\theta_H - \theta_H^\dagger\right) \xrightarrow{d} \mathcal{N}\!\left(0, \mathcal{H}_H^{-1}\Lambda_H\mathcal{H}_H^{-1}\right).
\]
\end{proposition}

The per-vintage centering condition holds for $U_{v,H}$ at $\theta_0$ under the conditions of Theorem~\ref{thm:finite_pool}(T7) for any profile of pool sizes, because every vintage's expected criterion is stationary at $\theta_0$. It can fail for L2 when $n_v$ varies across vintages (because the T3--T6 variance tilt can move each vintage's own minimizer) and can also fail for shared-shock $U_{v,H}$ fits under T8 (where a zero average duration score at $H_0^\ast$ need not zero each vintage's expected score; Online Supplement Remark~\ref{rem:per_vintage_centering}); we therefore attach fixed-$b$ significance markers only to $U_{v,H}$ and qualify tests attached to shared-shock horizon sensitivities accordingly.

For finite-$V$ inference, let $\tilde{s}_{v,H} := s_{v,H}(\hat\theta_H) - \bar{s}_H(\hat\theta_H)$ be the centered vintage score and form the 1D calendar-distance Bartlett long-run variance estimator \citep{C99,KS11,LLSW18}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:shac_H}
\hat\Lambda_H(b) = \frac{1}{V}\sum_{v=1}^V\sum_{w=1}^V K\!\left(\frac{|y_v - y_w|}{S}\right)\tilde{s}_{v,H}\tilde{s}_{w,H}^\top,
\qquad
K(x) = \max(1 - |x|, 0),
\qquad
S = \left\lceil 1.3\sqrt{V} \right\rceil,
\end{equation}
with sandwich matrix $\widehat{\mathrm{Avar}}(\hat\theta_H) = V^{-1}\hat{\mathcal{H}}_H^{-1}\hat\Lambda_H(b)\hat{\mathcal{H}}_H^{-1}$ and bandwidth ratio $b = S/V$.
Because $S/V \to 0$ under \eqref{eq:shac_H} whereas Corollary~\ref{cor:fixed_b_ar} holds $b \in (0,1]$ fixed, evaluating \citet{KV05} critical values at the realized ratio $b \approx 0.26$--$0.30$ ($V = 23$--$27$) serves as a finite-sample approximation that prevents the severe over-rejection of normal critical values ($13.3\%$--$14.1\%$ even on independent Gaussian draws; Online Supplement Table~\ref{tab:mc_size}).
Moreover, the \citet{KV05} limit is pivotal only under the homogeneous partial sums of A5, which trending pool sizes ($n_v$ rising from 2 in the 1980s to 41 in Buyout and 56 in VC) can threaten. We therefore check size by design: in the core Monte Carlo designs, which reproduce the observed vintage calendars and fund counts, $U_{v,0}$ rejects a true $\beta$ in $4.6\%$ (Buyout) and $6.4\%$ (VC) of draws (Table~\ref{tab:mc_core}).

\begin{corollary}[One-dimensional fixed-$b$ Wald/$t$ and identification-robust AR asymptotics]
\label{cor:fixed_b_ar}
Fix $b \in (0,1]$ with $S = \lfloor b V \rfloor$, and let $t_{\mathrm{KV}}(b) := W_1(1)/\sqrt{\Xi_1(b)}$ denote the pivotal \citet{KV05} Bartlett fixed-$b$ distribution (where $\Xi_k(b)$ is the Bartlett functional of a $k$-dimensional Brownian bridge $B_k$).
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[\textbf{(i)}] Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:asymptotics_H}(A1)--(A5) with $\mathbb{E}[s_{v,H}(\theta_H^\dagger)] = 0$ for every $v$, $\hat\Lambda_H(b) \Rightarrow \Lambda_H^{1/2}\Xi_p(b)\Lambda_H^{1/2}$ as $V \to \infty$, so any scalar contrast $t$-statistic for $H_0: a^\top\theta_H^\dagger = r_0$ converges to $t_{\mathrm{KV}}(b)$ (and a $q$-restriction Wald statistic converges to $W_q(1)^\top \Xi_q(b)^{-1} W_q(1)$).
  \item[\textbf{(ii)}] Under Condition~(P) at $H=0$ (or Condition~(X) at $H>0$) together with Assumptions~A2--A3, $L_{2+\delta}$ integrability, homogeneous long-run variance $\Omega_g > 0$, and sparse gaps, the centered AR statistic in \eqref{eq:ar_set} satisfies $\sqrt{V}\bar{g}_H(\theta_0)/\sqrt{\hat\Omega_g(\theta_0;b)} \Rightarrow t_{\mathrm{KV}}(b)$ without requiring unique roots, interior optimization, or a nonzero moment Jacobian \citep{AR49,SW00}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

In practice, we evaluate all $p$-values and 95\% intervals using $c_{0.05}^{\mathrm{fb}}(b)$ at $b = \lceil 1.3\sqrt{V}\rceil / V$ ($2.790$ for $V=25$; $2.867$ for $V=23$; $2.722$ for $V=27$).
Index-lag weighting $K(|v - w|/S)$ shifts Buyout $U_{v,0}$ SEs by $\le 0.003$ and lowers VC $U_{v,0}$ SEs by $0.031$ (calendar weighting is more conservative; no 5\% $H=0$ decision changes; 5/80 $p$-values cross a threshold; Online Supplement Remark~\ref{rem:calendar_vs_index_shac}).

\paragraph{Horizon-invariance specification diagnostic.}
Under T7, $U_{v,H}$ targets $\theta_0$ at every horizon, so $\beta_0^\dagger = \beta_{H_1}^\dagger$ is a necessary implication. For a single factor ($\alpha = 0$), we test $\beta_0^\dagger = \beta_{H_1}^\dagger$ via $t_{\mathrm{HI}}(H_1) = (\hat\beta_0 - \hat\beta_{H_1})/\widehat{\mathrm{se}}$, applying \eqref{eq:shac_H} to the stacked vintage influence terms. Under T7 and A5, $t_{\mathrm{HI}}(H_1) \Rightarrow t_{\mathrm{KV}}(b)$, consistent against separated targets ($\beta_0^\dagger \neq \beta_{H_1}^\dagger$) with shrinking contrast SE (Online Supplement Remark~\ref{rem:horizon_invariance}); common bias across both horizons is invisible, and finite-$V$ power is modest, though shared vintages make the contrast much sharper than either estimate alone ($H_1 = 15$~yr primary, $12.5$~yr secondary).

\subsection{Calibrated cash-flow DGP and design architecture}
\label{sec:mc_design}

We evaluate estimator bias, precision, horizon dynamics, and fixed-$b$ test calibration across 72 data-shaped Monte Carlo designs (1,456 summary rows; 250--1,000 replications per design, 1,000 in the two cores and fund-size Design~B, 500 in the path-shock, growing-$V$, and Optimal-$H$ Regime~1--3b suites) using common deal draws across estimators and horizons.
In the two \emph{core designs}, we match the exact North American Preqin Buyout (25 vintages, 383 funds) and Venture Capital (27 vintages, 432 funds) calendars and fund counts through 2010, the historical U.S.\ $q^5$ factor path, 15 deals per fund with quarterly cash flows (5-year investment window, holding lives up to 11.5 years), and exact pricing at $\beta_{\mathrm{MKT}} = 1$ ($\alpha = 0$) with mean-one multiplicative lognormal deal shocks calibrated to match empirical within-vintage cross-sectional dispersion (monthly $\sigma = 0.130$ Buyout, $0.154$ VC).
Because deal shocks are multiplicative and mean-one, both~(P) and~(X) hold exactly in the cores (unlike additive shocks with default truncation; Supplement Appendix~\ref{sec:dlp_simulation}).
Because public-calibrated SDFs and cash-flow-identified deflators target distinct objects from disjoint moments (Online Supplement Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_sdf}), we compare estimators targeting the same cash-flow parameter $\theta_0$: individual-fund L2, L2 VYP, $U_{v,H}$, inception linear IV/AR sets, and uninstrumented $\widetilde{U}_{H,m}^{\mathrm{cross}}$.

\subsection{Does $U_{v,0}$ remove finite-pool bias without distorting inference?}
\label{sec:mc_core}

Table~\ref{tab:mc_core} and Figure~\ref{fig:mc_decomposition} present the core single-factor results and yield four main conclusions:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{Individual-fund L2 is severely biased across horizons} ($+0.249$ at $H=0$; $-0.190$ BO / $-0.370$ VC at $H=30$), rejecting $\beta=1$ in up to $79.6\%$ of draws.
  \item \emph{Vintage-portfolio L2 reduces but does not eliminate finite-pool bias.} At $H=0$, L2 VYP is biased upward by $+0.075$ (BO) and $+0.177$ (VC), crosses near zero at 15 years ($-0.007$ / $-0.028$), and drops to $-0.111$ (BO) and $-0.375$ (VC) at 30 years. Figure~\ref{fig:mc_decomposition} confirms that almost this entire horizon slope stems from the population target shift $\theta_H^\dagger - \theta_0$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:finite_pool}(T3--T6)); L2 VYP rejects $\beta=1$ in only $4.1\%/6.3\%$ of draws at $H=0$ because positive target shift and negative finite-sample bias partially offset, whereas against its own target $\theta_0^\dagger$ it rejects in $13.1\%$ (BO) and $34.9\%$ (VC) of draws (Online Supplement Table~\ref{tab:mc_hardening}).
  \item \emph{Equal-vintage $U_{v,H}$ ($q_v=1$) eliminates finite-pool bias at every horizon and minimizes RMSE at $H = 0$.} At $H=0$, $U_{v,0}$ has near-zero bias ($-0.007$ BO, $-0.017$ VC), the lowest RMSE among estimators with near-nominal tests ($0.084$ BO, $0.107$ VC---less than half L2 VYP's $0.296$ in VC), and near-nominal fixed-$b$ rejection ($4.6\%$ BO, $6.4\%$ VC). Extending $H$ leaves $U_{v,H}$ nearly unbiased while monotonically inflating SD ($0.084 \to 0.186$ BO; $0.106 \to 0.273$ VC).
  \item \emph{Fund-count vintage weighting ($q_v = n_v$) ruins fixed-$b$ size.} Because late 2005--2008 vintages hold far more funds than 1985--1995 vintages, $q_v = n_v$ concentrates effective sample size in a handful of overlapping vintages and rejects $\beta=1$ in $20.3\%$ (BO) and $20.0\%$ (VC) of draws at $H=0$, although its RMSE is lower ($0.056$ BO, $0.086$ VC). A leave-one-vintage-out jackknife with $t_{V-1}$ critical values brings the Buyout rejection down to $10.3\%$, still twice nominal (Online Supplement Table~\ref{tab:mc_se_study}). We therefore use $q_v = 1$ for all empirical inference.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{How do fund sizes, path correlation, and heavy tails affect inference?}
\label{sec:mc_hardening}

Online Supplement Tables~\ref{tab:mc_size}--\ref{tab:mc_hardening}, \ref{tab:mc_v400_studentization}, and Figure~\ref{fig:mc_growing_v} test harder departures from the core design:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Heterogeneous fund sizes (Design~B, Table~\ref{tab:mc_hardening}, Panel~A):} Calibrated lognormal fund sizes ($\sigma_{\log S} = 1.19$ BO, $0.90$ VC) leave FW $U_{v,0}$ nearly unbiased ($-0.013$ BO, $-0.018$ VC) with mild over-rejection at $H = 0$ ($8.1\%$ BO, $8.5\%$ VC), which we flag as a qualification on FW markers in Table~\ref{tab:u_l2_beta}.
  \item \emph{Path-correlated shocks and AR replay (Designs~A$'$ and~A, Table~\ref{tab:mc_hardening}, Panels~B--C):} When deal payoffs depend on realized factor paths in a way that preserves (P) while violating (X) (Design~A$'$), L2 and $U_{v,H}$ stay biased as $V$ grows from $25$ to $100$ ($+0.15$ to $+0.30$), whereas $H=0$ IV approaches $\beta_0 = 1$. Replaying the centered fixed-$b$ AR test \eqref{eq:ar_set} at $\beta = 1$ yields $4.0\%/3.4\%$ rejection in the independent BO/VC cores, $9.4\%/11.1\%$ under persistent AR(1) M4 shocks ($\rho_W = 0.6$, $c/2c$), and $19.2\%/8.8\%$ ($c$) and $21.8\%/9.8\%$ ($2c$) in Design~A$'$ ($V = 25/100$), showing root robustness and finite-$V$ persistence coverage are distinct.
  \item \emph{Large-$V$ studentization and score concentration (Tables~\ref{tab:mc_size_additions} and~\ref{tab:mc_v400_studentization}):} On resampled paths at $V = 400$, equal-vintage $U_{v,H}$ still rejects $\beta_0 = 1$ in $6.8\%$--$10.8\%$ of draws with low $N_{\mathrm{eff}} = 13$--$27$ and left-tail excess, though $\hat\beta - 1$ is near-Gaussian (kurtosis $3.0$--$3.5$) and scales near $V^{-1/2}$. At $V = 1{,}600$, $H=0$ Wald size returns to nominal ($4.4\%$ at $n_v=20$, $5.0\%$ at $n_v=5$), and ablations (Online Supplement Appendix~\ref{sec:v400_studentization_app}) isolate two compounding-kurtosis channels: (1)~at $H=0$, lognormal payoffs produce left-skewed scores and $\mathrm{Corr}(\hat\beta-1, \widehat{\mathrm{SE}}) \in [+0.28, +0.47]$, shrinking $\widehat{\mathrm{SE}}$ in the left tail (unchanged under Gaussian returns; removed by low-$\sigma$ or symmetric shocks); (2)~at $H=15$~yr, $A_{v,15}^2$ inflates score kurtosis via $A_{v,15}^8$ \citep[cf.][]{Martin12}, halving $N_{\mathrm{eff}}$ even under symmetric deals ($50.4 \to 19.2$, rejection $3.2\% \to 9.0\%$). Evaluating the centered fixed-$b$ score test at $\beta_0$ avoids plug-in leverage shrinkage ($3.6\%$--$4.8\%$ at $H=15, V=400$; $4.2\%$--$5.4\%$ at $V=1{,}600$; $6.0\%$--$7.8\%$ at $H=0, V=400\text{--}800$). Practically, this motivates reporting $N_{\mathrm{eff}}$ alongside fixed-$b$ SEs and pairing Wald tests with AR diagnostics when $N_{\mathrm{eff}}$ is modest.
  \item \emph{Reconciliation with \citet{DLP12} (Online Supplement Appendix~\ref{sec:dlp_simulation}):} Table~\ref{tab:mc_grid} isolates the $n_v \times V$ infill noise effect within our simulator, while Table~\ref{tab:driessen_comparison} documents the remaining scenario differences relative to \citet[Table~1]{DLP12}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Data, constant-composition pooling, and implementation}
\label{sec:empirical_data}

We illustrate the methodology on the Preqin cash-flow dataset (exported April~14, 2022) restricted to North American USD-denominated Buyout (BO) and Venture Capital (VC) funds matched to U.S.\ $q^5$ factors (\url{http://global-q.org/factors.html}; \citealp{HXZ15,hou2021augmented}).
To construct \emph{constant-composition} vintage portfolios free of early-reporting artifacts, our seasoned baseline ($V \le 2010$) retains funds with complete histories (last record $> 5$ years after vintage start or still reporting near March~31, 2022), yielding \textbf{383 Buyout funds across 25 vintages} (1985--2010; 1989 absent, all 25 with $n_v \ge 2$) and \textbf{432 Venture Capital funds across 27 vintages} (1982--2010; 1983--1984 absent; 23 eligible vintages with $n_v \ge 2$ for $U_{v,H}$ after excluding four singleton vintages 1982, 1988, 1989, and 1991).
Cash flows are aligned on a monthly calendar from each vintage's first observed cash flow $t_v^0$, with final reported NAV booked as a terminal distribution for non-liquidated funds.

Online Supplement Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_data_engineering} documents three safeguards:
(i)~within-vintage median size imputation for the $10/383$ BO and $15/432$ VC missing sizes (avoiding pooled-mean weights up to $94.9\%$/$98.8\%$ in 1980s vintages while keeping BO FW $U_{v,0} \in [1.197, 1.226]$; Table~\ref{tab:fw_imputation_sensitivity});
(ii)~0\%--100\% terminal-NAV haircuts, which move inception L2 betas smoothly ($1.283 \to 1.170$ BO EW, $1.224 \to 1.133$ BO FW, $1.925 \to 1.877$ VC EW, $2.287 \to 2.245$ VC FW; Figure~\ref{fig:nav_disc_BOVC_MKT}, Table~\ref{tab:terminal_nav_shares});
and (iii)~stability of $U_{v,0}$ across complete histories, all funds (495 BO / 538 VC), and a 20\%-NAV early-stopper filter (328 BO / 344 VC; Table~\ref{tab:robustness_samples}; descriptive $hv$-block CV for L2 inception loss in Table~\ref{tab:appendix_e_cv_folds}).
We study single-factor \{MKT\} ($\alpha = 0$) and two-factor \{MKT + $\alpha$, ME, IA, ROE, EG\} models, leading with equal-vintage $U_{v,0}$ ($q_v=1$) paired with calibrated horizon sensitivities, L2 VYP, $N_{\mathrm{eff}}$, and fixed-$b$ AR sets.

\subsection{Single-factor market exposure ($\alpha$ fixed at zero) and AR diagnostics}
\label{sec:empirical_single_bovc}

All single-factor results in Figure~\ref{fig:u_beta_profiles} and Table~\ref{tab:u_l2_beta} fix $\alpha = 0$ and report the market loading $\hat\beta_{\mathrm{MKT}}$ of the fitted cash-flow benchmark (Panel~A) alongside inception ($H=0$) L2$-U_{v,0}$ gaps, IV root diagnostics, score-concentration counts $N_{\mathrm{eff}}(U_{v,0})$, and nominal 95\% AR sets (Panel~B; full 12-horizon and four-horizon tables appear in Online Supplement Tables~\ref{tab:u_l2_beta_all} and~\ref{tab:iv_comparison}).
Throughout, all-fund VC AR sets and full-sample VC L2 use all 27 VC vintages (no pair denominator is required), whereas VC $U_{v,H}$ uses the 23 pair-eligible vintages ($n_v \ge 2$).
We present each asset class in the sequence \emph{inception estimate / calibrated horizon sensitivity / inception AR set}.

\paragraph{Buyout ($\alpha$ fixed at zero).}
At inception ($H = 0$), equal-vintage $U_{v,0}$ gives $\hat\beta_{\mathrm{MKT}} = 1.147$ (SE $0.118$, $N_{\mathrm{eff}} = 5.3$) under EW and $1.197$ (SE $0.143$, $N_{\mathrm{eff}} = 8.9$) under FW.
Under T7, both reject $\beta = 0$ ($p < 0.001$; Design~B FW size $8.1\%$), and neither rejects $\beta_{\mathrm{MKT}} = 1$ ($p_1 = 0.325$ EW, $0.280$ FW).
Across horizons, $U_{v,H}$ stays flat through $H = 10$~yr ($1.158$ EW, $1.213$ FW) and moves modestly to $1.184$/$1.258$ at $12.5$~yr and $1.268$/$1.379$ at $15$~yr; the horizon-invariance diagnostic yields $p \ge 0.51$ at every horizon (Online Supplement Table~\ref{tab:horizon_invariance}).
By contrast, squared-loss L2 starts above $U_{v,0}$ ($1.283$ EW, $1.224$ FW) and falls to $1.108$/$0.989$ at 15 years and $0.933$/$0.798$ at 30 years.
In the Fix-$A$ exercise (Online Supplement Table~\ref{tab:fixA_jacobian}), T5 confirms a large positive Scale-Jacobian score at $H=30$~yr; as a reference-dependent counterfactual at $\bar\theta = \hat\beta_{U,0}$, freezing $A_{v,H}$ moves Buyout L2 only to $1.266$ (EW) and $1.106$ (FW) (gaps $-0.334$/$-0.308$).
Under the weaker inception-mean restriction (Condition~P alone), the 95\% AR sets $[0.949, 3.994]$ (EW) and $[0.805, 3.994]$ (FW) rule out low Buyout loadings while extending to the search ceiling.

\paragraph{Venture Capital ($\alpha$ fixed at zero).}
At inception ($H = 0$), equal-vintage $U_{v,0}$ yields $\hat\beta_{\mathrm{MKT}} = 1.493$ (SE $0.325$, $N_{\mathrm{eff}} = 4.5$) EW and $1.742$ (SE $0.342$, $N_{\mathrm{eff}} = 4.9$) FW.
The cleanest apples-to-apples comparison of the two fitted criteria is the matched 23-vintage difference between pooled L2 and the normalized pair estimator $U_{v,0}$ under identical $\alpha = 0$ and weighting: $+0.435$ EW and $+0.551$ FW, reflecting both diagonal removal and the $(1-h_v)^{-1}$ pair normalization of \eqref{eq:u_identity} (full-sample 27-vintage L2 is $1.925$ EW and $2.287$ FW, an observed gap of $+0.432$/$+0.546$, roughly twice the core MC expectation).
As $H$ rises, VC L2 collapses to negative values at 30 years ($-0.373$ EW, $-0.573$ FW), while freezing $A_{v,H}$ at $\hat\beta_{U,0}$ yields counterfactual estimates of $2.256$/$2.680$ (counterfactual gap $-2.629$/$-3.253$, matching the dominant Term~(II) score in Table~\ref{tab:fixA_jacobian}).
Meanwhile, VC $U_{v,H}$ peaks at 5 years ($1.992$/$2.290$) and declines to $1.382$/$1.583$ at $12.5$~yr, $1.250$/$1.423$ at $15$~yr, and $0.760$/$-0.076$ at $30$~yr, consistent with shared vintage effects ($c_v \neq 0$, T8) or cohort heterogeneity across the 1993--1997 boom and 1999--2000 bust (Online Supplement Figure~\ref{fig:vintage_residuals}).
The horizon-invariance diagnostic at $H_1 = 15$~yr gives $\hat\beta_0 - \hat\beta_{15} = 0.243$ (EW, SE $0.142$, $t_{\mathrm{HI}} = 1.71$, $p = 0.200$) and $0.319$ (FW, SE $0.147$, $t_{\mathrm{HI}} = 2.18$, $p = 0.115$; Online Supplement Table~\ref{tab:horizon_invariance}), which is weak evidence because power against VC-scale shared shocks ($\sigma_W = 0.50$, expected gap $0.38$) is only $33.2\%$.
We therefore report the two VC regimes side by side as \emph{sensitivity estimates}: $1.493$/$1.742$ at inception (under T7 with $c_v = 0$) and $1.382$/$1.583$ at $H = 12.5$~yr (motivated by the stylized $12.65$-yr static-shock duration calculation and backed by VC shared-shock Monte Carlo calibration evidence, where additive-model $U_{v,12.5}$ has $-0.008$ bias).
These are sensitivity readouts rather than a bracketing interval: heterogeneity or a failure of path exogeneity~(X) could move both estimates to the same side of truth, and tests attached to the $12.5$-year estimates carry both the T8 per-vintage centering caveat (Remark~\ref{rem:per_vintage_centering}) and persistent-shock over-rejection (Table~\ref{tab:mc_regimes_verification}).
Under Condition~(P) alone, the 27-vintage inception AR sets are $[-0.244, 3.994]$ (EW) and $[-0.549, 3.994]$ (FW): both include zero and one, so the weak-restriction exercise provides little directional precision for VC, in contrast to the stronger-model (T7) Wald rejections of $\beta = 0$ at 1\% (while non-rejection of $\beta = 1$, $p_1 = 0.247$ EW / $0.116$ FW, reflects wide uncertainty rather than a precise unit-beta confirmation).
Four-year vintage-block bootstrap 95\% intervals corroborate fixed-$b$ bands at $H=0$ for BO EW ($[0.929, 1.508]$ vs.\ $[0.818, 1.476]$), VC EW ($[0.745, 1.996]$ vs.\ $[0.562, 2.425]$), and VC FW ($[0.707, 2.284]$ vs.\ $[0.762, 2.722]$), while flagging BO FW upper-tail sensitivity ($4.6\%$ hit $3.994$; Table~\ref{tab:block_bootstrap}).

\paragraph{IV root fragility and score concentration.}
Panel~B of Table~\ref{tab:u_l2_beta} shows why root-based IV point estimation is unreliable in Buyout: at $H = 0$, the mean moment has two interior roots ($2.146; 2.577$ EW and $2.023; 2.659$ FW), and deleting one vintage destroys all roots in \textbf{11/25} (EW) and \textbf{10/25} (FW) refits.
Inverting the centered fixed-$b$ test \eqref{eq:ar_set} bypasses root selection; all four AR sets cover $\beta = 1$ and $U_{v,0}$ and reach $3.994$ where the mean moment flattens.
Moreover, $N_{\mathrm{eff}}(U_{v,0})$ ($4.5$--$8.9$) exceeds L2 in every series (VC: $4.5/4.9$ vs.\ $2.9/1.9$), yet measures score concentration rather than independent degrees of freedom, underscoring why stronger-model Wald SEs should be paired with weak-restriction AR sets.

In Buyout, freeing $\alpha$ shifts inception $U_{v,0}$ market beta sharply down---from $1.147$ to $0.362$ (SE $0.207$) EW and from $1.197$ to $0.439$ (SE $0.178$) FW---while assigning $\hat\alpha = 0.605\%$ (SE $0.145\%$) and $0.568\%$ (SE $0.192\%$) per month; by 30 years, $U_{v,30}$ hits the lower beta bound in EW ($\hat\beta = -1.000^\dagger$).
Online Supplement Table~\ref{tab:alpha_beta_identification} and Figure~\ref{fig:alpha_beta_contours} trace this ridge and bound sensitivity to a large positive Hessian cross-term ($0.646$ EW, $0.609$ FW normalized) and strong Bartlett sampling correlation ($\widehat{\mathrm{Corr}}(\hat\alpha,\hat\beta) = -0.783$ EW, $-0.810$ FW at $H=0$; $-0.99$ at $H=15$), illustrating why separating constant $\alpha$ from $\beta F_{\mathrm{MKT}}$ is fragile on $V = 25$ vintages (while not constituting a formal weak-identification asymptotic sequence).

In Venture Capital, both $U_{v,0}$ and L2 collapse onto the $+2\%$ monthly alpha ceiling at inception ($\hat\beta = -0.080^\dagger$ EW, $0.368^\dagger$ FW for $U_{v,0}$), mirroring the shared-shock mechanism in Table~\ref{tab:mc_alpha_beta}, Panel~B.
At sufficiently late horizons satisfying Lemma~\ref{lem:coercivity}(b), the criterion diverges as $\alpha \to +\infty$; empirically, interior fits occur at $H = 5, 15$, and $30$~yr (tail growth alone cannot force a minimum inside $[-0.02, 0.02]$), with $\hat\alpha$ reversing from $+2.00\%$ ($H=0$) to $-0.47\% / -0.56\%$ ($H=15$) and $-1.00\% / -1.13\%$ ($H=30$) as $\hat\beta$ climbs above $2.0$.
Excluding a single five-year cohort---vintages \textbf{1993--1997}---removes the $+2\%$ boundary hit completely and yields smooth interior $U_{v,H}$ profiles ($\hat\beta$: $1.666 \to 1.958$ EW, $1.607 \to 1.881$ FW; $\hat\alpha$ consistently negative at $-0.50\%$ to $-0.81\%$/mo; Online Supplement Table~\ref{tab:vc_without_1993_1997}).

Two-factor $q^5$ specifications pairing MKT with ME, IA, ROE, or EG similarly display severe horizon instability and poor identification (Online Supplement Figures~\ref{fig:empirical_BO_q_factors}--\ref{fig:empirical_VC_q_factors}).
In Buyout, interior $U_{v,0}$ models keep $\hat\beta_{\mathrm{MKT}} \in [0.93, 1.10]$ while non-market loadings are noisy and attenuate toward zero by 15 years (and Buyout FW MKT+ME hits the return floor at $H=0$, $\hat\beta_{\mathrm{MKT}} = 2.837^\dagger, \hat\beta_{\mathrm{ME}} = 3.605^\dagger$, before returning to $1.352 / 0.394$ at $H=15$).
In VC, every non-market $U_{v,H}$ loading swings from large positive values at $H=0$ (EG $1.59 / 2.10$, IA $2.59 / 2.67$, ROE $1.36 / 7.27$) to near zero or negative at $H=15$ ($-0.61$ to $+0.04$).
Placed beside representative studies (Online Supplement Table~\ref{tab:published_estimates}), our $\alpha = 0$ Buyout $U_{v,0}$ loadings ($1.15$--$1.20$) align with cash-flow estimates ($0.91$ in \citealp{KN24} to $1.22$--$1.32$ in \citealp{ACGP18,DLP12,FNP12}) and lie below secondary-market indices ($1.76$--$1.79$, \citealp{boyer2023discount}).
For Venture Capital, our $\alpha = 0$ $U_{v,0}$ loadings ($1.49$--$1.74$) lie near \citet{ACGP18} ($1.72$--$1.80$) and below \citet{DLP12} ($2.73$) and \citet{KN24} ($2.37$); because \citet[Table~5, pp.~527--528]{DLP12} estimate VC $\beta = 2.73$ jointly with $\alpha = -1.09\%$/mo and jointly estimated final market values on TVE 1980--1993 data, that cross-study gap reflects specification and sample differences rather than the L2-to-$U_{v,0}$ change alone, whose matched 23-vintage difference between pooled L2 and the normalized pair estimator $U_{v,0}$ is $+0.435$ (EW) and $+0.551$ (FW).

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
%% =============================================================================

This article develops an econometric framework for pricing non-traded assets directly from pooled or cohort-grouped cash flows.
Because nonlinear discounting links payoff variance to the discounting parameters, conventional squared net present value estimators can suffer from a finite-pool bias that additional cohorts do not remove.
Separating the two layers of cash-flow noise yields a simple resolution: a within-cohort cross-unit estimator eliminates idiosyncratic noise directly, while forward-horizon compounding helps offset shared-shock bias that pooling alone cannot diversify away.
Combined with valid small-sample and identification-robust inference, our simulations and North American private equity application show that these methods substantially reduce squared-loss distortions while clarifying what typical cash-flow panels can---and cannot---reliably identify.

Future research may develop data-driven horizon selection, accommodate time-varying exposures, and apply these tools to other non-traded domains such as private credit, infrastructure, real assets, and private-company cohorts.
Taken together, our results point to a simple organizing principle for pooled cash-flow asset pricing: eliminate what you can; cancel what you cannot.
