\begin{abstract}
We study semiparametric stochastic discount factor (SDF) estimation from pooled, non-traded cash flows when vintage portfolios contain a finite number of funds.
Minimizing squared pooled net present values---the inception criterion of \citet{DLP12}---does not target the structural pricing parameter at finite pool sizes: within-vintage cash-flow noise enters the population objective through a parameter-dependent variance term and tilts the estimand even at inception.
Averaging pricing errors across post-inception compounding horizons $H$ multiplies each vintage's inception error by a parameter-dependent scale factor, displaying the noise tilt through reweighting and a scale-Jacobian pull rather than removing it.
To eliminate the diagonal variance tilt under finite pool sizes, we introduce a cross-fund pair-product criterion $U_{v,H}$ that targets the structural parameter at every horizon---including inception ($H=0$)---under conditional fund independence without shared vintage shocks, and pair it with an identification-robust fixed-$b$ moment test on the inception pricing restriction.
In a cleaned North American Preqin panel of 383 Buyout and 432 Venture Capital funds (vintages 1982--2010), equal-vintage $U_{v,0}$ yields market betas of 1.15 (EW) and 1.20 (FW) for Buyout and 1.49 (EW) and 1.74 (FW) for Venture Capital---none distinguishable from unity---while squared-loss estimates decline sharply with horizon and turn negative at 30 years in Venture Capital.
In contrast, abnormal performance ($\alpha$) and second $q^5$ factor loadings are only weakly identified: Buyout $(\alpha,\beta)$ estimates trade off sharply along a flat ridge with over-rejecting nominal tests, and Venture Capital inception estimates hit the $+2\%$ monthly alpha ceiling solely because of the 1993--1997 cohort.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Stochastic Discount Factor, Semiparametric Estimation, Spatial Dependence, U-Statistics, Private Equity, Illiquid Assets

\section*{Introduction}
\else
\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
\fi

How should stochastic discount factor (SDF) estimators be identified and interpreted when the underlying data consist of pooled, non-traded cash flows rather than traded returns?
This question sits at the center of the empirical private-market asset-pricing literature on abnormal performance and systematic risk \citep[see][for reviews]{K19,korteweg2023risk}.
The semiparametric cash-flow SDF estimators of \citet{DLP12} and \citet{KN16} recover factor exposures ($\beta$) and abnormal performance ($\alpha$) without imposing parametric return distributions.
Yet an essential econometric question remains open: in realistic panels with a modest number of overlapping vintages and finite numbers of funds per vintage, which objects do squared-error cash-flow criteria actually target, and which parameters can be identified with reliable inference?

This paper shows that the standard squared-loss criterion of \citet{DLP12} has a structural identification flaw whenever vintage portfolios contain a finite number of funds ($n_v < \infty$).
Minimizing the average squared vintage-portfolio net present value (NPV) targets the minimizer of $\mathbb{E}[PV_v(\theta)^2] = \operatorname{Var}(PV_v(\theta)) + (\mathbb{E}[PV_v(\theta)])^2$.
Because the discounted payoff variance depends on $\theta$---higher discount rates shrink random future payouts toward zero---the variance term tilts the population minimizer upward even at inception ($H=0$), where the mean pricing restriction $\mathbb{E}[PV_v(\theta_0)] = 0$ holds exactly.
Only in the \citet{DLP12} infill limit ($n_v \to \infty$ inside fixed vintages, with no shared vintage shocks) does this variance term vanish.

A natural modification is to average pricing errors over additional compounding dates $\mathcal{T}_{v,H}$ beyond fund inception, spanning a horizon $H \ge 0$ years.
We establish an exact algebraic rescaling identity: for any vintage portfolio sharing a common origin $t_v^0$, the horizon-averaged pricing error equals $\bar\epsilon_{v,H}(\theta) = A_{v,H}(\theta)\,PV_v(\theta)$, where $A_{v,H}(\theta)$ is the average compounding factor over $\mathcal{T}_{v,H}$.
This identity transforms how horizon variation must be interpreted.
When deal-level pricing errors have zero conditional mean given the factor path, the horizon-averaged pricing error preserves exact mean zero at $\theta_0$ for every $H \ge 0$; yet squared loss still embeds the finite-pool variance term, now multiplied by $A_{v,H}(\theta)^2$.
Because $A_{v,H}(\theta)$ increases in $\theta$ at longer horizons, differentiating $A_{v,H}(\theta)^2$ introduces a downward \emph{Scale-Jacobian pull} that pushes the squared-loss target below $\theta_0$ as $H$ grows.
Consequently, the horizon profile of a squared-loss estimator \emph{displays} finite-pool noise distortion rather than curing it: any intermediate horizon where squared-loss bias crosses zero is a data-generating-process-specific accident where positive inception variance tilt cancels negative long-horizon scale pull.

To solve the finite-pool variance tilt directly, we propose the cross-fund pair-product criterion $U_{v,H}$, which drops the own-fund diagonal squares within each vintage and averages distinct-fund cross-products $\bar\epsilon_{i,H}(\theta)\bar\epsilon_{j,H}(\theta)$ ($i \neq j$) across vintages with equal vintage weights ($q_v = 1$).
Under conditional independence of funds within a vintage given the factor path, eligibility ($n_v \ge 2$), fund sizes, and vintage origin---and in the absence of shared vintage-level shocks ($c_v = 0$)---the diagonal variance term vanishes identically in expectation.
The criterion $U_{v,H}$ therefore targets the structural parameter $\theta_0$ at \emph{every} horizon, including inception ($H = 0$), without requiring $n_v \to \infty$.
Because $H = 0$ avoids horizon-induced scale noise and minimizes finite-sample root mean squared error (RMSE) for single-factor $\beta$ and joint $(\alpha,\beta)$ in our data-shaped simulations, $U_{v,0}$ serves as our headline estimator, while the profile across $H$ serves as a diagnostic for residual shared vintage effects or misspecification.
To probe sensitivity to shared vintage shocks ($c_v \neq 0$) without relying on unstable point-root selection, we pair $U_{v,0}$ with an identification-robust diagnostic obtained by inverting the centered Bartlett fixed-$b$ test of the inception mean pricing moment $V^{-1}\sum_v PV_v(\theta) = 0$ \citep{AR49,SW00,KV05}.

We develop increasing-domain vintage-year asymptotics for both criteria, using the \citet{LLSW18} Bartlett bandwidth $S = \lceil 1.3\sqrt{V}\rceil$ and \citet{KV05} fixed-$b$ critical values, and validate finite-sample behavior across 55 Monte Carlo designs (1,246 design-estimator-horizon cells) calibrated to the empirical Preqin vintage calendars, fund counts, deal volatilities, fund-size dispersion, and path-correlated shock environments.
We then apply the framework to a cleaned North American USD Preqin panel with constant-composition vintage portfolios covering 383 Buyout funds across 25 vintages (1985--2010) and 432 Venture Capital funds across 27 vintages (1982--2010; 23 vintages with $n_v \ge 2$).

The empirical analysis yields four main findings:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{Single-factor market betas are well anchored near unity for Buyout and higher, though less precise, for Venture Capital.}
  At $H = 0$, equal-vintage $U_{v,0}$ delivers $\hat\beta_{\mathrm{MKT}} = 1.147$ (SE $0.118$) equal-weighted (EW) and $1.197$ (SE $0.143$) fund-size weighted (FW) for Buyout, and $1.493$ (SE $0.325$) EW and $1.742$ (SE $0.342$) FW for Venture Capital.
  None of the four inception $U_{v,0}$ estimates rejects $\beta_{\mathrm{MKT}} = 1$ at the 5\% level ($p_1$ between $0.116$ and $0.325$).
  In Buyout, $U_{v,H}$ stays flat through $H = 10$ years ($1.147$--$1.158$ EW; $1.197$--$1.213$ FW) whereas squared-loss L2 declines monotonically from $1.283$ to $0.933$ (EW) and from $1.224$ to $0.798$ (FW) by 30 years.
  In Venture Capital, L2 falls from $1.925$ to $-0.373$ (EW) and from $2.287$ to $-0.573$ (FW) at 30 years, driven overwhelmingly by the Scale-Jacobian pull.

  \item \emph{The inception mean-moment diagnostic is wide and confirms that root-based IV point estimates are fragile in Buyout.}
  At $H = 0$, the Buyout mean pricing moment has two interior roots ($2.146$ and $2.577$ EW; $2.023$ and $2.659$ FW), and deleting a single vintage destroys all roots in 11 of 25 (EW) and 10 of 25 (FW) refits.
  Inverting the centered Bartlett fixed-$b$ moment test avoids root selection and weak-slope division, yielding wide nominal 95\% confidence sets---$[0.949, 3.994]$ (BO EW), $[0.805, 3.994]$ (BO FW), $[-0.244, 3.994]$ (VC EW), and $[-0.549, 3.994]$ (VC FW)---all of which include both $\beta = 1$ and $U_{v,0}$ and reach the upper parameter ceiling.
  In VC, matching L2 to the same 23 eligible ($n_v \ge 2$) vintages used by $U_{v,H}$ removes composition drift across horizons and widens the 15-year $\mathrm{L2}-U_{v,H}$ gap from $0.019$ to $0.158$ (EW) and from $0.172$ to $0.314$ (FW).

  \item \emph{Abnormal performance ($\alpha$) and second $q^5$ factor loadings are weakly identified in the current cash-flow panel.}
  In Buyout, joint $(\alpha,\beta)$ estimation with $U_{v,0}$ shifts $\hat\beta_{\mathrm{MKT}}$ down to $0.362$ (SE $0.207$) EW and $0.439$ (SE $0.178$) FW with $\hat\alpha = 0.605\%$ and $0.568\%$ per month, along a strongly negatively correlated ridge ($\widehat{\mathrm{Corr}}(\hat\alpha,\hat\beta) = -0.78$ EW, $-0.81$ FW), while core Monte Carlo simulations show that nominal 5\% fixed-$b$ tests of $\alpha = 0$ over-reject in 12.1\% (Buyout) and 10.9\% (VC) of EW draws.
  In Venture Capital, both $U_{v,0}$ and L2 hit the $+2\%$ monthly alpha ceiling at inception; excluding the five dot-com vintages 1993--1997 eliminates the boundary solution entirely and produces stable interior estimates with negative alpha at every horizon.
  Two-factor $q^5$ specifications (pairing MKT with ME, IA, ROE, or EG) similarly exhibit large standard errors, boundary solutions (Buyout FW MKT+ME hits the return floor at $H = 0$), and sign reversals across horizons in Venture Capital.

  \item \emph{Data construction---especially constant-composition pooling and fund-size imputation---is a first-order identification choice.}
  Missing fund sizes (10 of 383 Buyout and 15 of 432 VC funds in $V \le 2010$) are heavily concentrated in small early vintages.
  Imputing the pooled cross-vintage mean (\$1{,}842M Buyout; \$379M VC) assigns up to 94.9\% weight to a single missing-size 1986 Buyout fund and 98.8\% to two missing-size 1985 VC funds, exaggerating the Buyout FW L2 0--30 year decline from $0.427$ to $0.634$ and flipping the inception $\mathrm{L2}-U$ gap from $+0.028$ to $-0.013$.
  Adopting within-vintage median imputation as our baseline preserves the full vintage panel while preventing modern mega-funds from distorting 1980s--1990s portfolios, and all results are documented alongside pooled-mean and missing-size-deletion sensitivities.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Related literature.}
This paper contributes to the semiparametric cash-flow SDF literature initiated by \citet{DLP12} and \citet{KN16}.
The \citet{DLP12} squared-NPV approach has been applied across venture capital, buyout, real estate, and human capital \citep{kroencke2013return,kim2015estimation,farrelly2019risk,lee2019new,zhao2020master,TP24}, while the \citet{KN16} public-SDF generalized PME framework has been extended across private equity, infrastructure, REITs, CLOs, impact funds, and private debt \citep{giommetti2021risk,GN18,andonov2021institutional,riddiough2022private,boyer2023discount,cordell2023clo,erel2024risk,jeffers2024risk,KN24,korteweg2024private}.
We complement \citet{GSW20} on NPV versus per-period return biases and \citet{burnside2016identification} on SDF identification by isolating the finite-pool variance tilt of squared-NPV criteria, formalizing the exact horizon rescaling mechanism, and introducing the pair-product $U_{v,H}$ estimator with fixed-$b$ increasing-domain inference \citep{PP97,NM94,JP09,JP12,KS11,KV05,LLSW18}.
Our empirical diagnostics also connect to parametric private-equity models \citep{FNP12,ACGP18}, tailored PME benchmarks \citep{HJK14,RS16}, secondary-market discount-rate risk \citep{boyer2023discount}, NAV smoothing and residual-value adjustments \citep{BGK19,JLRS20,brown2023nowcasting}, and drift-uncertainty limits on return-based alpha inference \citep{Merton80}.

\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:spatial_sdf_methodology}

We build a semiparametric framework for SDF estimation from pooled, non-traded cash flows.
Section~\ref{sec:asset_pricing_pooled} states the deal-level timing restrictions and establishes when pooled fund cash flows satisfy a conditional zero-moment condition.
Section~\ref{sec:fundwise_lmd_estimator} derives the exact horizon-rescaling identity, proves why finite-pool squared loss is biased even at inception, introduces the cross-fund pair-product estimator $U_{v,H}$, and defines our identification-robust inception moment diagnostic.
Section~\ref{sec:asymptotic_framework} presents the increasing-domain asymptotic theory and fixed-$b$ SHAC inference.
Section~\ref{sec:comparison_to_similar_estimators} positions our framework relative to \citet{DLP12}, \citet{DLP09wp}, and \citet{KN16}.

\subsection{Asset pricing for pooled cash flows}
\label{sec:asset_pricing_pooled}

Private-equity funds deploy capital across multiple quarters, so any fund-level pricing restriction aggregates deal-level cash flows initiated at different dates.
Throughout, the deal-level structure serves solely as a latent accounting device justifying fund-level moments; only pooled fund cash flows enter the estimator.
For fund $i$, let $CF_{i,t}$ denote the observed net cash flow at month $t$, and define fund inception as $t_i^0 := \min_j t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Inv}}$.

\begin{assume}[Deal-level decomposition]
\label{ass:deal_level_dgp}
For each fund $i$, there exist $J_i$ underlying deals such that:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Deal $j$ has a single negative investment cash flow $\mathrm{Inv}_{i,j} < 0$ at date $t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Inv}} \in \{0,1,\dots,T-1\}$.
  \item Deal $j$ generates a finite stream of positive divestments $(\mathrm{Div}_{i,j,k})_{k=1}^{K_{i,j}}$ with $\mathrm{Div}_{i,j,k} > 0$ and $t_{i,j,k}^{\mathrm{Div}} > t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Inv}}$.
  \item Observed fund cash flows equal the cross-deal sum $CF_{i,t} = \sum_{j=1}^{J_i} cf_{i,j,t}$ for all $i,t$, with $CF_{i,t} = 0$ for $t < t_i^0$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{assume}

Let $\Psi_t = \prod_{u=1}^t M_u$ ($\Psi_0 = 1$) denote the cumulative SDF discounting a time-$t$ cash flow to time $0$, and write $\Psi_{\tau,t} = \Psi_t / \Psi_\tau$.
Under exact deal-level pricing at acquisition, the deal-level pricing error
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:deal_pricing_error}
\delta_{i,j} := \mathrm{Inv}_{i,j} + \sum_{k=1}^{K_{i,j}} \frac{\Psi_{t_{i,j,k}^{\mathrm{Div}}}}{\Psi_{t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Inv}}}}\,\mathrm{Div}_{i,j,k}
\end{equation}
satisfies the conditional zero-moment restriction
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:deal_pricing}
\mathbb{E}\!\left[\delta_{i,j} \,\middle|\, \mathcal{F}_{t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Inv}}}\right] = 0 \qquad \forall\, i,j.
\end{equation}
Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_sdf} extends \eqref{eq:deal_pricing} to random $\mathcal{F}_u$-stopping-time entry and exit dates, characterizes when a reciprocal benchmark deflator constitutes a PE-specific SDF, and contrasts PE-estimated benchmark deflators with public-calibrated kernels \citep{HR87,HJ91,KN16,KN24}.

\begin{lemma}[Deal-level timing]
\label{lem:deal_level_timing}
Suppose Equation~\eqref{eq:deal_pricing} holds and all products below are integrable.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item If $\tau \le t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Inv}}$, then
  \begin{equation}
  \label{eq:zero_deal_expectation}
  \mathbb{E}\!\left[\frac{\Psi_{t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Inv}}}}{\Psi_\tau}\,\delta_{i,j} \,\middle|\, \mathcal{F}_\tau\right] = 0.
  \end{equation}
  \item If $\tau > t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Inv}}$, then
  \begin{equation}
  \label{eq:non_zero_deal_expectation}
  \mathbb{E}\!\left[\frac{\Psi_{t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Inv}}}}{\Psi_\tau}\,\delta_{i,j} \,\middle|\, \mathcal{F}_\tau\right]
  = \frac{\Psi_{t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Inv}}}}{\Psi_\tau}\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[\delta_{i,j} \,\middle|\, \mathcal{F}_\tau\right],
  \end{equation}
  which generally differs from zero because payoffs realized by date $\tau$ are $\mathcal{F}_\tau$-measurable.
  \item If $\tau \le t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Inv}} < t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Future}}$, compounding the deal pricing error forward to $t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Future}}$ yields the net-future-value (NFV) moment
  \[
  m_{i,j}^{\mathrm{NFV}}\!\left(\tau,t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Future}}\right)
  := \frac{\Psi_{t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Inv}}}}{\Psi_{t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Future}}}}\,\delta_{i,j}
  = \mathrm{Inv}_{i,j}\,\frac{\Psi_{t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Inv}}}}{\Psi_{t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Future}}}} + \sum_{k=1}^{K_{i,j}} \frac{\Psi_{t_{i,j,k}^{\mathrm{Div}}}}{\Psi_{t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Future}}}}\,\mathrm{Div}_{i,j,k},
  \]
  whose conditional expectation given $\mathcal{F}_\tau$ equals
  \begin{equation}
  \label{eq:future_value_bias_derivation}
  \mathbb{E}\!\left[m_{i,j}^{\mathrm{NFV}}\!\left(\tau,t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Future}}\right) \,\middle|\, \mathcal{F}_\tau\right]
  = \mathbb{E}\!\left[\mathrm{Cov}\!\left(\frac{\Psi_{t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Inv}}}}{\Psi_{t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Future}}}},\,\delta_{i,j} \,\middle|\, \mathcal{F}_{t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Inv}}}\right) \,\middle|\, \mathcal{F}_\tau\right],
  \end{equation}
  which generally contains a nonzero conditional covariance wedge whenever $\delta_{i,j}$ covaries with the forward compounding factor $\Psi_{t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Inv}}}/\Psi_{t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Future}}}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Part~(1) follows from the law of iterated expectations since $\Psi_{t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Inv}}}/\Psi_\tau$ is $\mathcal{F}_{t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Inv}}}$-measurable and $\mathbb{E}[\delta_{i,j}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Inv}}}] = 0$.
Part~(2) holds because $\Psi_{t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Inv}}}/\Psi_\tau$ is $\mathcal{F}_\tau$-measurable when $\tau > t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Inv}}$.
For Part~(3), conditioning first on $\mathcal{F}_{t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Inv}}}$ gives
\[
\mathbb{E}\!\left[\frac{\Psi_{t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Inv}}}}{\Psi_{t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Future}}}}\,\delta_{i,j} \,\middle|\, \mathcal{F}_{t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Inv}}}\right]
= \mathrm{Cov}\!\left(\frac{\Psi_{t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Inv}}}}{\Psi_{t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Future}}}},\,\delta_{i,j} \,\middle|\, \mathcal{F}_{t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Inv}}}\right)
+ \mathbb{E}\!\left[\frac{\Psi_{t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Inv}}}}{\Psi_{t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Future}}}} \,\middle|\, \mathcal{F}_{t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Inv}}}\right]
\underbrace{\mathbb{E}\!\left[\delta_{i,j} \,\middle|\, \mathcal{F}_{t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Inv}}}\right]}_{=0},
\]
and taking $\mathbb{E}[\cdot \mid \mathcal{F}_\tau]$ yields \eqref{eq:future_value_bias_derivation}.
\end{proof}

Notice that the forward compounding ratio in Part~(3) is $\Psi_{t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Inv}}}/\Psi_{t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Future}}} = \prod_{u=t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Inv}}+1}^{t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Future}}} G_u$, which compounds a time-$t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Inv}}$ dollar forward to $t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Future}}$ under the reciprocal deflator $M_u = G_u^{-1}$.
Aggregating Part~(1) across deals yields the pooled fund-level inception pricing restriction.

\begin{proposition}[Price of a pooled cash-flow stream at or before inception]
\label{theo:inception_value}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:deal_level_dgp} and Equation~\eqref{eq:deal_pricing}, if $\tau \le t_i^0$, then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:price_pooled_cash_flows}
\mathbb{E}\!\left[\sum_{t=\tau}^{T} \frac{\Psi_t}{\Psi_\tau}\,CF_{i,t} \,\middle|\, \mathcal{F}_\tau\right] = 0.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Because $\tau \le t_i^0 \le t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Inv}}$ for every deal $j$ in fund $i$, summing $\sum_t (\Psi_t/\Psi_\tau)CF_{i,t} = \sum_{j=1}^{J_i}(\Psi_{t_{i,j}^{\mathrm{Inv}}}/\Psi_\tau)\delta_{i,j}$ and applying \eqref{eq:zero_deal_expectation} term by term yields zero.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Finite-pool identification, the horizon mechanism, and the $U_{v,H}$ criterion}
\label{sec:fundwise_lmd_estimator}

For any unit $i$ (an individual fund or a vintage-year portfolio $v$) and candidate parameter vector $\theta \in \Theta \subset \mathbb{R}^p$, define the time-$\tau$ revaluation of the full cash-flow stream and its horizon average over $\mathcal{T}_{i,H} = \{t_i^0, t_i^0+1, \dots, \min(t_i^0 + 12H, T_i^{\max})\}$ ($H \ge 0$ in years) as
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:average_pricing_error}
\epsilon_{\tau,i}(\theta) := \sum_{t=0}^{T} \frac{\Psi_t(\theta)}{\Psi_\tau(\theta)}\,CF_{i,t},
\qquad
\bar\epsilon_{i,H}(\theta) := \frac{1}{|\mathcal{T}_{i,H}|} \sum_{\tau \in \mathcal{T}_{i,H}} \epsilon_{\tau,i}(\theta),
\end{equation}
with inception NPV denoted $PV_i(\theta) := \epsilon_{t_i^0,i}(\theta) = \bar\epsilon_{i,0}(\theta)$ (Figure~\ref{fig:npvs}).
The generalized Least-Mean-Distance (LMD) squared-loss estimator on vintage-year portfolios (L2 VYP) solves
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:estimator_H}
\hat\theta_{H}^{\mathrm{L2}} := \arg\min_{\theta \in \Theta} \frac{1}{V} \sum_{v=1}^{V} \bar\epsilon_{v,H}(\theta)^2
\equiv \arg\max_{\theta \in \Theta} Q_{V,H}^{\mathrm{L2}}(\theta),
\qquad
Q_{V,H}^{\mathrm{L2}}(\theta) := -\frac{1}{V}\sum_{v=1}^V \bar\epsilon_{v,H}(\theta)^2,
\end{equation}
which coincides with the identity-weighted cross-sectional GMM criterion of \citet{DLP12} at $H = 0$.

\begin{figure}[ht]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{npvs3.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{Construction of the horizon-averaged pricing error $\bar\epsilon_{i,H}(\theta)$.} Cash flows occur at dates $t$ (dots) and are revalued at each date $\tau \in \mathcal{T}_{i,H}$ (blue boxes) to form $\epsilon_{\tau,i}(\theta)$; $\bar\epsilon_{i,H}(\theta)$ averages across $\mathcal{T}_{i,H}$. By Theorem~\ref{thm:finite_pool}(T1), $\bar\epsilon_{i,H}(\theta) = A_{i,H}(\theta)\,PV_i(\theta)$.}

\end{figure}

To isolate the core mechanism before stating the general theorems, consider a single-deal unit that invests $1$ at $t=0$ and pays $G_1(\theta_0)\cdots G_T(\theta_0)\,N$ at $t=T$, where $N \ge 0$ is an idiosyncratic payoff shock independent of the factor path with $\mathbb{E}[N]=1$ and $\operatorname{Var}(N)=\sigma_N^2 > 0$.
At any horizon $H$ with $K = |\mathcal{T}_{H}|$ dates, exact factorization gives
\[
\bar\epsilon_H(\theta) = A_H(\theta)\,PV(\theta),
\qquad
A_H(\theta) = \frac{1}{K}\sum_{\tau=0}^{K-1}\frac{1}{\Psi_\tau(\theta)},
\qquad
PV(\theta) = \frac{\Psi_T(\theta)}{\Psi_T(\theta_0)}\,N - 1.
\]
Hence the population squared loss is
\[
\mathbb{E}\!\left[\bar\epsilon_H(\theta)^2 \,\middle|\, \mathcal{F}_T^{\mathrm{pub}}\right]
= A_H(\theta)^2\!\left[\left(\frac{\Psi_T(\theta)}{\Psi_T(\theta_0)} - 1\right)^2 + \sigma_N^2\,\left(\frac{\Psi_T(\theta)}{\Psi_T(\theta_0)}\right)^2\right].
\]
Differentiating with respect to $\theta$ and evaluating at $\theta = \theta_0$ (where $\Psi_T(\theta_0)/\Psi_T(\theta_0) = 1$) immediately yields
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:one_deal_gradient}
\left.\nabla_\theta\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[\bar\epsilon_H(\theta)^2 \,\middle|\, \mathcal{F}_T^{\mathrm{pub}}\right]\right|_{\theta=\theta_0}
= 2\,\sigma_N^2\,A_H(\theta_0)^2\!\left[\nabla_\theta \log \Psi_T(\theta_0) + \nabla_\theta \log A_H(\theta_0)\right].
\end{equation}
Every core phenomenon in our paper is visible in this five-line calculation.
At inception ($H = 0$), $A_0(\theta) \equiv 1$ so $\nabla_\theta \log A_0 = 0$; since $\nabla_\theta \log \Psi_T(\theta_0)$ points in the direction of lower discount rates, the gradient at $\theta_0$ is \emph{nonzero} whenever $\sigma_N^2 > 0$, tilting the inception squared-loss target toward a higher discount rate (higher $\beta$ or higher $\alpha$).
As $H$ grows, $A_H(\theta)$ compounds forward and its log-gradient $\nabla_\theta \log A_H(\theta_0)$ has the \emph{opposite} sign to $\nabla_\theta \log \Psi_T(\theta_0)$, pulling the squared-loss target downward.
For example, under a constant benchmark return $G_u \equiv 1$, $\partial_\alpha \log \Psi_T = -T$ while $\partial_\alpha \log A_H = (K-1)/2$, so the alpha gradient crosses zero at $K = 2T + 1$ valuation dates---roughly twice the deal holding period, depending on holding-period geometry rather than any universal horizon rule.
Finally, replacing squared error $\bar\epsilon_H(\theta)^2$ by a cross-product of two independent units $\bar\epsilon_{1,H}(\theta)\bar\epsilon_{2,H}(\theta)$ kills the $\sigma_N^2$ term completely: its gradient at $\theta_0$ is identically zero for every $H \ge 0$.

We now state the general finite-pool identification properties for vintage-year portfolios.
Consider $V$ vintages; vintage $v$ contains $n_v \ge 1$ funds $i \in \mathcal{I}_v$ with positive fund weights $\omega_{v,i}$ summing to $1$ ($\omega_{v,i} = 1/n_v$ under EW; $\omega_{v,i} = S_{v,i}/\sum_{j \in \mathcal{I}_v} S_{v,j}$ under FW), sharing a common vintage origin $t_v^0$ and common evaluation window $\mathcal{T}_{v,H}$.
Write the vintage portfolio cash flow as $CF_{v,t} = \sum_{i \in \mathcal{I}_v} \omega_{v,i} CF_{i,t}$, so $PV_v(\theta) = \sum_{i \in \mathcal{I}_v} \omega_{v,i} PV_i(\theta)$.
Let $\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{pub}}$ denote the public factor path and window environment.
Maintain two benchmark pricing conditions:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Condition (P) (Inception pricing):} $\mathbb{E}[PV_i(\theta_0) \mid \mathcal{F}_{t_v^0}] = 0$ for every fund $i \in \mathcal{I}_v$, as guaranteed by Proposition~\ref{theo:inception_value}.
  \item \emph{Condition (X) (Strict factor-path exogeneity):} $\mathbb{E}[PV_i(\theta_0) \mid \mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{pub}}, \mathcal{W}_v] = 0$, where $\mathcal{W}_v$ collects the vintage origin $t_v^0$, eligibility ($n_v \ge 2$), and fund sizes $\{S_{v,i}\}$.
  Condition~(X) holds automatically for multiplicative benchmark-plus-idiosyncratic deal payoffs $I_a (\prod_{u=a+1}^b G_u(\theta_0)) N$ when $\mathbb{E}[N \mid \mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{pub}}, \mathcal{W}_v] = 1$, and fails when unobserved payoff shocks correlate with the realized factor path during the holding window (as tested in Design~A$'$ in Section~\ref{sec:simulation_study}).
\end{itemize}

For vintages with $n_v \ge 2$ funds and deterministic or $\mathcal{W}_v$-measurable weights $\omega_{v,i}$, define the \emph{cross-fund pair-product criterion}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:u_criterion}
\widehat{U}_H(\theta) := \frac{\sum_{v:\, n_v \ge 2} q_v\, U_{v,H}(\theta)}{\sum_{v:\, n_v \ge 2} q_v},
\qquad
U_{v,H}(\theta) := \frac{\sum_{i \neq j \in \mathcal{I}_v} \omega_{v,i}\omega_{v,j}\,\bar\epsilon_{i,H}(\theta)\bar\epsilon_{j,H}(\theta)}{1 - \sum_{i \in \mathcal{I}_v}\omega_{v,i}^2},
\end{equation}
with main vintage weights $q_v = 1$ (equal vintage weighting; $q_v = n_v$ is examined as a precision sensitivity in the Monte Carlo study, where it over-rejects severely under fixed-$b$ inference because large late vintages dominate effective sample size).
Note the identity
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:u_identity}
U_{v,H}(\theta) = \frac{\bar\epsilon_{v,H}(\theta)^2 - \sum_{i \in \mathcal{I}_v}\omega_{v,i}^2\,\bar\epsilon_{i,H}(\theta)^2}{1 - \sum_{i \in \mathcal{I}_v}\omega_{v,i}^2}
= A_{v,H}(\theta)^2\,\frac{PV_v(\theta)^2 - \sum_{i \in \mathcal{I}_v}\omega_{v,i}^2 PV_i(\theta)^2}{1 - \sum_{i \in \mathcal{I}_v}\omega_{v,i}^2},
\end{equation}
which shows that $U_{v,H}(\theta)$ is computed in $O(n_v)$ time directly from fund-level NPVs and removes the own-fund diagonal squares $\sum_i \omega_{v,i}^2 PV_i(\theta)^2$.
Because $U_{v,H}(\theta)$ is not constrained to be nonnegative in finite samples, $\widehat{U}_H(\theta)$ can take negative values at its minimizer when sample cross-fund products are negative; a negative fitted value of $\widehat{U}_H(\hat\theta)$ is a finite-sample realization and does \emph{not} by itself establish the absence of shared vintage effects.

\begin{theorem}[Finite-pool identification of L2 and $U_{v,H}$]
\label{thm:finite_pool}
Suppose all funds in vintage $v$ share origin $t_v^0$ and evaluation set $\mathcal{T}_{v,H}$, with $\mathcal{W}_v$-measurable fund weights $\omega_{v,i} > 0$ summing to $1$, and assume the required moments exist and differentiation under the expectation is valid (proofs in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_theory}).
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[\textbf{T1}] \emph{(Exact rescaling identity.)} For every unit $i$ (or vintage portfolio $v$) and horizon $H \ge 0$,
  \[
  \bar\epsilon_{v,H}(\theta) = A_{v,H}(\theta)\,PV_v(\theta),
  \qquad
  A_{v,H}(\theta) := \frac{1}{|\mathcal{T}_{v,H}|}\sum_{\tau \in \mathcal{T}_{v,H}} \frac{\Psi_{t_v^0}(\theta)}{\Psi_\tau(\theta)}.
  \]
  \item[\textbf{T2}] \emph{(Horizon mean preservation under (X).)} Under Condition~(X), $\mathbb{E}[\bar\epsilon_{v,H}(\theta_0) \mid \mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{pub}}, \mathcal{W}_v] = 0$ and hence $\mathbb{E}[\bar\epsilon_{v,H}(\theta_0)] = 0$ for every $H \ge 0$.
  \item[\textbf{T3}] \emph{(Inception L2 variance tilt.)} At $H = 0$, the population L2 gradient at $\theta_0$ equals
  \[
  \nabla_\theta\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[PV_v(\theta)^2\right]\Big|_{\theta=\theta_0}
  = \nabla_\theta\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[\operatorname{Var}\!\left(PV_v(\theta) \,\middle|\, \mathcal{F}_{t_v^0}\right)\right]\Big|_{\theta=\theta_0}
  = 2\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[PV_v(\theta_0)\,\nabla_\theta PV_v(\theta_0)\right],
  \]
  which is generally nonzero whenever $\operatorname{Var}(PV_v(\theta)\mid \mathcal{F}_{t_v^0})$ varies with $\theta$.
  \item[\textbf{T4}] \emph{(Finite-$n_v$ L2 decomposition.)} Under Condition~(X) with conditionally independent funds of common conditional variance $\sigma_v^2(\theta) := \operatorname{Var}(PV_i(\theta)\mid \mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{pub}},\mathcal{W}_v)$ and equal weights $\omega_{v,i} = 1/n_v$,
  \[
  \mathbb{E}\!\left[\bar\epsilon_{v,H}(\theta)^2\right]
  = \mathbb{E}\!\left[A_{v,H}(\theta)^2\,\mu_v(\theta)^2\right]
  + \mathbb{E}\!\left[A_{v,H}(\theta)^2\,n_v^{-1}\,\sigma_v^2(\theta)\right],
  \]
  where $\mu_v(\theta) := \mathbb{E}[PV_i(\theta)\mid \mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{pub}},\mathcal{W}_v]$ vanishes at $\theta_0$ (under general weights, $n_v^{-1}$ is replaced by $\sum_i \omega_{v,i}^2$; keeping $n_v^{-1}$ or $\sum_i \omega_{v,i}^2$ inside the expectation accounts for random or vintage-varying fund counts).
  \item[\textbf{T5}] \emph{(Horizon gradient decomposition.)} Evaluating the L2 gradient at any $\theta$ gives
  \[
  \nabla_\theta\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[\bar\epsilon_{v,H}(\theta)^2\right]
  = \underbrace{2\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[A_{v,H}(\theta)^2\,PV_v(\theta)\,\nabla_\theta PV_v(\theta)\right]}_{\text{reweighted inception score (Fix-}A\text{)}}
  + \underbrace{2\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[A_{v,H}(\theta)\,\nabla_\theta A_{v,H}(\theta)\,PV_v(\theta)^2\right]}_{\text{Scale-Jacobian pull}}.
  \]
  \item[\textbf{T6}] \emph{(Horizon displays, does not cure, bias.)} Because both terms in T5 vanish at $\theta_0$ only when $\sigma_v^2(\theta_0)=0$, varying $H$ moves the L2 target $\theta_H^\dagger$ along a DGP-dependent curve; any horizon with $\theta_H^\dagger = \theta_0$ reflects accidental cancellation between inception variance tilt and Scale-Jacobian pull.
  \item[\textbf{T7}] \emph{(Identification of $U_{v,H}$.)} Suppose Condition~(X) holds, funds $i \neq j \in \mathcal{I}_v$ are conditionally independent given $(\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{pub}}, \mathcal{W}_v)$ with no shared vintage shock ($c_v = 0$), and the weighted pair mean satisfies $\mathbb{E}[PV_i(\theta)PV_j(\theta)\mid \mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{pub}},\mathcal{W}_v] = \mu_{v,i}(\theta)\mu_{v,j}(\theta)$ with $\mu_{v,i}(\theta_0)=0$. Then for any $\mathcal{W}_v$-measurable vintage weights $q_v > 0$,
  \[
  \mathbb{E}\!\left[q_v\,U_{v,H}(\theta)\right] = \mathbb{E}\!\left[q_v\,A_{v,H}(\theta)^2\,\frac{\sum_{i \neq j}\omega_{v,i}\omega_{v,j}\,\mu_{v,i}(\theta)\mu_{v,j}(\theta)}{1 - \sum_i \omega_{v,i}^2}\right]
  \]
  (reducing to $\mathbb{E}[q_v A_{v,H}(\theta)^2 \mu_v(\theta)^2] \ge 0$ when $\mu_{v,i}(\theta)=\mu_v(\theta)$), which attains its global minimum $0$ at $\theta_0$ for every $H \ge 0$. Provided $\theta_0$ is the unique minimizer on $\Theta$ and the uniform convergence conditions of Section~\ref{sec:asymptotic_framework} hold, $\hat\theta_H^U := \arg\min_{\theta \in \Theta} \widehat{U}_H(\theta) \xrightarrow{p} \theta_0$ for every fixed $H \ge 0$ under bounded $n_v \ge 2$.
  \item[\textbf{T8}] \emph{(Shared vintage shocks, $c_v \neq 0$.)} If fund pricing errors contain an additive or multiplicative common vintage component $c_v$ orthogonal to idiosyncratic fund noise with conditional variance $\tau_v^2(\theta) := \operatorname{Var}(c_v(\theta)\mid \mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{pub}},\mathcal{W}_v) > 0$, then
  \[
  \mathbb{E}\!\left[q_v\,U_{v,H}(\theta)\right] = \mathbb{E}\!\left[q_v\,A_{v,H}(\theta)^2\left(\mu_v(\theta)^2 + \tau_v^2(\theta)\right)\right].
  \]
  Hence $U_{v,H}$ removes idiosyncratic fund noise completely, while any remaining shared vintage variance $\tau_v^2(\theta)$ induces the same two-regime horizon behavior as in T5 (an inception tilt mitigated or reversed by the Scale-Jacobian pull at longer $H$).
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{Admissible parameter domain $\Theta$.}
Because the reciprocal SDF $M_u(\theta) = G_u(\theta)^{-1}$ requires strictly positive gross benchmark returns $G_u(\theta) = 1 + r_{f,u} + \alpha + \beta^\top F_u$ at every month $u$ spanned by the sample cash flows, we define the compact admissible domain
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:admissible_domain}
\Theta := \left\{\theta = (\alpha,\beta) \in [-0.02, 0.02] \times [-1, 4]^{p_\beta} \;:\; \min_{u \in \mathcal{U}} G_u(\theta) \ge \underline{G}\right\},
\end{equation}
with $\underline{G} = 0.08$ (monthly gross return floor) and box bounds $\alpha \in [-2\%,+2\%]$ per month (preventing the ``exploding alpha'' boundary degeneracy of \citealp[Footnote~8]{DLP12}; cf.\ Figure~\ref{fig:exploding_alpha}) and $\beta_m \in [-1,4]$.
Whenever a minimizer lies on an active boundary of $\Theta$, standard interior sandwich errors are invalid and are suppressed (marked with $^{\dagger}$ in all tables).

\paragraph{Identification-robust inception moment diagnostic (Anderson--Rubin sets).}
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite_pool}(T8) shows that $U_{v,H}$ remains unbiased at finite $n_v$ when $c_v = 0$, whereas shared vintage shocks ($\tau_v^2 > 0$) or failures of strict exogeneity~(X) can bias both L2 and $U_{v,H}$.
By contrast, the linear inception pricing moment
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:iv_moment}
\bar{g}_H(\theta) := \frac{1}{V}\sum_{v=1}^{V} z_{v,H}\,PV_v(\theta),
\qquad
z_{v,H} := \frac{A_{v,H}(1)}{A_{v,0}(1)},
\end{equation}
equals the simple mean inception NPV $V^{-1}\sum_v PV_v(\theta)$ at $H = 0$ ($z_{v,0} \equiv 1$), which has exact mean zero under Condition~(P) alone---even when $c_v \neq 0$ and even when (X) fails (as verified in Design~A$'$ below).
However, solving $\bar{g}_0(\beta) = 0$ for a point estimate is fragile for two reasons.
First, the nonlinear pricing moment can admit multiple roots on $\Theta$---for instance, even in a one-period model with risk-free gross return $1$ and equally likely market excess returns $\{-0.4, +0.6\}$, both the structural $\beta_0 = 1$ and the public growth-optimal root $\beta^\ast = 5/12$ satisfy $\mathbb{E}[PV(\beta)] = 0$ (Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_sdf})---so selecting the smallest root has no general identification justification.
Second, in finite samples the empirical moment can lose all roots on $\Theta$ or exhibit near-zero slope, invalidating Wald standard errors and delete-one jackknifes.

To avoid root selection and weak-slope division, we invert the centered Bartlett fixed-$b$ moment test \citep{AR49,SW00,KV05} across all $\beta \in \Theta$: at each candidate $\beta$, let $s_v(\beta) = z_{v,H} PV_v(\beta)$, form the centered long-run variance $\hat\Omega_g(\beta) = V^{-1}\sum_{v,w} K(|y_v - y_w|/S)\,(s_v(\beta)-\bar{g}_H(\beta))(s_w(\beta)-\bar{g}_H(\beta))$, and collect all $\beta \in \Theta$ satisfying
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ar_set}
\left|\frac{\sqrt{V}\,\bar{g}_H(\beta)}{\sqrt{\hat\Omega_g(\beta)}}\right| \le c_{0.05}^{\mathrm{fb}}(b),
\qquad
b = \frac{S}{V},
\end{equation}
with interval endpoints refined numerically via root finding.
Two properties must be kept distinct: (i) the test \eqref{eq:ar_set} is fully robust to multiple roots, zero-root samples, and weak moment slopes; (ii) its finite-$V$ coverage under cross-vintage persistence still reflects the small-$V$ Bartlett approximation (Section~\ref{sec:simulation_study}), so we present the resulting sets as a nominal, calibrated-by-design diagnostic alongside $U_{v,0}$.

\subsection{Increasing-domain asymptotics and fixed-$b$ SHAC inference}
\label{sec:asymptotic_framework}

Let $\mathcal{L}_{v,H}(\theta)$ denote the vintage-$v$ criterion contribution: $\bar\epsilon_{v,H}(\theta)^2$ for L2 VYP, or $q_v U_{v,H}(\theta)$ for the pair-product estimator on eligible vintages ($n_v \ge 2$).
We work in an increasing-domain vintage-year framework ($V \to \infty$ with bounded fund counts $n_v$ and bounded fund lifetimes), so new vintages bring new aggregate macroeconomic realizations while overlap dependence across vintages remains local \citep{PP97,NM94,JP09,JP12}.
Let $\theta_H^\dagger := \arg\min_{\theta \in \Theta} \lim_{V \to \infty} V^{-1}\sum_{v=1}^V \mathbb{E}[\mathcal{L}_{v,H}(\theta)]$ denote the population criterion target (equal to $\theta_0$ for $U_{v,H}$ under Theorem~\ref{thm:finite_pool}(T7), and generally distinct from $\theta_0$ for L2).

\begin{proposition}[Consistency and asymptotic normality]
\label{prop:consistency_clt}
Under the increasing-domain regularity, bounded-moment, local-dependence, and unique-interior-identification conditions in Appendix~\ref{sec:proofs_asymptotics_H} (Assumption~\ref{ass:asymptotics_H}):
\begin{enumerate}
  \item $\hat\theta_H \xrightarrow{p} \theta_H^\dagger$ as $V \to \infty$ (with $\theta_H^\dagger = \theta_0$ for $U_{v,H}$ under Theorem~\ref{thm:finite_pool}(T7)).
  \item $\sqrt{V}\left(\hat\theta_H - \theta_H^\dagger\right) \overset{d}{\longrightarrow} \mathcal{N}\!\left(0, \mathcal{H}_H^{-1}\Lambda_H\mathcal{H}_H^{-1}\right)$, where $\mathcal{H}_H := \lim_{V\to\infty} V^{-1}\sum_{v=1}^V \mathbb{E}[\nabla_{\theta\theta}\mathcal{L}_{v,H}(\theta_H^\dagger)]$ and $\Lambda_H := \lim_{V\to\infty} \operatorname{Var}\!\left(V^{-1/2}\sum_{v=1}^V \nabla_\theta\mathcal{L}_{v,H}(\theta_H^\dagger)\right)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

Appendix~\ref{sec:proofs_asymptotics_H} gives the complete proof using score centering and the exact integrated Hessian $\bar{\mathcal{H}}_V = \int_0^1 \nabla_{\theta\theta}\widehat{Q}_{V,H}(\theta_H^\dagger + u(\hat\theta_H - \theta_H^\dagger))\,du$, which holds for vector parameters without requiring a scalar mean-value point per coordinate.

For inference in finite $V$ ($V = 25$ Buyout, $V = 23$ or $27$ VC), let $s_{v,H}(\theta) := \nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}_{v,H}(\theta)$ be the vintage-$v$ score, centered at the sample mean $\tilde{s}_{v,H} := s_{v,H}(\hat\theta_H) - \bar{s}_H(\hat\theta_H)$, and estimate $\Lambda_H$ with the Bartlett spatial HAC (SHAC) kernel \citep{KS11} over vintage-year calendar distances $d_{v,w} = |y_v - y_w|$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:shac_H}
\hat\Lambda_H = \frac{1}{V}\sum_{v=1}^V\sum_{w=1}^V K\!\left(\frac{|y_v - y_w|}{S}\right)\tilde{s}_{v,H}\tilde{s}_{w,H}^\top,
\qquad
K(x) = \max(1 - |x|, 0),
\qquad
S = \left\lceil 1.3\sqrt{V} \right\rceil,
\end{equation}
following the \citet{LLSW18} bandwidth rule ($S = 7$ years for $V \in \{23, 25, 27\}$).
Standard errors follow from the sandwich matrix $\widehat{\mathrm{Avar}}(\hat\theta_H) = V^{-1}\hat{\mathcal{H}}_H^{-1}\hat\Lambda_H\hat{\mathcal{H}}_H^{-1}$.
Because $b = S/V \approx 0.26$--$0.30$ is non-negligible in our panels, normal critical values ($1.96$) severely understate uncertainty (rejecting 13.3\%--14.1\% even under independent Gaussian draws; Table~\ref{tab:mc_size}).
We therefore compute all $p$-values and 95\% confidence bands using \citet{KV05} fixed-$b$ critical values ($c_{0.05}^{\mathrm{fb}} = 2.790$ for $V=25$, $b=0.280$; $2.867$ for $V=23$, $b=0.304$; $2.728$ for $V=27$, $b=0.259$).
We also cross-check our fixed-$b$ intervals against a four-year overlapping vintage-block bootstrap (Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_empirical_extra}, Table~\ref{tab:block_bootstrap}).

\label{sec:comparison_to_similar_estimators}

Table~\ref{tab:comparison} contrasts our framework with \citet{DLP12}, \citet{DLP09wp}, and \citet{KN16}.
At the objective level, L2 VYP at $H = 0$ nests \citet{DLP12} exactly, while Theorem~\ref{thm:finite_pool} reveals why their portfolio-infill consistency ($n_v \to \infty$ inside fixed vintages) breaks down at finite $n_v$ and how $U_{v,H}$ restores identification under vintage-year increasing-domain asymptotics ($V \to \infty$, bounded $n_v \ge 2$).

\section{Data, implementation, and Monte Carlo evidence}
\label{sec:empirical_application}

\subsection{Private-equity sample and constant-composition pooling}
\label{sec:data}

We use the Preqin cash-flow dataset as of April 14, 2022, restricted to North American, USD-denominated Buyout (BO) and Venture Capital (VC) funds to match the U.S.\ public equity factors.
To prevent survivor and reporting-horizon distortions, we build \emph{constant-composition} vintage-year portfolios (VYPs): every fund entering a vintage portfolio must have a complete cash-flow and NAV history from inception through liquidation or the sample end date (December 31, 2021), dropping funds whose Preqin reporting truncates early with positive unliquidated NAV.
All fund cash flows and public factor returns are aligned on a monthly calendar grid anchored at each fund's (or vintage portfolio's) first observed cash flow ($t_v^0$), and for non-liquidated funds the final reported NAV is booked as a terminal distribution.

In our baseline seasoned sample (vintages up to 2010, selected to limit reliance on unrealized NAVs as discussed in Section~\ref{sec:model_selection}), the complete-history filter yields \textbf{383 Buyout funds across 25 vintages} (1985--2010; 1989 has no complete-history BO funds) and \textbf{432 Venture Capital funds across 27 vintages} (1982--2010; 1983--1984 absent).
Every Buyout vintage has at least two funds ($n_v \ge 2$), so L2 and $U_{v,H}$ share identical 25-vintage support in Buyout.
In Venture Capital, four early vintages (1982, 1988, 1989, and 1991) contain a single fund ($n_v = 1$), so $U_{v,H}$ operates on the \textbf{23 eligible vintages} with $n_v \ge 2$ while full-sample L2 uses all 27 vintages.
Wherever we compare $\mathrm{L2}$ against $U_{v,H}$ in VC, we report both full-sample (27-vintage) and matched-vintage (23-vintage) L2 estimates so that estimator differences are not confounded by vintage-composition changes.
To test sample-selection sensitivity, we also estimate every model on (i) an \emph{all-funds} sample that retains truncated histories (495 BO / 538 VC funds) and (ii) a \emph{20\% terminal-NAV-threshold} sample that additionally drops any fund whose final reported NAV exceeds 20\% of total value (328 BO / 344 VC funds; Table~\ref{tab:robustness_samples}).

\subsection{Fund-size imputation}
\label{sec:fund_size_imputation}

Fund-size weighted (FW) portfolios weight each fund $i$ in vintage $v$ by its reported fund size $S_{v,i}$.
In Preqin, fund size is missing for \textbf{10 of 383 Buyout funds} (2.6\%) and \textbf{15 of 432 Venture Capital funds} (3.5\%) in the baseline $V \le 2010$ sample.
Crucially, these missing sizes are not spread uniformly across time: they are heavily concentrated in the smallest, earliest vintages (1985--1995 in Buyout; 1982--1994 in VC), when typical fund sizes were an order of magnitude smaller than in the 2000s and 2010s.

This vintage concentration makes naive cross-vintage imputation highly consequential.
Imputing missing fund sizes with the pooled cross-vintage strategy mean (\$1{,}842.24M for Buyout over 1985--2021; \$379.43M for VC over 1982--2021) assigns massive mechanical weight to individual early funds whose true sizes were far smaller: a single missing-size 1986 Buyout fund receives \textbf{94.9\%} of its entire vintage portfolio, two missing-size 1992 Buyout funds receive \textbf{92.8\%}, two missing-size 1988 Buyout funds receive \textbf{51.5\%}, two missing-size 1985 VC funds receive \textbf{98.8\%}, and two missing-size 1986 VC funds receive \textbf{87.3\%}.
(For the singleton 1982 VC fund, any positive imputed size yields 100\% weight in that vintage and leaves its L2 portfolio unchanged.)

To avoid letting modern mega-fund averages distort 1980s and early-1990s vintage portfolios, our baseline FW construction uses \textbf{within-vintage median imputation}: each missing fund size is replaced by the median observed fund size in the same strategy and vintage year, expanding symmetrically to the nearest adjacent vintages with observed sizes when a vintage has no reported sizes (applicable only to singleton 1982 VC, which receives the 1985 median of \$9M).
Table~\ref{tab:fw_imputation_sensitivity} in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_empirical_extra} compares all three rules---within-vintage median (baseline), pooled cross-vintage mean, and dropping missing-size funds---with explicit vintage counts ($V_{\mathrm{L2}}/V_U$) and matched-vintage gaps:
\begin{itemize}
  \item In Buyout FW, the headline inception $U_{v,0}$ estimate is robust across all three rules ($1.197$ median, $1.205$ pooled mean, $1.226$ drop).
  By contrast, the L2 horizon profile responds substantially: under the pooled mean, Buyout FW L2 falls by $0.634$ from inception ($1.192$) to 30 years ($0.558$) and exhibits a slightly negative inception $\mathrm{L2}-U$ gap ($-0.013$).
  Switching to within-vintage median imputation cuts the 0--30 year L2 decline by a third to $0.427$ ($1.224 \to 0.798$, closely matching the $0.419$ decline when dropping missing-size funds) and restores a positive inception $\mathrm{L2}-U$ gap ($+0.028$, versus $+0.042$ on matched 22-vintage drop support).
  Note that dropping missing-size funds shrinks Buyout $U_{v,H}$ from 25 to 22 vintages while L2 retains 25, underscoring why matched-support comparison is essential.
  \item In Venture Capital FW, the pattern is preserved across all three rules: $U_{v,0}$ ranges from $1.649$ (mean) to $1.742$ (median) and $1.807$ (drop), and the large positive inception $\mathrm{L2}-U$ gap ($+0.440$ to $+0.557$) and steep L2 horizon collapse persist throughout.
  \item Crucially, log fund-size dispersion under within-vintage median imputation is $\mathrm{SD}(\log S) = 1.185$ (BO) and $0.941$ (VC), virtually identical to the values calibrated in our FW Monte Carlo Design~B ($\sigma_{\log S} = 1.19$ BO, $0.90$ VC), so our FW simulation calibration remains directly representative.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{SDF specification, public factors, terminal NAVs, and cross-validation}
\label{sec:model_selection}

Following \citet{DLP12}, our baseline SDF is the monthly reciprocal linear benchmark deflator
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:linear_sdf}
\Psi_{\tau,t}^{\mathrm{SL}}(\theta)
= \frac{\prod_{h=1}^{\tau}\left(1 + \alpha + r_{f,h} + \sum_m \beta_m F_{m,h}\right)}{\prod_{h=1}^{t}\left(1 + \alpha + r_{f,h} + \sum_m \beta_m F_{m,h}\right)},
\end{equation}
using the U.S.\ $q^5$ investment-factor dataset (\url{http://global-q.org/factors.html}; \citealp{HXZ15,hou2021augmented}) through December 31, 2024: market excess return (MKT), size (ME), investment (IA), return on equity (ROE), and expected growth (EG).
To limit overfitting, we focus on six parsimonious specifications: single-factor \{MKT\} and two-factor \{MKT + $\alpha$, ME, IA, ROE, or EG\}, minimized over the admissible domain $\Theta$ of \eqref{eq:admissible_domain} across the horizon grid $H \in \{0, 1/12, 2.5, 5, 10, 12.5, 15, 17.5, 20, 22.5, 25, 27.5, 30\}$ years.

\paragraph{Terminal NAV shares, haircuts, and the 2010 vintage cutoff.}
Treating a non-liquidated fund's final NAV as a terminal cash flow substitutes a reported valuation for a realized exit.
In our seasoned $V \le 2010$ sample, vintage-level booked terminal NAV shares---summing each fund's own final reported NAV over total distributions plus terminal NAV---are modest in older cohorts but rise in 2006--2010: the median vintage share is $8.1\%$ (BO EW), $5.4\%$ (BO FW), $10.8\%$ (VC EW), and $8.1\%$ (VC FW), with 2006--2010 means of $12.2\% / 10.7\%$ in Buyout and $27.1\% / 24.2\%$ in VC (Table~\ref{tab:terminal_nav_shares} and Figure~\ref{fig:terminal_nav_shares} in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_empirical_extra}).
Across five constant terminal-NAV haircuts from 0\% (full NAV) to 100\% (complete write-off), single-factor L2 market betas shift smoothly and modestly at $H=0$: from $1.283$ to $1.170$ (BO EW), $1.224$ to $1.133$ (BO FW), $1.925$ to $1.877$ (VC EW), and $2.287$ to $2.245$ (VC FW; Figure~\ref{fig:nav_disc_BOVC_MKT}).
Writing off terminal NAV mechanically lowers total inflows and therefore requires a lower discount rate ($\hat\beta$) to match the reduced payoffs; the five-treatment span provides a transparent uncertainty envelope without imposing a parametric residual-value model \citep{JLRS20,brown2023nowcasting}.
Extending the maximum vintage cutoff from 2010 to 2021 produces only modest movements at inception ($H=0$), but unseasoned post-2010 NAVs still distort longer compounding horizons; keeping the 2010 cutoff ensures that our baseline panels consist predominantly of realized cash flows.

\paragraph{$hv$-block cross-validation as a stability diagnostic.}
To complement fixed-$b$ asymptotic inference, we compute rolling $hv$-block cross-validation \citep{R00} with 3-year validation blocks buffered by 3-year exclusion zones on each side (8 folds in $V \le 2010$).
Because held-out folds are not independent draws from the full-sample estimator's sampling distribution, fold dispersion $\mathrm{SD}_{CV}$ and the stability ratio $\mathrm{SR}_{CV} = \hat\theta/\mathrm{SD}_{CV}$ serve strictly as \emph{descriptive stability diagnostics} rather than inferential standard errors.
To compare horizons on a common scale, we evaluate every horizon's fold estimates on the same held-out $H=0$ inception loss (Table~\ref{tab:appendix_e_cv_folds} in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_empirical_extra}): held-out $H=0$ loss is minimized at $H=0$ for Buyout FW ($0.117$), VC EW ($0.303$), and VC FW ($0.375$), and is essentially flat in Buyout EW ($0.109$ at $H=0$ vs.\ $0.106$ at $H=20$).

\subsection{Monte Carlo evidence}
\label{sec:simulation_study}

We evaluate estimator accuracy, horizon dynamics, and fixed-$b$ test calibration across 55 data-shaped Monte Carlo designs (1,246 estimator-horizon cells, 1,000 replications in all core and hardening EW/FW designs) using common deal draws across estimators and horizons.
In the two \emph{core designs}, we match the exact Preqin Buyout (25 vintages, 383 funds) and VC (27 vintages, 432 funds) calendars and fund counts through 2010, the historical U.S.\ $q^5$ factor path, 15 deals per fund with quarterly cash flows, and exact pricing at $\beta_{\mathrm{MKT}} = 1$ ($\alpha = 0$) with mean-one lognormal multiplicative deal shocks calibrated to match empirical within-vintage cross-sectional dispersion (monthly deal volatility $\sigma = 0.130$ Buyout, $0.154$ VC).
Because deal shocks are multiplicative and mean-one, both Condition~(P) and Condition~(X) hold exactly in the core designs (unlike additive shocks with default truncation, which bias expected payoffs upward; see Appendix~\ref{sec:dlp_simulation}).

\input{../../r_project/results/mc_step4/paper/table_S1_core.tex}

Table~\ref{tab:mc_core} summarizes the core single-factor results and delivers four sharp conclusions:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{Individual-fund L2 is severely biased across horizons} ($+0.249$ at $H=0$, $-0.190$ BO / $-0.370$ VC at $H=30$), rejecting $\beta=1$ in up to $79.6\%$ of draws.
  \item \emph{Vintage-portfolio L2 reduces but does not eliminate finite-pool bias.} At $H=0$, L2 VYP is biased upward by $+0.075$ (Buyout) and $+0.177$ (VC), turns slightly negative at 15 years ($-0.007$ / $-0.028$), and drops to $-0.111$ (Buyout) and $-0.375$ (VC) at 30 years.
  Crucially, we reserve ``size'' for a test of a valid null: L2 VYP rejects the structural value $\beta=1$ in $4.1\%$ (Buyout) and $6.3\%$ (VC) of draws at $H=0$ only because positive target shift and negative finite-sample bias partially offset; against its own criterion target $\theta_0^\dagger$, L2 VYP rejects in $13.1\%$ (Buyout) and $34.9\%$ (VC) of draws (Table~\ref{tab:mc_hardening}).
  \item \emph{Equal-vintage $U_{v,H}$ ($q_v=1$) eliminates the finite-pool bias at every horizon and attains its lowest RMSE at $H = 0$.}
  At $H = 0$, $U_{v,0}$ has near-zero bias ($-0.007$ Buyout, $-0.017$ VC), the lowest RMSE among inference-calibrated estimators ($0.084$ Buyout, $0.107$ VC---less than half L2 VYP's $0.296$ in VC), and near-nominal fixed-$b$ rejection ($4.6\%$ Buyout, $6.4\%$ VC).
  Increasing $H$ leaves $U_{v,H}$ nearly unbiased while monotonically inflating its standard deviation ($0.084 \to 0.186$ Buyout; $0.106 \to 0.273$ VC).
  \item \emph{Fund-count vintage weighting ($q_v = n_v$) improves point RMSE but ruins fixed-$b$ size.}
  Because late 2005--2008 vintages hold far more funds than 1985--1995 vintages, $q_v = n_v$ concentrates effective sample size in a handful of overlapping vintages and rejects $\beta = 1$ in $20.3\%$ (Buyout) and $20.0\%$ (VC) of draws at $H = 0$ (bracketed in Table~\ref{tab:mc_core}).
  We therefore use equal vintage weights ($q_v = 1$) for all empirical inference.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Two-factor $q^5$ models and joint $(\alpha,\beta)$ calibration.}
Table~\ref{tab:mc_two_factor} evaluates two-factor models pairing MKT ($\beta_{\mathrm{MKT}}=1$) with each non-market $q^5$ factor ($\beta_{\mathrm{2nd}}=0.5$).
Two important nuances emerge.
First, unlike single-factor $\beta$ and joint $(\alpha,\beta)$---where $H = 0$ minimizes $U_{v,H}$ RMSE---in two-factor models $H = 15$ years lowers $U_{v,H}$'s \emph{second-loading} RMSE for ME ($1.29 \to 0.49$, cutting boundary hits from $11.5\%$ to $0.6\%$), IA ($0.76 \to 0.27$), and ROE ($1.15 \to 0.25$), while \emph{market-beta} RMSE rises in ROE ($0.083 \to 0.152$).

\paragraph{Hardening designs: FW sizes, path-correlated shocks, and large-$V$ studentization.}
Appendix Tables~\ref{tab:mc_size}--\ref{tab:mc_hardening} and Figure~\ref{fig:mc_growing_v} test inference under harder departures from the core design:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Heterogeneous fund sizes (Design~B, Table~\ref{tab:mc_hardening}, Panel~A):} Drawing lognormal fund sizes matching Preqin dispersion ($\sigma_{\log S} = 1.19$ BO, $0.90$ VC) leaves FW $U_{v,0}$ nearly unbiased ($-0.013$ BO, $-0.018$ VC) with mild over-rejection at $H = 0$ ($8.1\%$ BO, $8.5\%$ VC), which we flag explicitly as a qualification on FW single-factor markers in Table~\ref{tab:u_l2_beta}.
  \item \emph{Path-correlated shocks (Designs~A$'$ and~A, Table~\ref{tab:mc_hardening}, Panels~B--C):} When deal payoffs depend on realized factor paths in a way that preserves inception pricing (P) while breaking strict exogeneity (X) (Design~A$'$), both L2 and $U_{v,H}$ remain biased as $V$ grows from $25$ to $100$ (bias $+0.15$ to $+0.30$), whereas the $H = 0$ inception moment (IV) approaches $\beta_0 = 1$ (Wald rejection $9.0\%$--$10.6\%$ at $V=100$; at $H=15$ yr the path-dependent instrument $z_{v,15}$ also fails (X) and rejects $32.2\%$--$33.8\%$).
  At the same time, replaying our centered Bartlett fixed-$b$ AR moment test \eqref{eq:ar_set} at true $\beta = 1$ gives $4.0\% / 3.4\%$ rejection in the independent BO/VC cores, $9.4\% / 11.1\%$ under persistent AR(1) M4 shocks ($\rho_W = 0.6$, $c/2c$), and $21.8\% / 9.8\%$ in Design~A$'$ ($V = 25 / 100$)---confirming that root robustness and finite-$V$ persistence coverage are distinct.
  \item \emph{Growing-$V$ studentization (Table~\ref{tab:mc_size_additions}):} On resampled factor paths even at $V = 400$, equal-vintage $U_{v,H}$ still rejects in $6.8\%$--$10.8\%$ of draws with low effective sample size ($N_{\mathrm{eff}} = 13$--$27$) and a marked left-tail excess; replacing the random sandwich denominator with the Monte Carlo SD removes that excess, diagnosing studentization distortion from heavy-tailed compounded weights \citep[cf.][]{Martin12} rather than cross-vintage dependence.
  \item \emph{Reconciliation with \citet{DLP12} (Appendix~\ref{sec:dlp_simulation}):} Within our simulator, Table~\ref{tab:mc_grid} isolates the $n_v \times V$ infill noise effect; the remaining gap between our scenario~(S3) and Table~1 of \citet{DLP12} also reflects their simulated market and return-linked shifted additive lognormal shock specification (Table~\ref{tab:driessen_comparison}).
\end{itemize}


\section{Empirical results for Buyout and Venture Capital}
\label{sec:empirical_results_bovc}

We now apply the framework to the North American USD complete-history Preqin panel ($V \le 2010$), leading with equal-vintage $U_{v,H}$ ($q_v = 1$) at inception ($H = 0$) as our headline estimator and using L2 VYP, the horizon profile, and the centered fixed-$b$ AR moment sets as structured diagnostics.
All code, scripts, and replication logs are available at \url{https://github.com/quant-unit/Fundwise_SDF/tree/master/r_project}.

\subsection{Single-factor market exposure: $U_{v,H}$ and L2 across horizons}
\label{sec:empirical_single_bovc}

Shaded bands around $U_{v,H}$ represent pointwise 95\% fixed-$b$ confidence intervals ($\pm 2.790\,\mathrm{SE}$ for Buyout, $\pm 2.867\,\mathrm{SE}$ for VC), matching the table inference; triangles plot $U_{v,H}$ plus the expected $\mathrm{L2} - U_{v,H}$ estimator gap from our core Monte Carlo designs without shared vintage shocks.

\paragraph{Buyout.}
At inception ($H = 0$), our headline $U_{v,0}$ estimate is $\hat\beta_{\mathrm{MKT}} = 1.147$ (SE $0.118$) under EW and $1.197$ (SE $0.143$) under FW.
Both estimates are sharply above zero ($p < 0.001$; note the mild FW finite-sample over-rejection of $8.1\%$ in Design~B), yet neither rejects unit market beta $\beta_{\mathrm{MKT}} = 1$ ($p_1 = 0.325$ EW, $p_1 = 0.280$ FW).
As the horizon expands from $0$ to $10$ years, $U_{v,H}$ remains remarkably flat ($1.147$, $1.154$, $1.158$ EW; $1.197$, $1.198$, $1.213$ FW), before drifting modestly upward to $1.268$ / $1.379$ at 15 years and $1.309$ / $1.428$ at 30 years.

The squared-loss L2 profile behaves in the exact opposite direction predicted by Theorem~\ref{thm:finite_pool}(T3--T6): L2 starts above $U_{v,0}$ at inception ($1.283$ EW, $1.224$ FW) and falls monotonically to $1.108$ (EW) and $0.989$ (FW) at 15 years and $0.933$ (EW) and $0.798$ (FW) at 30 years.
Appendix Table~\ref{tab:fixA_jacobian} decomposes this L2 drop directly using the Fix-$A$ vs.\ Scale-Jacobian split of Theorem~\ref{thm:finite_pool}(T5): holding the horizon weight $A_{v,H}$ fixed at $\beta_{\mathrm{ref}} = \hat\beta_{U,0}$ leaves Buyout L2 virtually flat from $0$ to $30$ years ($1.283 \to 1.266$ EW; $1.224 \to 1.106$ FW), showing that almost the entire L2 decline ($-0.334$ EW, $-0.308$ FW) is mechanical Scale-Jacobian pull from differentiating $A_{v,H}(\beta)^2$.

\paragraph{Venture Capital.}
At inception ($H = 0$), $U_{v,0}$ estimates a substantially higher market exposure than Buyout: $\hat\beta_{\mathrm{MKT}} = 1.493$ (SE $0.325$) EW and $1.742$ (SE $0.342$) FW.
Again, both reject $\beta = 0$ at the 1\% level (with the Design~B FW qualification of $8.5\%$) while remaining statistically indistinguishable from $\beta = 1$ ($p_1 = 0.247$ EW, $p_1 = 0.116$ FW) due to VC's much higher cross-vintage dispersion.
In contrast to Buyout, VC L2 starts much higher than $U_{v,0}$ at inception ($1.925$ EW, $2.287$ FW---an observed gap of $+0.432$ EW and $+0.546$ FW, roughly twice the core Monte Carlo expectation) and plunges to negative values at 30 years ($-0.373$ EW, $-0.573$ FW).
The Fix-$A$ decomposition (Table~\ref{tab:fixA_jacobian}) is dramatic: holding $A_{v,H}$ fixed moves VC L2 slightly \emph{upward} to $2.256$ (EW) and $2.680$ (FW) at 30 years, proving that the Scale-Jacobian pull accounts for $-2.629$ (EW) and $-3.253$ (FW) of the collapse.
Meanwhile, VC $U_{v,H}$ itself exhibits a hump at 5 years ($1.992$ EW, $2.290$ FW) and declines toward $0.760$ (EW) and $-0.076$ (FW) by 30 years, indicating either shared vintage-level shocks ($c_v \neq 0$, T8) or non-constant risk exposure across the 1990s dot-com boom and 2000s cohorts (confirmed by the vintage-level residual plot in Appendix Figure~\ref{fig:vintage_residuals}).

\paragraph{Robustness across bootstrap intervals and sample definitions.}
Four-year overlapping vintage-block bootstrap 95\% percentile intervals (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:block_bootstrap}) broadly corroborate the fixed-$b$ intervals at $H = 0$ for BO EW ($[0.929, 1.508]$ vs.\ $[0.818, 1.476]$), VC EW ($[0.745, 1.996]$ vs.\ $[0.562, 2.425]$), and VC FW ($[0.707, 2.284]$ vs.\ $[0.762, 2.722]$), while flagging upper-tail sensitivity in BO FW $U_{v,0}$ ($4.6\%$ of block-bootstrap draws hit the upper bound $3.994$, yielding $[1.036, 3.994]$ vs.\ fixed-$b$ $[0.799, 1.595]$).
Across the three sample definitions (main complete histories, all-funds including truncated histories, and the 20\% terminal-NAV-threshold sample; Appendix Table~\ref{tab:robustness_samples}), single-factor $U_{v,0}$ betas remain stable in both Buyout and Venture Capital.

\subsection{Mean pricing moment, matched-vintage gaps, and AR confidence sets}
\label{sec:empirical_iv}

Table~\ref{tab:iv_comparison} compares $U_{v,H}$ against full-sample L2, reports the observed $\mathrm{L2} - U_{v,H}$ gaps alongside core Monte Carlo expectations, and presents the inception and horizon-weighted IV moment diagnostics.

\input{../../r_project/results/figures_bovc_us_g1/iv_comparison.tex}

Three diagnostic conclusions follow from Table~\ref{tab:iv_comparison}:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{Matched-vintage support matters in Venture Capital.}
  Because full-sample VC L2 includes four singleton vintages ($n_v = 1$) excluded from $U_{v,H}$, comparing full-sample L2 to $U_{v,H}$ mixes estimator differences with vintage composition.
  Computing L2 on the exact same 23 eligible ($n_v \ge 2$) vintages (shown in brackets) leaves the inception gap virtually unchanged ($+0.435$ EW, $+0.551$ FW) but widens the 5-year and 15-year $\mathrm{L2} - U_{v,H}$ gaps substantially (at 15 years, from $0.019$ to $0.158$ EW and from $0.172$ to $0.314$ FW).
  \item \emph{Observed $\mathrm{L2} - U_{v,H}$ gaps exceed the pure-idiosyncratic core benchmark at long horizons.}
  In Buyout EW, the inception gap $+0.136$ (LOO jackknife SE $0.062$) is broadly consistent with the core Monte Carlo prediction $+0.082$, whereas by 30 years the observed gap reaches $-0.376$ (SE $0.136$) versus $-0.118$ in the core simulation (and $-0.630$ in Buyout FW).
  This excess divergence at long horizons aligns with a modest shared vintage component ($c_v \neq 0$) or heavier tail dispersion than in the lognormal core.
  \item \emph{Point-root IV is fragile in Buyout, while identification-robust AR confidence sets are wide and cover both $\beta=1$ and $U_{v,0}$.}
  At $H = 0$, the Buyout mean pricing moment admits two interior roots on $\Theta$ ($2.146$ and $2.577$ EW; $2.023$ and $2.659$ FW), and deleting a single vintage on the fixed full-sample domain causes \textbf{11 of 25} (EW) and \textbf{10 of 25} (FW) leave-one-out refits to lose every root entirely.
  Any point-estimate comparison or jackknife SE that silently substitutes a squared-moment minimizer for zero-root refits would be invalid.
  Instead, inverting the centered Bartlett fixed-$b$ moment test \eqref{eq:ar_set} yields valid identification-robust nominal 95\% confidence sets without root selection: $[0.949, 3.994]$ (BO EW), $[0.805, 3.994]$ (BO FW), $[-0.244, 3.994]$ (VC EW), and $[-0.549, 3.994]$ (VC FW).
  All four sets include $\beta = 1$ and $U_{v,0}$ and extend to the upper boundary $3.994$ because the mean pricing moment flattens asymptotically as discount rates rise.
  Consistent with our simulation calibration (Section~\ref{sec:simulation_study}), these sets confirm that the inception mean moment rules out very low market betas in Buyout ($<0.95$ EW, $<0.81$ FW) while providing almost no upper-tail identification---and under finite-$V$ persistence they remain nominal diagnostics rather than exact finite-sample guarantees.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Joint $(\alpha,\beta)$ estimation and the VC 1993--1997 cohort}
\label{sec:empirical_alpha_bovc}

Table~\ref{tab:u_l2_alpha_beta} reports joint $(\alpha,\beta_{\mathrm{MKT}})$ estimates for $U_{v,H}$ and L2 across horizons.
As established in Section~\ref{sec:simulation_study}, significance stars are intentionally omitted because nominal 5\% fixed-$b$ tests of $\alpha = 0$ over-reject in $12.1\%$ (Buyout) and $10.9\%$ (VC) of EW core draws at $H = 0$, FW joint calibration is not separately established, and 30-year inference suffers severe boundary distortion.

\input{../../r_project/results/figures_bovc_us_g1/main_u/table_E2_alpha_beta.tex}

In Buyout, adding an $\alpha$ intercept pulls the inception market beta sharply downward---from $1.147$ to $0.362$ (SE $0.207$) under EW and from $1.197$ to $0.439$ (SE $0.178$) under FW---while assigning positive monthly alpha of $0.605\%$ (SE $0.145\%$) EW and $0.568\%$ (SE $0.192\%$) FW.
At 30 years, $U_{v,30}$ deteriorates to the lower beta bound ($\hat\beta = -1.000^\dagger$ EW, $-0.873$ FW) with $\hat\alpha \approx 1.8\%$--$1.9\%$ per month.
Appendix Table~\ref{tab:alpha_beta_identification} and Figure~\ref{fig:alpha_beta_contours} diagnose the geometry behind this trade-off: although the cross-vintage NPV gradients with respect to $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are nearly orthogonal in Buyout at $(\beta=1,\alpha=0)$ (collinearity $-0.049$ EW, $-0.227$ FW), the normalized Hessian cross-term is large ($0.646$ EW, $0.609$ FW) and the Bartlett sampling correlation between $\hat\alpha$ and $\hat\beta$ reaches $-0.783$ (EW) and $-0.810$ (FW) at $H=0$ (and $-0.99$ at $H=15$).
Moreover, over our 37-year factor span (1985--2021, annualized market volatility $15.4\%$), the \citet{Merton80} lower bound on public equity drift uncertainty alone is $2.54\%$/year ($0.21\%$/month), illustrating why separating a small constant drift $\alpha$ from market risk premium $\beta\,\mathbb{E}[F_{\mathrm{MKT}}]$ is inherently fragile in a 25-vintage panel.

In Venture Capital, joint $(\alpha,\beta)$ estimation at $H = 0$ collapses onto the $+2\%$ monthly alpha ceiling under \emph{both} $U_{v,0}$ ($\hat\beta = -0.080^\dagger$ EW, $0.368^\dagger$ FW) and L2 ($\hat\beta = 0.674^\dagger$ EW, $1.169^\dagger$ FW).
As the horizon increases, $\hat\alpha$ flips sign sharply from $+2.00\%$ at $H = 0$ to $-0.47\%$ / $-0.56\%$ at 15 years and $-1.00\%$ / $-1.13\%$ at 30 years, while $\hat\beta$ climbs above $2.0$.
Appendix Table~\ref{tab:vc_without_1993_1997} traces this entire phenomenon to a single five-year cohort: the extraordinary dot-com vintages \textbf{1993--1997}.
Excluding 1993--1997 removes the $+2\%$ boundary solution completely and stabilizes VC estimates across every horizon: under $U_{v,H}$, $\hat\beta$ moves smoothly from $1.666$ ($H=0$) to $1.958$ ($H=30$) EW and from $1.607$ to $1.881$ FW, while $\hat\alpha$ is consistently negative ($-0.52\%$ to $-0.81\%$ EW; $-0.50\%$ to $-0.72\%$ FW).
Likewise, in the sample-robustness check (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:robustness_samples}, Panel~C), VC FW $U_{v,0}$ remains pinned on the $+2\%$ alpha bound across all three samples while its associated $\hat\beta$ shifts from $0.368^\dagger$ (complete histories) to $0.542^\dagger$ under the 20\%-NAV-threshold filter (and from $-0.080^\dagger$ to $0.150^\dagger$ under EW).
These diagnostics confirm that the full-sample VC horizon sign flip is an artifact of five extreme vintages interacting with the horizon scale factor $A_{v,H}(\theta)$ rather than a well-identified structural alpha.

Finally, Appendix Table~\ref{tab:published_estimates} places our headline $U_{v,0}$ and L2 estimates beside representative cash-flow and secondary-market studies \citep{DLP12,FNP12,HJK14,KN16,RS16,ACGP18,GN18,boyer2023discount,KN24}.
Our Buyout $U_{v,0}$ market betas ($1.15$ EW, $1.20$ FW) sit squarely inside the consensus range of cash-flow studies ($1.0$--$1.4$; below the $1.75$--$1.79$ secondary-market transaction betas of \citealp{boyer2023discount}), while our Venture Capital $U_{v,0}$ betas ($1.49$ EW, $1.74$ FW) lie well below \citet{DLP12}'s single-factor $2.73$---consistent with eliminating finite-pool squared-loss upward tilt---and closely align with \citet{ACGP18} ($1.6$--$1.8$) and \citet{KN16} ($1.0$--$2.0$).
Our finding that abnormal performance $\alpha$ is weakly identified in pooled cash flows without external public-SDF restrictions helps reconcile why prior studies report markedly divergent alpha conclusions depending on sample window, weighting, and benchmark structure.


\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper shows that finite-pool cross-sectional noise fundamentally alters the identification of semiparametric cash-flow SDF estimators.
Minimizing squared vintage-portfolio NPVs \citep{DLP12} embeds a parameter-dependent variance term that tilts the population target upward even at inception ($H = 0$).
Averaging pricing errors over post-inception compounding horizons multiplies every vintage's inception error by a parameter-dependent scale factor $A_{v,H}(\theta)$; differentiating $A_{v,H}(\theta)^2$ creates a downward Scale-Jacobian pull that displays finite-pool bias across horizons rather than curing it.

Dropping own-fund diagonal squares via the cross-fund pair-product criterion $U_{v,H}$ with equal vintage weights ($q_v = 1$) eliminates idiosyncratic within-vintage noise tilt at every horizon, restoring structural identification at inception ($H = 0$) under bounded fund counts per vintage ($n_v \ge 2$) when funds are conditionally independent without shared vintage shocks.
Paired with fixed-$b$ Bartlett SHAC inference ($S = \lceil 1.3\sqrt{V}\rceil$) and an identification-robust Anderson--Rubin inversion of the inception mean pricing moment, the framework separates what pooled private-equity cash flows can identify from what they cannot.

In our cleaned North American Preqin panel (383 Buyout and 432 Venture Capital funds, vintages 1982--2010), equal-vintage $U_{v,0}$ recovers economically coherent single-factor market betas of $1.15$ (EW) and $1.20$ (FW) for Buyout and $1.49$ (EW) and $1.74$ (FW) for Venture Capital, none distinguishable from unity.
By contrast, joint $(\alpha,\beta)$ and two-factor $q^5$ models are weakly identified: Buyout $\alpha$ and $\beta$ trade off tightly with over-rejecting nominal tests, Venture Capital inception alpha hits the $+2\%$ monthly ceiling solely due to the 1993--1997 cohort, and non-market $q^5$ loadings fluctuate or reverse sign across horizons.
Moreover, sample construction choices---constant-composition pooling, within-vintage median fund-size imputation, and vintage-cutoff seasoning---materially affect fund-size-weighted inference and should be documented transparently in empirical private-market asset pricing.
