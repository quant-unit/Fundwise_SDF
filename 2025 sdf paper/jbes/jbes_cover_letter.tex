We are pleased to submit our manuscript, \emph{``Cash-Flow Pricing of Non-Traded Assets: Finite-Pool Identification, Horizon Selection, and Inference,''} for consideration for publication in the \emph{Journal of Business \& Economic Statistics}. The manuscript is original, has not been published elsewhere, and is not under consideration by any other journal.

\textbf{Scope and Econometric Contributions.} Across the non-traded economy---from private companies (themselves pools of untraded corporate investment projects) and closed-end private capital funds (\$13+ trillion across buyout, venture capital, private real estate, private credit, infrastructure, natural resources, and impact) to illiquid structured-credit residuals (such as CLO equity tranches), specialized claim pools, and cohort-grouped direct real assets, whole loans, or human-capital contracts---periodic market prices are unobserved or noisy appraisals, and irregular cash flows are the primary record. Whether units are natively pooled or grouped into origination cohorts to dampen noise, estimating nonlinear discounting parameters directly from cash flows creates a finite-pool M-estimation problem fundamentally distinct from linear traded-return factor regressions. Our paper makes four methodological contributions:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em, itemsep=2pt, topsep=3pt]
  \item \emph{Finite-pool identification failure (Theorem~1, T1--T6):} We prove that minimizing squared pooled net present values (including the inception criterion of Driessen, Lin, and Phalippou 2012) fails consistency at finite cohort sizes whenever parameter-dependent payoff variance shifts the population target, and establish an exact factorization showing how post-inception horizon compounding (Tausch and Pietz 2024, eq.~4) generates an opposing Scale-Jacobian pull.
  \item \emph{Cross-unit pair-product criterion $U_{v,H}$ (Theorem~1, T7--T8):} Connecting to leave-one-out quadratic-form debiasing, U-statistic M-estimation, and conditional-moment methods (Angrist, Imbens, and Krueger 1999; Dom\'{i}nguez and Lobato 2004; Honor\'{e} and Powell 1994; Kline, Saggio, and S{\o}lvsten 2020; Mikusheva and Sun 2022; Muandet, Jitkrittum, and K\"{u}bler 2020), we introduce $U_{v,H}$ ($q_v = 1$), a tuning-free $O(n_v)$ upgrade eliminating the idiosyncratic diagonal contribution at $H=0$ with as few as two units per cohort when shared cohort shocks are absent and conditional means are sign-aligned.
  \item \emph{Cross-cohort rank-1 impossibility and duration-matched horizon selection (Proposition~2):} Under shared vintage or calendar shocks (which persist even as $n_v \to \infty$), we prove that uninstrumented leave-band-out cross-cohort pair products collapse asymptotically to rank one and are finite-$V$ indefinite, and show how forward-horizon compounding becomes a constructive lever: matching compounding sensitivity to payoff duration yields a duration-matching score root $H_0^\ast$ that cancels first-order shared-shock bias and restores coercivity, with global bounds, marginal-law AR(1) invariance, calendar-overlap conditions, and finite-$V$ MSE expansions.
  \item \emph{Primitive 1D increasing-domain and identification-robust inference (Proposition~3, Corollary~1, and Monte Carlo study):} Framing vintage overlap directly on 1D calendar time $y_v \in \mathbb{Z}$, we verify mixing and moment inheritance from primitive bounded-lifetime DGP conditions (Assumptions~A1--A5, Lemma~D.1), establish valid small-sample (fixed-$b$) Bartlett and identification-robust Anderson--Rubin asymptotics, validate finite-sample behavior across 72 calibrated designs plus a $V = 25\text{--}1{,}600$ ablation study resolving compounding-kurtosis studentization dynamics, and illustrate the methodology on North American Buyout and Venture Capital cash flows.
\end{enumerate}

Given the paper's focus on nonlinear financial econometrics, asset-pricing identification, horizon selection, and identification-robust inference, we respectfully believe it aligns particularly well with the financial econometrics editorial track (e.g., Professors Dacheng Xiu or Andrew Patton), while leaving editorial assignment entirely to your discretion.
