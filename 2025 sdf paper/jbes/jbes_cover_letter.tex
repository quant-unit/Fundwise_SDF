We are pleased to submit our manuscript, \emph{``Cash-Flow Pricing of Non-Traded Assets: Finite-Pool Identification, Horizon Selection, and Inference,''} for consideration for publication in the \emph{Journal of Business \& Economic Statistics}. The manuscript is original, has not been published elsewhere, and is not under consideration by any other journal.

\textbf{Scope and Econometric Contributions.} For private capital funds (private equity including buyout and venture capital, real estate, infrastructure, and natural resources) and project finance, secondary trades are sparse, reported values are appraisals or book values, and the reliable record consists of irregular, pooled cash-flow streams grouped into finite vintage cohorts whose active lives overlap in calendar time. Estimating nonlinear discounting parameters directly from such pooled cash flows creates a finite-pool M-estimation problem fundamentally distinct from linear traded-return factor regressions. Our paper makes four methodological contributions:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em, itemsep=2pt, topsep=3pt]
  \item \emph{Finite-pool identification failure and exact horizon rescaling (Theorem~1, T1--T6):} We prove that minimizing squared pooled net present values (including the inception criterion of Driessen, Lin, and Phalippou 2012) fails consistency at finite pool sizes $n_v < \infty$ because parameter-dependent payoff variance shifts the population target, and prove an exact rescaling identity showing that post-inception horizon averaging (as in Tausch and Pietz 2024, eq.~4) adds no new payoff realizations while introducing a Scale-Jacobian pull.
  \item \emph{Cross-fund pair-product criterion $U_{v,H}$ (Theorem~1, T7--T8):} Connecting to leave-one-out quadratic-form debiasing, U-statistic M-estimation, and conditional-moment methods (Angrist, Imbens, and Krueger 1999; Dom\'{i}nguez and Lobato 2004; Honor\'{e} and Powell 1994; Kline, Saggio, and S{\o}lvsten 2020; Mikusheva and Sun 2022; Muandet, Jitkrittum, and K\"{u}bler 2020), we introduce $U_{v,H}$ ($q_v = 1$), eliminating idiosyncratic variance tilt at $H=0$ under bounded $n_v \ge 2$ when shared vintage shocks are absent and conditional means are sign-aligned.
  \item \emph{Multi-deal duration matching and rank-1 collapse of uninstrumented cross-vintage pairs (Proposition~2):} Under shared and nearby cross-vintage shocks, we derive the exact multi-deal duration-matching score and its constant-return score-root corollary $H_0^\ast$, establish global bounds, marginal-law AR(1) invariance, calendar-overlap conditions, and finite-$V$ MSE expansions, and prove that uninstrumented leave-band-out cross-vintage pair products collapse asymptotically to rank one and are finite-$V$ indefinite.
  \item \emph{Primitive 1D increasing-domain fixed-$b$ and AR inference (Proposition~3, Corollary~1, and Monte Carlo study):} Framing vintage overlap directly on 1D calendar time $y_v \in \mathbb{Z}$, we verify mixing and moment inheritance from primitive bounded-lifetime DGP conditions (Assumptions~A1--A5, Lemma~D.1), prove fixed-$b$ Bartlett and identification-robust Anderson--Rubin asymptotics, validate finite-sample behavior across 72 calibrated designs plus a $V = 25\text{--}1{,}600$ ablation study resolving compounding-kurtosis studentization dynamics, and illustrate the methodology on North American Buyout and Venture Capital cash flows.
\end{enumerate}

Given the paper's focus on nonlinear financial econometrics, asset-pricing identification, and fixed-$b$ / weak-identification inference, we respectfully believe it aligns particularly well with the financial econometrics editorial track (e.g., Professors Dacheng Xiu or Andrew Patton), while leaving editorial assignment entirely to your discretion.
